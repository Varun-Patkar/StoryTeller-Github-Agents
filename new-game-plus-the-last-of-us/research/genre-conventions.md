# Genre Conventions

## Gamer System / LitRPG Fanfiction
- **Core appeal**: Measurable progression; the satisfaction of watching numbers go up
- **System introductions**: Usually happen in Chapter 1; keep the tutorial brief and let readers discover mechanics through use
- **Point farming montages**: Acceptable but should include character moments and worldbuilding, not just "killed 50 runners, got 500 points"
- **Shop scenes**: Fun to read when the MC faces genuine dilemmas about what to buy
- **Stat blocks**: Use sparingly; readers skim long stat dumps. Show stats through action instead
- **Common pitfall**: MC becomes so OP that all tension disappears. Counter this with social/political challenges and emotional stakes that stats can't solve

## Self-Insert / Transmigration
- **Meta-knowledge**: MC knows the plot of TLOU — this is the core advantage and the core tension
- **Butterfly effect**: Small changes cascade; the further from canon, the less meta-knowledge helps
- **Emotional authenticity**: MC should genuinely struggle with being in a death world, not treat it as a game (despite having a gamer system)
- **Common pitfall**: MC is too perfect, too calm, too genre-savvy. Give them real flaws, real fears, real mistakes
- **Reader connection**: MC's reactions to canon events (especially emotional ones like Sarah's near-death) should feel earned

## Fix-It Fic
- **Core promise**: Canon's tragedies are averted or mitigated
- **Satisfaction loop**: Reader knows what SHOULD have happened badly; MC prevents it
- **Important**: Not everything can be fixed. Some losses should still happen to maintain stakes
- **Canon characters must still feel like themselves**: Joel is still gruff and protective; Ellie is still sarcastic and brave; Tommy is still warm but practical

## Kingdom Building / Community Building
- **Progression structure**: Individual survival → small group → settlement → town → regional power
- **Challenges**: Food, water, shelter, defense, governance, morale, internal politics, external threats
- **Satisfying moments**: First generator powering up, first harvest, first successful defense against a horde
- **Common pitfall**: Skipping the hard parts. Show the MC dealing with sewage, disputes, lazy freeloaders — not just heroic battles
- **Democratic vs. autocratic**: MC should default to democracy but the apocalypse makes authoritarianism tempting. This creates interesting tension

## Post-Apocalyptic Genre
- **Environmental storytelling**: Describe the beauty in ruins; nature reclaiming cities; silence where there used to be noise
- **Scarcity drives plot**: Every resource matters; every bullet counts (early game)
- **Human villains are scarier than infected**: Bandits, slavers, cannibals — humanity's dark side
- **Hope is the most powerful element**: Small moments of normalcy (a meal together, a joke, music) hit harder than action scenes
- **Children in danger**: Universally effective tension; protect the kids

## Tone Balance (Per User Preference)
- **Lighthearted default**: MC brings humor, optimism, and modern sensibilities to a grim world
- **Dark moments earned**: When bad things happen, they hit harder because the baseline is warm
- **MC's moral code**: No killing innocents. Bad people are fair game. This is a clear, consistent line
- **Avoid edgelord grimdark**: Even when showing humanity's worst side, frame it through the MC's refusal to become part of it
- **Humor sources**: MC's modern references (that no one gets), system notifications at awkward times, Sarah's reactions to MC's weirdness
