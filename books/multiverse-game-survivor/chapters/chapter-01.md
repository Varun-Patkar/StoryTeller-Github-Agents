# One More Life

## Outline

1. Elena defies their parents to visit Alaric in his private hospital room.
2. Alaric comforts her, gives her one of his games, and asks her to choose her own life.
3. Alaric dies wishing for another chance to act instead of merely watching.
4. The Second Chance System answers him and offers a life spent changing doomed worlds.
5. Alaric wakes in a healthy fifteen-year-old body outside ruined Boston.
6. He learns his local identity, examines the system, and receives his first optional quest.

**Arc beats advanced:** Alaric dies and awakens in a post-outbreak world.
**Foreshadowing planted:** Alaric's need to be useful; the system's unknown origin; the limits of treating worlds like games.
**Foreshadowing paid off:** None.

## Chapter Text

Elena had stolen the visitor's chair.

That was the first thing Alaric noticed when the door eased shut behind her. Not that his thirteen-year-old sister had slipped past hospital security, crossed half the city alone, and arrived after midnight. Not the green coat swallowing her narrow shoulders or the backpack hanging from one hand.

The chair mattered because it had spent the last six months empty.

She dragged it across the floor with both hands. Its wooden legs made a soft, expensive scrape against the soft, expensive floor.

"Careful," Alaric whispered. "That probably costs more than a small car."

Elena stopped. Her gray eyes flicked from the chair to the machines around his bed. "It's ugly."

"A large, ugly car, then."

Her mouth twitched. The victory cost him a breath, but it was worth paying for.

She finished positioning the chair and sat close enough that her knees touched the bed rail. Only then did she look at him properly.

Alaric knew what she saw. Skin bleached by years indoors. Arms too thin to fill the sleeves of his sleep shirt. Dark hair plastered to his forehead except for the white streak that had appeared after his last round of treatment. Clear tubes ran beneath his nose and into the back of his hand. Other lines disappeared under the blanket.

The room tried hard to make all of it tasteful. Pale wood hid the oxygen connections. The monitor glowed at its dimmest setting. Beyond the broad window, the city shone in clean grids of white and gold.

Money had bought him a beautiful place to die.

"How did you get here?" he asked.

"Train. Then I walked."

"Alone?"

"I'm not six."

"No, at six you had better operational security. You used to bring snacks."

Elena set her backpack between her shoes. "I brought your charger."

"My mistake. An elite rescue mission."

She reached inside and placed a charging cable on the bedside table. It was the wrong cable. Alaric did not tell her.

For a while, neither of them spoke. Air sighed through the vent. The monitor counted his heartbeats with the patience of something that knew it would win.

Elena stared at the shelf opposite the bed. Their parents had sent a new game whenever a doctor ran out of encouraging language. Most were collector's editions. Steel cases, art books, statues, maps. An entire row remained sealed because his hands had started shaking too badly to use a controller.

"Mum thinks I'm asleep at Sera's," she said.

"And Sera?"

"Thinks I'm asleep at home."

"You've built your alibi on two people never speaking to each other."

"They hate each other."

"Then it's practically mathematics."

This time she did not smile.

"They said I shouldn't come."

Alaric turned his head toward the window. A red light moved between the buildings below, silent at this height.

"It's late," he said. "They worry."

"No. They said seeing you would make it harder." Her fingers tightened around the straps of her backpack. "They never asked which part was supposed to be easy."

His first instinct was to explain them. Their father handled fear by converting it into invoices. Their mother dealt with pain by arranging it out of sight. Neither had missed a payment, an appointment, or an opportunity to send something costly in their place.

Explanations were one of Alaric's better tricks. If he could make a hurt logical, he could make it smaller.

Tonight, the trick felt cheap.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Elena scrubbed at one eye with the heel of her palm. "You didn't do it."

"I know. Still sorry."

"They love you."

The monitor gave three measured beeps while Alaric considered the safest answer.

"Probably," he said. "They're just bad at the practical portion of the exam."

"You always do that."

"Do what?"

"Make it easier for everyone else."

He attempted a shrug. The blanket barely moved. "Limited market for making terminal illness more difficult."

"You don't have to make me feel better." Her voice cracked, and anger rushed in to cover it. "You're allowed to be scared too."

He looked at his little sister and saw that she had come for honesty, not comfort. She had lied to two households, navigated the city after dark, and committed grand theft furniture for it.

So Alaric gave her what he could.

"I'm terrified."

Elena inhaled sharply.

"Not of hurting," he continued. "They've handled that part. I keep thinking there should have been more time after all the waiting. Time when I was better. Time when I could go somewhere because I wanted to, not because a specialist had an opening."

"It's not fair."

"No."

"I hate it."

"So do I."

Her hand found his above the blanket. She was careful at first, as if he might break under her fingers. Alaric turned his palm and held on as firmly as he could.

They stayed that way until his eyes began to close without permission.

"Pick one," he said.

"What?"

He nodded toward the shelf.

Elena frowned. "I don't want one."

"That's devastating. I've invested heavily in becoming the favorite dead brother."

"Don't say that."

"Elena."

She stared down at their joined hands.

"Pick a game," Alaric said. "Play it badly. Ignore every helpful tutorial. Make choices I would hate."

"Why?"

"Because they're yours."

Her grip tightened.

Alaric waited until she looked at him. "Don't spend your life waiting for permission. Not from them. Not from me. Promise?"

"That's a horrible last request."

"I had a more dramatic one, but it required a volcano."

A wet laugh escaped her. "Promise."

Elena chose an old case from the bottom shelf, one with worn corners instead of shrink-wrap. She tucked it into her backpack, then leaned over the rail and pressed her forehead against his.

"I love you," she whispered.

For one ugly moment, Alaric could not speak. His lungs tightened. The monitor changed rhythm.

He forced the words through what little air remained.

"Love you too, menace."

The night nurse found Elena ten minutes later. She scolded her gently, called someone to take her home, and pretended not to see that Alaric had kept hold of his sister's hand until the last possible second.

Then the door closed.

The chair remained beside the bed.

Alaric watched the city until its lights blurred. He imagined Elena on the train with the game case in her bag. He imagined his parents waking to a call they had expected for years. He tried not to imagine nothing.

The machines noticed the change before he did.

An alarm began as a polite chime. His chest pulled tight. Air entered but brought no relief, as though his lungs had forgotten what it was for. A nurse hurried through the door, followed by another. Hands adjusted lines. Someone spoke his name.

Alaric could not answer.

The shelf of games stood beyond their shoulders. Worlds he had crossed from a bed. People he had saved because the story permitted it, lost because the story demanded it, and guided to endings that never changed no matter how many times he returned.

He had always known they were fictional.

That had never stopped him from caring.

His heart stumbled. The city vanished behind a white haze.

One more time, Alaric thought. Let me be better. Let me do something. Let me play one more time.

The monitor became a single, uninterrupted note.

Then even that was gone.

---

There was no light, no body, and no pain.

Alaric waited for the lack of all three to frighten him. Instead, relief spread through what remained of his awareness. No burning veins. No weight on his chest. No careful inventory of which part of him had worsened while he slept.

Words appeared in the dark.

**FINAL WISH ACKNOWLEDGED.**

He read them twice.

"This is either death or a truly aggressive licensing agreement."

More text formed beneath the first line, pale and steady.

**Hello, Alaric.**

The words carried no sound, yet warmth lived inside them. Not human warmth. Something cleaner and more deliberate, like a voice designed to reach him without startling him.

**Your life ended before you were given the chance to live it. Your final wish asked for another opportunity: to grow, to act, and to return to the worlds that mattered to you.**

Alaric tried to move and discovered that trying was enough. A shape gathered around him, an outline suggested by his own memory. Hands. Feet. Breath, though he did not need it.

"Who are you?"

**I am the Second Chance System.**

The title settled into place above a simple pane of light.

**I can give you another life. More than one, if you continue to choose them. You will enter damaged worlds and receive opportunities to alter preventable tragedies. You may gain skills, attributes, traits, and resources. What you earn can travel with you.**

Images moved at the edge of the void. A ruined city beneath summer green. A tunnel lit by weak electric lamps. A rider crossing red earth. A mansion window flashing with distant lightning.

They vanished before he could focus on any one of them.

"Why me?"

**Because you asked for a second chance, and because you wished to become capable of giving one.**

It was too neat. Alaric distrusted neat answers on principle.

"That's the purpose. It isn't an origin."

The warmth did not change.

**Correct.**

"Will you tell me your origin?"

**Not yet.**

Honesty, at least. Or a sufficiently good imitation.

A new pane opened.

**Support does not mean safety. The people you meet will possess free will. Information drawn from a world's known history will become less reliable as you change events. Injury and death remain possible. Failed opportunities will not reset.**

Alaric thought of Elena. He hoped she had made it home before the call. He hoped someone had remembered that she was thirteen and grieving, rather than inconvenient and awake.

"Can I go back?"

The answer came without delay.

**Your original life has ended. I cannot return you to it.**

The truth hurt. That surprised him. He had spent years preparing to leave everything and had mistaken preparation for readiness.

"Will I ever know what happened to her?"

**Not through any function currently available to you.**

Currently. A careful word, placed where he would notice it.

Alaric studied the darkness as though it might reveal the machinery behind the voice. "What do you get?"

**The fulfillment of my function.**

"Which you define."

**Which you may judge through my actions.**

Not trust, then. Not yet. But an offer did not have to be pure to be valuable.

On one side waited whatever came after refusing. On the other waited pain, danger, mysteries, and the impossible chance to matter inside worlds where he had once been only an observer.

Alaric thought of the empty chair. He thought of Elena filling it because she had decided that love mattered more than permission.

"What do I have to say?"

The light brightened.

**Choose.**

He had asked Elena to do the same.

"I accept."

The void broke open.

---

Alaric hit wet concrete shoulder-first and rolled into a patch of weeds.

Pain flashed down his arm.

He lay still, stunned by it.

Not because it hurt. Because it stopped.

His shoulder throbbed once, twice, then settled into the ordinary complaint of a bruise. Air rushed into his lungs. Cool, damp, foul air smelling of mud, old fuel, and something sweetly rotten.

Alaric dragged in another breath. Then another.

He laughed.

It came out too loud and too young. Birds exploded from the skeleton of an overpass above him. Somewhere beyond the trees, metal struck metal.

Alaric clapped a hand over his mouth.

The hand was wrong. Broader than his had been, the knuckles scraped, the nails dirty. He pushed himself upright and found a body lean with hunger but solid beneath it. No lines. No tremor. No ache sunk into the marrow. He pressed two fingers to his wrist and felt a strong, quick pulse.

For several seconds, the ruined world ceased to matter.

He stood.

His knees held.

He took three steps, then five. He crouched and rose without assistance. The movement was so easy that grief struck him harder than the concrete had. This was what everyone else had possessed without noticing: a body that obeyed.

"Thank you," he said quietly.

**You are welcome, Alaric.**

The reply appeared at the edge of his vision without blocking it.

He looked down at himself. Faded cargo trousers. Boots a size too large, tightened with extra socks. A gray jacket bearing the cracked insignia of a FEDRA preparatory school. A battered backpack lay where he had landed.

His reflection waited in a rain puddle.

The face was younger than the one that had died. Fifteen, perhaps. Healthy beneath dirt and a fading bruise. Dark hair hung over watchful gray eyes. A white forelock divided it near the temple.

Still him.

Alaric touched the white strands. "Subtle."

**Identity continuity improves psychological stability.**

"And here I thought it was branding."

A translucent panel appeared when he focused on the thought of himself.

**ALARIC STARK**  
**World Age:** 15  
**Identity:** Displaced student, Boston FEDRA Preparatory Annex  
**Condition:** Healthy; mild dehydration; caloric deficit

**ATTRIBUTES**  
Physique — Developing  
Agility — Developing  
Endurance — Developing  
Perception — Competent  
Intellect — Exceptional  
Will — Competent

**SKILLS**  
Academic Knowledge — Expert  
Strategic Analysis — Competent  
Firearms — Novice  
Fieldcraft — Novice  
Close Combat — Untrained

**TRAITS**  
Second Life  
Adaptive Regeneration — Dormant/Developing  
System Inventory

Qualitative ranks. No convenient number telling him that ten push-ups would increase his strength. No health bar promising exactly how much blood he could lose.

Good. Numbers would have tempted him to mistake certainty for safety.

The backpack held two sealed water bottles, three ration bars, a folding knife, a spare shirt, a basic first-aid kit, and identity papers softened by damp. The dates made his throat tighten.

Twenty years after the outbreak.

Boston.

Summer.

He knew this world.

Not as a map. Games compressed distance, skipped uneventful days, and placed supplies where desperate people would have taken them years ago. But he knew what grew inside the infected. He knew what waited in dark buildings. He knew the quarantine zone, the Fireflies, and a hardened smuggler whose life had broken on the first night of the end.

Alaric closed the status panel and listened.

Wind moved through leaves. Water dripped beneath the overpass. Far away, a bird called twice.

Then came a human voice.

"—check the road again. Kid couldn't have gone far."

Another voice answered with a laugh.

Alaric lowered himself behind the rusted shell of a car. The joy of his restored body cooled into focus. He checked the folding knife, judged the distance to the tree line, and searched for anything that might serve as a weapon.

A notification opened.

**OPTIONAL QUEST: CLEAR THE ROAD**

**A bandit camp is controlling the eastern service route and hunting vulnerable travelers. Break the camp's control of the route. How you accomplish this is your choice.**

**Deadline:** 47:59:59  
**Reward:** Firearms Aptitude; Endurance Progress; item cache  
**Failure Penalty:** None

The timer lost a second.

Alaric looked toward the voices moving through the trees.

For the first time in his life, he had power over what happened next.

For the first time in his life, choosing wrong could kill someone other than him.
