# One More Life

## Outline

1. Elena visits Alaric in his private hospital room shortly before his death.
2. The siblings say goodbye, and Alaric gives Elena one of his games.
3. Alaric dies wishing he could live again and do more with his life.
4. The Second Chance System offers to send him into game worlds where he can save people and grow stronger.
5. Alaric accepts and wakes in a healthy fifteen-year-old body outside Boston.
6. He checks his status, learns his local identity, and receives his first optional quest with exact rewards.

**Arc beats advanced:** Alaric dies and awakens in a post-outbreak world.
**Foreshadowing planted:** Alaric wants to be useful to the people he cares about; changing known stories will make his game knowledge less reliable.
**Foreshadowing paid off:** None.

## Chapter Text

Alaric was halfway through a video when his little sister walked into his hospital room.

He paused it and stared at her. "Elena?"

She shut the door behind her and looked down the hallway through the small window. Then she hurried to his bed.

"What are you doing here?" he asked. "It's after midnight."

"I wanted to see you."

"Did Mum bring you?"

Elena shook her head.

Alaric looked at the backpack on her shoulders and understood. "You came alone?"

"I took the train."

"You're thirteen."

"I know how old I am."

Elena pouted and pulled both backpack straps tight against her shoulders. It was the same face she had used as a little girl whenever he caught her stealing snacks from his room.

He wanted to tell her how dangerous that was. He also knew she might leave if he sounded angry, and he did not want that.

"Come sit down," he said.

Elena pulled the visitor's chair closer and sat beside him. She kept looking at the tubes connected to his arm and the monitor beside the bed.

His parents paid for the best room in the hospital. It had a large television, a private bathroom, a good view of the city, and enough space for a family to spend the night.

No one ever did.

His mother visited once a week. His father came when the doctors needed a signature. Both of them always asked if he needed anything. They bought whatever he requested, but they never stayed long.

Elena was the first person who had come just because she wanted to see him.

"How did you get past the nurses?" he asked.

"I told them Dad was parking the car."

"And they believed you?"

"I started crying."

She looked a little proud of herself.

Alaric smiled. "That was smart."

Her proud look faded. "I wasn't pretending the whole time."

His smile disappeared. "Oh."

Elena rubbed her eyes with her sleeve.

"Hey," he said. "I'm okay."

"No, you're not."

He could not argue with that. The doctors had stopped talking about new treatments two days ago. Now they only talked about keeping him comfortable.

"No," he admitted. "I'm not."

She reached through the bed rail and held his hand. Her fingers were cold.

"Mum said I shouldn't come," she said. "She said I should remember you from before."

Alaric almost laughed. Before his illness became serious, Elena had been seven. Most of her memories of him came from hospital visits and video calls.

"I'm glad you came," he said.

"Are you scared?"

He looked at the dark television screen. He had spent years telling his family that he was fine. It made visits easier. They did not have to think about the fact that he was dying, and he did not have to watch them search for something useful to say.

Elena deserved a real answer.

"Yeah," he said. "I'm scared."

"Does it hurt?"

"Not much right now. The medicine helps."

"I hate this."

"Me too."

She lowered her head and started crying.

Alaric squeezed her hand as hard as he could. It was not very hard anymore.

"I'm sorry I didn't visit more," she said.

"That wasn't your fault."

"I could've asked."

"You were a kid."

"I'm still a kid."

"Exactly. You're proving my point."

Elena gave him a small, unhappy smile.

They sat quietly for a while. Alaric liked it. He did not need her to say anything special. Having her there was enough.

A shelf stood near the television. It was filled with games his parents had bought him over the years. Some had been his idea. Others had arrived whenever his mother felt guilty.

"Do you want one of my games?" he asked.

Elena looked at the shelf. "I don't play as much as you."

"You might later. Pick one."

"Why?"

"Because I want you to have it."

She hesitated, then chose an old case from the bottom shelf. It was one they had played together years ago.

"That one?" he asked.

Elena hugged it to her chest. "You said any one. No taking it back."

"It's a good choice."

"I know." She gave him a quick grin and put it in her backpack before he could change his mind.

"Elena?"

"Yeah?"

"Don't let Mum and Dad decide everything for you. Listen to them, but make your own choices too."

"Like coming here?"

"Maybe don't take the train across the city alone at midnight again."

"So only make choices you like?"

"I'm your brother. I'm allowed to be a hypocrite."

She stuck her tongue out at him. It was childish, and completely normal. Alaric wished he could stop time and stay there.

A nurse entered a few minutes later. She looked shocked when she saw Elena, but she did not call security. Instead, she phoned Elena's friend and arranged for the girl's mother to drive her home.

Elena tried her most innocent smile on the nurse. It did not save her from a long lecture, but it did make Alaric laugh.

Elena leaned over the bed before she left.

"I love you," she whispered.

Alaric had known she loved him. Hearing it still hurt in a way the medicine could not stop.

"I love you too," he said. "Go home safe."

She hugged him carefully and left with the nurse.

The room felt much larger after she was gone.

Alaric turned his video back on, but he could not focus. His eyes kept moving to the empty chair and the gap on the game shelf.

He was sixteen years old. He had spent most of his life studying, reading, and playing games because those were the things his body allowed him to do. He had learned quickly and remembered almost everything. Doctors called him gifted. Teachers called him brilliant.

None of it had saved him.

His breathing became harder near dawn.

The monitor started beeping. A nurse rushed in and pressed a button on the wall. More people followed her.

Alaric heard them talking, but their voices seemed far away. His chest hurt. He tried to breathe and could not get enough air.

He thought about Elena on her way home. He hoped she would keep the game. He hoped she would remember the good days and not only this room.

He also thought about all the games he would never play again.

He wanted more time. He wanted a body that worked. He wanted to see places beyond hospitals and hotels near hospitals. Most of all, he wanted the chance to do something real with everything he had learned.

Please, he thought. Just one more chance.

His heart stopped.

---

Alaric opened his eyes and saw nothing.

He could not feel the bed or the tubes. He could not feel any pain either.

A blue screen appeared in front of him.

**FINAL WISH RECEIVED.**

**SECOND CHANCE SYSTEM ACTIVATED.**

Alaric read the words again. Then a new message appeared.

**Hello, Alaric Stark. Your original body has died. Would you like a second chance at life?**

There was only one possible answer.

"Yes."

The screen changed at once.

**You will travel to worlds you know as games. Each world contains people whose lives ended in tragedy. Complete the main mission to give them a second chance. Optional quests will provide additional rewards.**

**Skills, attributes, traits, items, and inventory contents will remain with you when you travel to a new world.**

Alaric felt excitement replace the fear from his final moments.

Game worlds were real. He could enter them. He could meet the people he had watched for hundreds of hours and change what happened to them.

It sounded impossible, but he was already dead and talking to a floating screen. Impossible had stopped being useful as an argument.

"Can I get stronger?"

**Yes. You will receive rewards for completed quests. Skills provide knowledge and talent, but you must practice to master them. Attribute rewards provide exact points. Ten points raise an attribute by one rank.**

That was fair. Alaric did not want to spend another life trapped in a weak body, but instant strength would make every world boring. Earning it sounded much better.

"What happens if I fail an optional quest?"

**You receive no reward. There is no additional penalty.**

"And the main mission?"

**You may leave a world after completing its main mission. You may also remain there for as long as you wish.**

Alaric liked that answer. He did not want to save someone and then vanish before he could enjoy the result.

"Will you help me?"

**I will answer questions about your abilities, track your status, provide quests, manage your inventory, and assist with travel between worlds. I will not control other people or complete quests for you.**

"Good enough for me."

A final message appeared.

**Your first world is ready.**

**Do you accept?**

Alaric did not waste time thinking about it.

"I accept."

The darkness vanished.

---

He landed on wet concrete.

The fall hurt, but the pain faded after a few seconds. Alaric pushed himself up and took a deep breath.

Then he took another.

His lungs filled without pain.

He stood so quickly that he almost slipped. His legs held him up. They were thin, but they were strong enough to carry his weight without shaking.

Alaric laughed.

He ran several steps, stopped, and ran back. Then he jumped.

It was not a high jump. It did not matter. He had not been able to jump in years.

"This is amazing."

**Your new body is healthy,** the system replied.

"Thank you. Seriously."

**You are welcome.**

Alaric looked at his hands. They were dirty and covered in small scratches, but they looked like his hands from before the disease became severe. He found a puddle beside the road and checked his reflection.

He looked about fifteen. His face was healthy, his gray eyes were clear, and the white patch in his dark hair was still there.

The system had kept him looking like himself.

He touched the white hair and smiled. "I can live with that."

Only then did he pay attention to the area around him.

He stood beneath a broken highway overpass. Plants grew through cracks in the road. Rusted cars had been pushed against the sides, and several buildings in the distance had collapsed. The air smelled damp and rotten.

A gray jacket lay beside him with a worn FEDRA symbol on the shoulder. There was also a backpack.

Alaric froze when he saw the symbol.

"FEDRA," he said.

A status screen opened.

**ALARIC STARK**

**World:** The Last of Us  
**World Age:** 15  
**Identity:** Former student, Boston FEDRA Preparatory Annex  
**Condition:** Healthy, mildly dehydrated, hungry

**ATTRIBUTES**

Physique: Developing, 0/10  
Agility: Developing, 0/10  
Endurance: Developing, 0/10  
Perception: Competent, 0/10  
Intellect: Exceptional, 0/10  
Will: Competent, 0/10

**SKILLS**

Academic Knowledge: Expert  
Strategic Analysis: Competent  
Firearms: Novice  
Fieldcraft: Novice  
Close Combat: Untrained

**TRAITS**

Second Life  
Adaptive Regeneration: Developing  
System Inventory

Alaric stared at the world name.

The Last of Us.

He knew exactly how dangerous this world was. The infected were only part of the problem. Bandits, soldiers, hunters, and desperate survivors could be worse.

He also knew when he had arrived. The identity papers in the backpack gave him the date. It was about a year before Joel and Ellie would leave Boston together.

That gave him time.

Tess could be saved. Sam and Henry could be saved. Joel would never have to lie to Ellie about the hospital because Alaric could find another way to study her immunity.

He could change all of it.

First, he had to survive.

The backpack contained two bottles of water, three ration bars, a folding knife, a spare shirt, a basic first-aid kit, and his identity papers. Alaric drank half a bottle and ate one ration bar while he studied the road.

A man's voice came from the trees ahead.

"Check near the cars. He can't be far."

Another man answered, "If the kid ran, let him run. We got his bag."

"Boss said no witnesses."

Alaric lowered the ration bar and moved behind a rusted car.

They were hunting a child.

A notification appeared.

**OPTIONAL QUEST: CLEAR THE ROAD**

**Objective:** Break the bandit camp's control of the eastern service road.  
**Deadline:** 48 hours  
**Failure Penalty:** None

**Rewards:**

- Firearms Aptitude
- 1 Endurance Point
- Basic Firearm Cache: one 9mm pistol, three 15-round magazines, 60 rounds of 9mm ammunition, one belt holster, and one pistol-cleaning kit

Those were real rewards. More importantly, there was a child nearby who needed help.

Alaric stored one water bottle and two ration bars in his inventory. They disappeared from his hands and appeared in a simple item list.

He kept the folding knife.

"System, can you tell me how many bandits are in the camp?"

**No. You must gather that information yourself.**

"Worth asking."

Alaric crouched beside the car and listened to the men moving closer.

He had a healthy body, a useful system, and forty-eight hours to complete his first quest.

For the first time in his life, he was looking forward to what came next.
