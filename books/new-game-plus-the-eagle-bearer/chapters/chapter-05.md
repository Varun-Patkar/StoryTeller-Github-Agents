# Chapter 5: A Ship Out

The rider carried a contract worth enough money to make Markos honest for almost a full minute.

He dismounted beside the vineyard press and handed Kassandra a sealed strip of parchment. The seal showed a merchant ship under full sail. His cloak was too clean for the road, his horse was rented, and he kept looking at the locked chest beneath Theron's workbench.

News of the eye had traveled quickly.

"My employer requests a private meeting," the rider said. "Both misthioi. This afternoon, at the guest house above Ktimene Beach. He will discuss payment in person."

"Name?" Kassandra asked.

"Elpenor of Kirrha."

Theron knew the name, the face, the mask Elpenor kept elsewhere, and the cave in Phokis where the man would eventually try to kill them. Perfect memory made surprise difficult. It did nothing to make the company pleasant.

Kassandra broke the seal and read the two lines inside. "He came from Phokis for us?"

"He came for the Cyclops," Theron said. "We became more interesting yesterday."

The rider gave him a careful look. "My employer heard you were capable before yesterday."

That was probably true. The Cult of Kosmos had watched Kassandra's family for years. Theron's place beside her would have earned a line in somebody's records even if Elpenor had never heard his name spoken aloud.

Theron smiled. "Then he can afford us."

The rider left without asking about the chest again.

Markos waited until the horse disappeared over the ridge. "Elpenor is a respected merchant. Ships, dyes, weapons. He has interests in every useful port between here and Athens."

"How much do you owe him?" Kassandra asked.

"Nothing."

They both stared at him.

Markos spread his hands. "A small amount, perhaps. It is unrelated. Entirely unrelated."

Phoibe sat on the wall with the badly painted dolphin cup in her hands. "Should I follow the rider?"

"No," Theron and Kassandra said together.

Phoibe drank her water. "You do that too often."

Elpenor had rented the best room in the guest house and improved it by removing most of the furniture. A table stood near the open shutters. Two guards waited outside, close enough to enter and far enough to let their employer deny needing them.

The merchant wore a dark red himation over a white chiton. He looked ordinary in the expensive, deliberate way of a man who paid to be remembered as respectable.

"Kassandra," he said. "The Eagle Bearer. And Theron, who opens doors before other men know they are locked. Please, sit."

Kassandra remained standing. Theron did the same.

Elpenor's pleasant expression adjusted by half a degree. "You have made the Cyclops look very small. I admire efficient work."

"You did not bring us here to admire it," Kassandra said.

"No. I have a problem on the mainland. A Spartan general is winning a war that benefits the wrong people. His soldiers call him the Wolf. I want him dead."

He put a purse on the table. The sound carried. Theron counted by ear and checked the shape of the bag.

"Two hundred drachmae now," Elpenor continued. "Eight hundred when you bring me his helmet. The general commands in Megaris."

A thousand drachmae to kill one of Sparta's most important generals was low enough to be insulting and high enough to tempt anyone trapped on Kephallonia. Elpenor had calculated for local hunger.

Kassandra looked at the purse. "Two hundred for two people."

"Two hundred to secure your interest."

"Three hundred now. Twelve hundred after."

Elpenor smiled. "You have not left your island, yet you price yourself like an Athenian fleet."

"An Athenian fleet would take longer."

Theron let the silence work. Elpenor wanted Kassandra in Megaris more than he wanted to protect five hundred drachmae. The contract was a Cult move against Nikolaos and a hook into the woman they still did not fully understand.

"Three hundred now," Elpenor said. "One thousand after."

Kassandra glanced at Theron.

He gave a small nod. The money mattered less than choosing when Elpenor believed he held the line.

"Name the Wolf," she said.

Elpenor reached into a leather case and placed a Spartan military token on the table. Lambda marked one side. A wolf's head had been cut into the other.

"Nikolaos of Sparta."

Kassandra did not move.

Theron watched her focus narrow to the token, the table, and the memory she kept behind both. Her Eagle Vision did not need to wake for this. She knew the mark. She knew the name.

"You knew," she said to Theron.

Elpenor looked between them with interest.

Theron chose the true part. "I knew the Wolf commanded in Megaris. I did not know this was the contract until the rider named Elpenor."

Kassandra kept her attention on him for another breath. Their rule covered assigned risk. This was hers, and he had given her the warning as soon as he could do it without Elpenor seeing too much.

She picked up the token.

"We accept," she said.

Elpenor slid the purse across the table. "Good. Sparta has blockaded Megaris. You will need a ship and a captain willing to test the gods."

"We will manage," Theron replied.

"I am sure you will."

That oily confidence nearly earned him an extra week alive out of professional respect. It did not.

Outside, Kassandra walked past the guards and down the hill without speaking. Theron kept beside her until the guest house disappeared behind the olive trees.

Then she stopped.

"Nikolaos."

"Yes."

"The first contract that can take us off this island, and it points at him."

"Elpenor chose us because it points at him."

She rubbed her thumb across the edge of the military token. "Why?"

"He wants the Wolf removed, and he wants to see what you do when you learn the name. There is an organization behind him. Wealthy, secretive, and interested in your family. I have pieces. I need proof before I tell you the rest."

Kassandra looked toward the sea. "Is Nikolaos part of it?"

"No. He is a target."

"And you are certain."

"Yes."

She put the token in her belt pouch. "Then we take their money, meet the Wolf, and learn why they want him dead."

Theron opened the contract purse after they reached an empty stretch of path. Three hundred drachmae, as promised. Kassandra divided it into two equal stacks on a flat stone.

It was the largest clean payment Theron had ever received. It covered none of the shop items that could alter the war. It could cover an excellent amount of rope.

Phoibe was waiting at the next bend.

Kassandra looked at her. "We told you not to follow the rider."

"I did not. I followed you."

"That is worse."

"It is also important. The Cyclops is at Kleptous Bay. He has a ship captain tied up at the beach and nearly twenty men with him. He says he will cut the captain apart until someone tells him where his eye is."

Theron looked back toward Elpenor's guest house, then west toward the bay.

A contract, a captain, and a ship. The day had become crowded.

"Did anyone see you?" he asked.

"No. Ikaros did."

The eagle circled high above the ridge as if called as a witness.

Kassandra sighed. "Next time, tell us before you scout a camp with twenty armed men."

Phoibe nodded quickly.

"Say it."

"Next time I tell you before I scout the armed camp."

"Good. Now show us where you watched from."

____

Kleptous Bay cut into the western coast behind steep stone walls. The Cyclops had built his camp across the beach and the terraces above it. Two watch platforms covered the road. A cave opened behind the main camp, its entrance hidden by patched sailcloth.

The Adrestia sat at anchor beyond the shallows. She was a lean trireme with a bronze ram and a red eye painted on either side of her bow. Six of her crew were penned behind a cargo fence near the water. More men remained aboard under guard.

Barnabas was tied to a post beside the Cyclops's dining table.

Ikaros gave Kassandra the open shape of the camp while they lay behind a limestone shelf above the bay. Nineteen armed men. Two archers on the road platforms. Four around the crew enclosure. Five near the water. The rest clustered around the Cyclops.

Glaux took the cave.

Theron shared her sight in brief pieces. The owl slipped through a tear above the sailcloth and crossed a storage chamber lit by three low lamps. Spare weapons lined one wall. Amphorae of oil stood in wooden racks. A narrow passage reached the beach behind Barnabas's post.

The cave also held the Cyclops's remaining records. Three shelves, two chests, and a writing table sat behind a locked screen.

He had brought what remained of his network into one defensible place.

Kassandra drew the bay in the dust. "I take the road platform, then come down the center."

"That puts every man in the camp in front of you."

"Yes."

"You say that like it solves the problem."

"It often does."

Theron marked the cave passage. "I enter here, open the crew pen, and free Barnabas. Glaux guides me through the cave. Ikaros stays with you and watches the water."

Phoibe leaned over the map. "What do I do?"

"Go back to the vineyard," Kassandra said.

"I found them."

"And you did that well. This part has arrows."

Phoibe frowned at the camp. "I can signal from the ridge."

"We already know where everyone is," Theron said. "What we do not know is whether the Cyclops sent another crew toward Markos while we came here. You take the upper path home, tell Markos to move the workers into Sami, and watch the southern road from the vineyard shed. That keeps them safe."

It was necessary work. Phoibe knew it.

"One signal if men come," she said. "Then I leave by the back path."

"Exactly."

Kassandra touched Phoibe's shoulder. "Run all the way home."

Phoibe left uphill at a pace she could hold.

Theron opened the shop after she disappeared beyond the ridge.

He had spent years arranging caches, merchants, and plausible explanations around System goods. Today she assumed he had planned for a harbor fight because he usually planned for everything.

Theron opened the black case.

The obsidian eye stared up at them, polished and intact.

"We show it to him," he said. "He keeps his attention on the only thing he came here to recover. When you throw the case, he will follow it."

"And if he catches it?"

"Then you take it back after you kill him."

Kassandra smiled. "Simple plan."

"One part of it."

"The best part."

They waited for sunset.

Glaux entered the cave first.

Theron followed through a crack above the high-tide line, carrying the net against his back. Salt water reached his waist before the passage climbed into dry stone. The owl waited on a beam inside, yellow eyes turned toward the locked screen.

Above the bay, Kassandra walked into firelight with the black case under one arm.

The Cyclops stood when he saw her. He was broad, shaved bald, and dressed in enough bronze to make swimming a theoretical concern. A leather patch covered his left eye.

"The little eagle," he said. "You emptied my warehouse. You broke my seal. You gave my coin back to peasants."

Kassandra stopped beyond spear reach. "They were unhappy."

"You have my eye."

She opened the case.

Every man near the table looked at the obsidian piece. So did the Cyclops.

Theron slid bronze wire into the screen lock. The Forge Key separated the mechanism into worn pins and a simple spring. One turn lifted the catch.

Inside, he found the warehouse ledger copies, harbor records, and a stack of debt tablets. He pushed all of them into a grain sack. Then he wrapped the first wire coil around the oil-rack supports and pulled it tight.

The Cyclops's voice carried through the passage.

"Give it to me, and I may kill Markos quickly. The girl too, if she asks politely."

Kassandra's answer came flat. "You will never go near them again."

That was the end of the negotiation.

Theron tipped one oil flask beneath the weapon rack. He left the second sealed, drew his knife, and moved through the beach passage.

Barnabas saw him first. The captain had grey in his beard, blood along one side of his face, and the expression of a man who had reached the interesting part of a story.

Theron put one finger to his lips.

Barnabas nodded with great seriousness, then looked pointedly at the two guards behind Theron.

Glaux dropped from the cave roof and struck the first guard across the face. He shouted and lifted both hands. Theron caught the second man's spear below its head, pulled him into the passage, and drove a knee into his stomach.

Barnabas kicked the first guard behind the knee.

Theron struck the man's temple with the knife pommel. Both guards stayed down.

"A clever owl," Barnabas whispered.

"She knows. Do not encourage her."

He cut the captain's bonds.

Kassandra threw the black case.

It passed over the Cyclops's shoulder and landed open in the sand. The eye rolled toward the water. He turned after it, exactly as Theron had expected.

Kassandra crossed the distance before his men understood the fight had begun.

She drove the base of the Spear into his breastplate and sent him backward across the table. Food, cups, and one expensive lamp went into the sand. Two guards attacked from her right. Ikaros dropped at the first archer, forcing him away from the platform rail.

Theron handed Barnabas a captured spear. "Your crew?"

"The pen and the ship. Twelve aboard, six there. Loyal people. Very angry."

"Good. Open the pen. I will release the ship."

"And miss this?"

"You can tell the story afterward."

Barnabas considered that and ran for the enclosure.

Theron pulled the second rope from his shoulder and looped it through the fence latch. Three Cyclops guards turned toward him. The heavy net lay folded between them.

He pulled the rope.

The net lifted from the sand, caught all three at the shins, and folded them into each other. Theron had bought a net meant to hold struggling tuna. Three surprised men performed below specification.

Barnabas reached the pen and freed the first sailor. That sailor took a club from the nearest fallen guard. The balance of the beach changed quickly after that.

The Cyclops came off the broken table with a heavy axe in both hands. Kassandra stepped around the first swing. The blade split the bench behind her. She struck his wrist, but the bronze bracer held.

He used the axe haft to drive her back.

Four more men closed from the beach. Kassandra saw them through her broad awareness and moved before the circle formed. She ducked beneath one spear, caught the shaft, and used it to bar the second. Then she kicked the first man into the Cyclops's path.

Theron ran for the boarding boats.

Two narrow craft rested above the tide, each held by a single stern line. He cut one line and shoved the hull down the wet sand. The boat drifted sideways into the shallows. He used the remaining bronze wire to lock the second boat's steering oar against its rail.

The Cyclops's men could swim to the Adrestia if they wished. Theron preferred them tired when they arrived.

A guard jumped from the ship's rail into waist-deep water and charged him. Theron's concentrated sight caught a loose strap beneath the man's shield grip. He threw the sealed oil flask at the shield.

The man raised it by reflex. The flask struck, oil spread across the wood, and the loose strap tore free under the sudden weight. Theron stepped inside and hit him twice, once under the ribs and once behind the jaw.

The man dropped into the water.

"Waste of good oil," Theron thought.

The Adrestia's crew acted as soon as Barnabas shouted. Two sailors overwhelmed their guard at the stern. Another cut the line holding the boarding ladder up. Theron climbed while the fight on the beach broke into smaller fights behind him.

He reached the deck, cut three bound crewmen free, and pointed toward the bow.

"Can you move her?"

A woman with a split lip took the knife from him and cut the next rope. "Give us our captain."

"He is opening the pen. Bring the ship close enough for him to board."

She shouted orders. Oars dropped into the water one bank at a time. The Adrestia turned in the bay.

Theron looked back toward the camp.

The Cyclops had lost most of his men. Some fled up the terraces. Others threw down weapons when Barnabas's freed crew reached them from behind.

Kassandra still had the largest problem.

The Cyclops swung low. She jumped over the axe head, landed close, and drove the broken Spear into the gap beneath his arm. He struck her with his free hand before she could pull it back.

Kassandra hit the sand and rolled. Blood marked the edge of her mouth when she rose.

The Cyclops charged.

Theron followed the line from the man's breastplate to its side buckles. One had been repaired with softer bronze. He could reach it from the Adrestia with a bow. He did not have a bow.

He did have a ship.

"Ram the beach," he told the woman at the steering oars.

She stared at him. "This is a trireme."

"Only the shallow edge. Turn the bow three points south."

"Three what?"

Theron pointed. "There."

The Adrestia's bronze ram drove into the wet sand below the Cyclops. The impact shook the deck and threw him one step sideways.

One step was enough.

Kassandra caught the repaired buckle with the hooked base of the Spear and tore it free. The breastplate opened along his right side. She struck the exposed ribs, turned past his axe, and drove the Spear under the edge of his patch.

The Cyclops fell beside the obsidian eye he had spent the fight trying to recover.

Kassandra stood over him for a moment. Then she picked up the eye, wiped sand from its gold rim, and put the intact capital back in the case.

Barnabas waded toward the Adrestia with six freed sailors around him. "You rammed my ship into my island."

Theron looked down at the bronze prow. "Your ship?"

Barnabas laughed so loudly that men on the road platform turned to listen.

____

The Adrestia came free with the next tide. Her ram had a fresh scrape. Barnabas inspected it three times, complained twice, and then admitted the sand had done no real damage.

His crew bound the surviving Cyclops men and left them beside the road with water, no weapons, and several hours to reconsider their employment.

The dead received a shorter arrangement. The Cyclops went under a cairn above the bay because Barnabas refused to have him thrown into Poseidon's sea.

"The god has enough problems," he explained.

Kassandra sat on an overturned crate while Theron cleaned the cut along her forearm. The Cyclops's axe had touched the bracer and opened the skin below it.

"Three stitches," Theron said.

"Two."

"Three closes it."

"Two hurts less."

"That is not how stitches work."

Barnabas crouched across from them. "You came for me without knowing me."

"We came for the Cyclops," Kassandra said.

"And a ship," Theron added.

"Honest heroes. The gods rarely send those."

"We are misthioi," Kassandra said. "Elpenor hired us to reach Megaris."

Barnabas's humor eased. "Megaris is under Spartan blockade. Athens holds the city, Sparta starves it, and every captain with sense sails around both."

"Do you have sense?" Theron asked.

"On calm days. Today you freed me, freed my crew, killed the man who stole my ship, and scraped my ram. The accounts favor you by a narrow margin."

Kassandra looked toward the Adrestia. "Can you get us through?"

"I can get you anywhere the sea reaches. Sometimes farther, if the gods are drunk."

Theron finished the third stitch before Kassandra could renegotiate.

Barnabas offered his forearm. Kassandra took it.

"Then take us to Megaris," she said.

"Gladly. But first we collect provisions, rowers, and anyone on Kephallonia you would regret leaving without a goodbye. A ship should never begin a voyage with regret. It attracts storms."

Theron had never seen that mechanic in the games. Barnabas believed it enough for everyone.

They returned to the vineyard after midnight.

Phoibe opened the gate before they knocked. Markos stood behind her holding a kitchen knife in one hand and the dolphin cup in the other.

"I was ready," he said.

"To drink at them?" Kassandra asked.

"The cup is distracting."

Phoibe saw the blood on Kassandra's arm. "What happened?"

"The Cyclops died," Kassandra said. "We have a ship."

Phoibe looked from her to Theron. "You got a ship in one evening?"

"It needs rowers," Theron said.

"Everything you buy is broken."

"We did not buy this one."

Markos lowered the knife. "A ship is an asset. A moving vineyard, except it carries goods and does not need rain."

"You are never borrowing against it," Kassandra said.

"I had not suggested such a thing."

"You were breathing in."

Barnabas had brought the Adrestia into Sami, the port town below the eastern hills, and half the harbor wanted to join him now that the Cyclops was dead. Markos had already promised wine to the crew at a price nobody had agreed to. Phoibe carried copies of the warehouse ledger between the vineyard and three village elders.

Theron gave her the original route list and the broken bronze seal.

"Keep these in separate places," he said. "If one of the Cyclops's officials tries to take the vineyard, send a copy of his payment record to Kranioi and another to Sami. Markos knows which elders received copies."

Phoibe tucked the list inside her tunic. "I know too. I delivered them."

"That is why I am giving this to you."

She looked toward the harbor. "I could come."

Kassandra sat beside her on the wall. "You could. We are sailing into a war to meet a man I may want to kill. Markos needs someone who knows where the records are and when he is lying."

"That is always."

"Then it is steady work."

Phoibe picked at the edge of the bronze seal. "You will come back?"

"Yes," Kassandra said.

Theron knew the road ahead would bring them to Athens before Kephallonia again. Phoibe's own route would eventually pull her there. He had years before the danger he remembered and no intention of letting her walk into it unprepared.

"Barnabas sails these waters," he said. "When the island is settled, we will send for you and Markos. Not into a war camp. Somewhere with doors."

"And food," Phoibe said.

"Good doors and food."

Markos joined them carrying three travel bags and a small amphora. "I packed provisions. Cheese, bread, olives, and one very fine wine from my private reserve."

Kassandra lifted the amphora. The seal named a cheap vintner in Sami.

"Private reserve?"

"It became private when I bought it."

She put it in the bag.

Markos hugged her first. The surprise on Kassandra's face lasted less than a breath before she put one arm around him.

Then he turned to Theron. "You have always been the sensible one."

"That cannot be what you meant to say."

"Bring her home. Bring yourself home too. Preferably with profit."

"There it is."

Markos pulled him into the hug anyway.

At the harbor, Barnabas stood on the Adrestia's deck calling every new rower by the wrong name. Ikaros circled above the mast. Glaux had claimed a shaded crossbeam near the stern and watched the crew load sacks around her.

Kassandra stepped aboard first.

Theron followed and checked his balance while the last mooring line came free.
