# Chapter 4: The Price of an Eye

"They are coming for Markos."

Phoibe reached the yard with dust to her knees and enough breath left to make the warning clear. She bent over, took two quick breaths, then straightened before either Theron or Kassandra could tell her to sit.

"The Cyclops's men," she continued. "Seven on the road from Kranioi. Maybe eight. They asked where Markos keeps his new vineyard, and a fisherman told them. He is an idiot."

Kassandra set down the wooden practice knife. "How long?"

"Less than an hour if they keep walking. Longer if they stop to threaten everyone on the way. They like doing that."

Theron looked toward the ridge. Ikaros had already turned south, a dark shape climbing into the open morning. Glaux left the roof at his whistle and flew low toward the olive groves.

Phoibe watched both birds go. "I saw four spears, two bows, and one man carrying a club. The leader has a red scarf and a bronze plate sewn over his left shoulder."

That was better information than most clients supplied after paying.

"Where is Markos?" Theron asked.

"At the vineyard. He said the grapes needed his personal attention."

Kassandra picked up the Spear of Leonidas and fitted it at her hip. "He is hiding behind a wine press."

"He called it supervising."

"Of course he did."

Theron went into the house and opened the System shop where nobody could see him staring at empty air.

`Bronze wire, 20 cubits ... 2 dr 4 ob`

`Numbing salve, pot ........ 1 dr 2 ob`

`Rope, 10 cubits ........... 3 ob`

The salve was insurance. The wire and rope were a plan.

The items appeared on his workbench. Theron packed them into his field bag, added two wooden wedges, a folded pulley, chalk, and the compact grapnel Kassandra had failed to break last winter.

When he returned to the yard, Kassandra had put Phoibe on the water jar and was checking her feet for cuts.

"I can still run," Phoibe said.

"That is why I am checking what you run on," Kassandra replied.

Theron spread a wax tablet on the ground. Markos's vineyard sat on the western slope above Sami, the port town on the island's eastern coast. The house and press yard occupied the middle terrace. A cart track climbed from the south, while a footpath crossed the upper vines and joined the road near an old stone storage shed.

"Ikaros watches the road," he said. "Glaux checks the press house and upper shed. We meet Markos, clear any workers, then let the collectors enter the yard."

Kassandra tapped the lower gate on his drawing. "You close this behind them."

"With the cart. I put wire across the narrow lane to separate the first three from the rest. You take the yard. I disable the archers or force them down from the terrace."

"Risks."

Fifteen years had turned the word into habit.

"They may split before reaching the gate. They may have a scout ahead. The press house gives them cover we cannot see through from the road. My wire will stop a man at a run, but a spear can cut it. If their leader carries the Cyclops's authority, he may have more men close enough to hear a horn."

Kassandra nodded. "My risk is the center. If all seven reach me together, I move toward the press where they cannot surround me. If I say leave, you take Phoibe by the upper path."

Phoibe leaned over the tablet. "My risk?"

"You do not have one," Kassandra said.

"Then I am not in the plan."

Kassandra looked at Theron. He left that negotiation where it belonged.

Phoibe pointed at the old storage shed. "I can watch the road from there. If more men come, I hang the white cloth outside. If someone goes around the vineyard, I hang the red one. Then I take the back path home."

"The shed has two exits," Theron said. "Glaux checks it first. You signal once and leave. You do not come toward the fight."

"I know what once means."

"Say it anyway," Kassandra said.

Phoibe rolled her eyes. "Signal once. Leave by the back. Do not come toward the fight."

Kassandra picked up the tablet. "Good. Now we find Markos before he sells them the vineyard to pay for the vineyard."

____

Markos was behind the wine press.

He came out when Kassandra called his name a second time. Grape skins clung to one sandal. He wore his best blue tunic, which suggested he had intended to negotiate with armed collectors before choosing the press as a better financial strategy.

"Wonderful, you received my message," he said.

Phoibe folded her arms. "You did not send one."

"I was about to. Initiative is a valuable quality."

Theron looked across the vineyard. Four hired workers were cutting weeds between the vines. "Send them down to Sami."

Markos's smile faded. "The Cyclops only wants a conversation."

"He sent seven armed men," Kassandra said.

"A serious conversation."

"How much do you owe?" Theron asked.

Markos adjusted his tunic. "The original amount is not important."

"How much?"

"Eight hundred drachmae."

Phoibe made a choking sound.

"You borrowed eight hundred drachmae to buy this place?" Kassandra asked.

Markos spread his hands toward the terraces. "Look at it. Land. Vines. A press. In two harvests it pays for itself."

Theron checked the vines. Half were old enough to produce. A quarter had blight on the lower leaves. The irrigation channel leaked above the third terrace.

"Four harvests," he said. "If the blight stops and nobody burns it."

"Three," Markos replied. "Let us not become pessimists."

Ikaros called from above. Kassandra turned south.

"They're close."

Markos sent the workers away without another argument. Phoibe ran to the upper shed with two cloths tucked beneath her belt. Glaux had already checked the roof, floor, and rear path through the quick, uneven images she shared with Theron.

The shed was empty.

He told Phoibe exactly where to wait and watched her bar the road-side door from within. She could leave through the narrow rear opening without crossing the approaching crew.

Then he went to work.

The cart rolled easily once Kassandra lifted the rear axle off its stone block. They left it beside the lower gate with the brake loose and a wedge under one wheel. Theron ran bronze wire across the lane one terrace higher, wrapped both ends around olive trunks, and covered the line with vine cuttings.

He tied the new rope to the press-house awning. The awning was built from hide stretched over three poles. Pulling the rope would drop the front edge without bringing down anything heavy enough to kill a man.

Terrain control had improved since a rope across a quarry ramp. Better materials helped. So did fifteen years of people trying to stab them.

Theron took position beside the irrigation channel. Kassandra walked into the middle of the press yard and rested one hand near the Spear.

She looked alone.

Nobody who knew her would have found that reassuring.

The collectors entered through the lower gate in two groups. Seven men, as Phoibe had counted. Three carried spears. Two had bows unstrung over their shoulders. One carried a club. The leader wore the red scarf and bronze shoulder plate.

An eighth man followed thirty paces behind them, moving along the upper vines.

Glaux saw him from inside the rafters of the storage shed. Theron caught the image, one sandal slipping on loose soil, a short knife in his right hand. He was watching the shed rather than Kassandra.

Theron whistled twice.

Phoibe hung the red cloth, slipped through the rear opening, and started down the back path exactly as agreed.

The eighth man saw the cloth move.

He ran after her.

Theron pulled the cord beside his knee. The grapnel released from the terrace wall and dragged a woven basket across the path. The man stepped through it, caught both shins, and fell into a row of vines.

Phoibe looked back once.

"Keep moving," Theron called.

She did.

The seven men in the yard turned toward his voice. Kassandra used the moment.

She struck the nearest spear shaft with the base of Leonidas's broken weapon. The old wood split at the man's grip. Her second blow caught his chest and put him into the press trough.

Theron kicked the cart wedge free.

The cart rolled across the lower gate. Two collectors turned to stop it. That left five in the yard and gave Kassandra room to move.

The leader drew a curved sword. "The Cyclops owns this land."

"Then he should weed it," Kassandra said.

One archer reached for his bow. Theron threw the pulley. It was a poor weapon and an excellent piece of iron. It struck the man's fingers before he could string the bow.

The second archer ran up the terrace lane to gain distance. His foot caught the bronze wire. He hit the ground hard enough to lose the bow.

Theron reached him first.

The collector rolled and drew a knife. Theron saw the split in its horn grip, the tension in the man's wrist, and the path his shoulder had already chosen. Concentrated Eagle Vision reduced the immediate world to a hand, a blade, and three useful angles.

He stepped outside the cut, trapped the wrist against his forearm, and struck the elbow with his palm. The knife dropped. Theron kicked it downhill and pressed his own iron blade beneath the man's jaw.

"Stay there."

The man stayed.

In the yard, Kassandra drove one attacker backward into another. The club came at her from the side. She caught the man's arm, turned under it, and sent him over her hip into a basket of grape skins.

The leader attacked while she released him. His sword cut the edge of her bracer.

Kassandra hit the bronze plate on his left shoulder with the Spear.

The leather stitches broke. The plate spun away, and the leader followed it with the confused expression of a man discovering that armor could become a handle.

He landed beside the press.

The eighth collector had cut himself free of the basket. Instead of running toward Kassandra, he changed direction and went after Phoibe again. She had nearly reached the bend in the back path.

Kassandra saw him through her broad awareness.

"Leave her, malaka!"

The curse came out hard enough to turn two heads.

She threw the broken Spear.

It crossed the terrace and struck the dirt in front of the man's leading foot. He tried to stop, stumbled, and Phoibe used both hands to shove him into the irrigation channel.

Kassandra reached him before he could stand. She picked up the Spear and put its point against his chest.

"She told you once," Phoibe said.

The man lifted both hands.

Theron almost corrected her. Kassandra had shouted once. Phoibe had never told him anything.

It seemed rude to damage a good line.

The rest of the fight ended quickly. The two men at the cart found its brake, moved it half a pace, and discovered Kassandra had finished with everyone else. They dropped their weapons.

Markos emerged from the press house after the last collector was tied.

"A complete success," he said. "Exactly as I expected."

Kassandra pointed at the grape skins on his sandal. "Go back inside."

He considered her face and obeyed.

____

The red-scarfed leader needed less convincing than Theron expected.

Kassandra sat opposite him on the edge of the press. Phoibe ate figs nearby and watched with open interest. Theron emptied the man's satchel onto a low table.

It held forty-three drachmae and two obols in collected coin, six debt tablets, a bronze seal shaped like an eye, and a route list for the next four days.

One tablet named Markos. Eight hundred drachmae borrowed, twelve hundred now demanded. The extra four hundred had appeared beneath the original figure in different wax.

Markos leaned over Theron's shoulder. "Interest."

"Fraud," Theron said.

"The distinction depends on who keeps the account."

Theron moved the tablet away from him.

The route list identified a warehouse below Kranioi, the fortified settlement in southern Kephallonia. Collections arrived there each evening before going west to the Cyclops's camp. A note beside tonight's entry mentioned an eye received from a merchant in Kirrha.

The obsidian eye.

In the game it had been an expensive joke involving a goat. Here it was cut black glass with enough value to purchase a ship, pay mercenaries, or keep a local strongman supplied for a year. Theron had no intention of putting it near livestock.

"How many guards at the warehouse?" Kassandra asked.

The leader looked at her, then at the broken stitches hanging from his shoulder armor. "Twelve at night. More when a shipment comes."

"Entrances?" Theron asked.

"Front gate. Roof hatch. Drain on the east wall. A boy might fit."

Phoibe stopped chewing.

"No," Kassandra and Theron said together.

Phoibe swallowed. "I did not say anything."

"Your face did," Kassandra replied.

Theron opened the Forge Key's translation trace over the bronze seal. It was ordinary metal, made locally, with a filed notch on one edge to prove authenticity. The route list used the same notch beside rerouted collections.

"The seal gets us through the front gate," he said. "The warehouse ledger tells us where the Cyclops keeps pressure on the island. We take the ledger, the eye, and any collateral he is holding. We leave the coin that belongs to named debtors."

Markos raised one finger. "As a named debtor, I support this policy."

"You still borrowed the original eight hundred," Theron said.

"A detail for later."

Kassandra looked at the prisoners. "And these?"

"They walk south carrying a message. The collection failed because the vineyard had eight armed guards. That explains the delay until we reach the warehouse."

The leader stared at Theron. "There were two of you."

"Then lie with confidence. Markos can teach you."

Markos looked pleased until he decided it was probably an insult.

They kept the crew's weapons and returned the forty-three drachmae and two obols to the six people named on the tablets. Phoibe knew five of them and found the sixth before sunset.

Nobody had hired her to do it.

That was why she was good.

____

The warehouse gate opened for the eye seal.

Theron wore the red scarf and walked beside a mule carrying two empty baskets. Kassandra followed in a collector's cloak with the hood low. The dark hid her height poorly, but men who recognized authority usually tried to explain strange details to themselves.

Glaux had entered through the roof hatch ten minutes earlier. Her sight gave Theron the main floor, the loft, and the narrow office at the back. Nine guards were visible. Two more played dice beyond the east wall. The last slept beside the office door.

Twelve, exactly as promised.

Ikaros waited above the road to warn Kassandra if reinforcements came from Kranioi. Phoibe remained at the vineyard with Markos and instructions to send the workers home if anyone approached.

No one had been assigned a risk they had not heard.

The gate guard looked at the seal, saw the filed notch, and waved them inside.

Theron led the mule toward the storage racks. Rows of confiscated amphorae, tools, grain sacks, fishing nets, and two small chests filled the floor. He did not need to examine every object. The office had the ledger. Glaux had seen a black stone case on the desk.

Kassandra stopped near the center support post.

Theron slipped the bronze wire from his sleeve and bent it through the gate's inner latch. The next person who pulled the release would lift an empty lever.

Then he dropped a clay bead.

It rolled beneath the office table.

Glaux struck the oil lamp above the loft. The lamp swung from its chain and threw light against the far wall. Three guards looked up. Kassandra kicked the center support brace out of its shallow socket.

The storage rack leaned across the floor.

It did not fall. Theron's rope caught it at an angle and left a narrow passage on one side, separating six guards from the office and the rest from Kassandra.

She pulled off the cloak.

"Evening," she said.

The first guard reached her with a spear. Kassandra knocked the point high, stepped inside his grip, and put him into the rack. Two others attacked from opposite sides. Her Eagle Vision had already given her both paths. She ducked one, caught the other by his belt, and drove him shoulder-first into his companion.

Theron entered the office.

The sleeping guard woke and grabbed for his ankle. Theron stepped on the man's wrist, drew the iron knife, and cut the cord holding his sword sheath closed. By the time the guard pulled the weapon free, the sheath came with it.

Theron struck him behind the ear with the knife pommel.

The man stopped participating.

The office door had a bronze lock with three pins. The Forge Key showed wear on the upper pin and a warped spring beneath it. Theron inserted the eye seal, turned until the first pin caught, then pressed the filed notch against the spring.

The lock opened.

Inside the desk lay the warehouse ledger, three bags of coin, fourteen signed debt tablets, and the black stone case.

Theron opened the case.

The obsidian eye rested in red cloth, polished black at the center and ringed with gold. A line of white stone around the pupil made it look irritated by the quality of its surroundings.

The System supplied an appraisal.

`[ Obsidian Eye ]`

`[ Estimated open-market value: 2,400 dr ]`

`[ System barter credit: 1,760 dr ]`

`[ Isu compatibility: none ]`

`[ Ownership status: disputed ]`

Disputed was doing considerable work.

He closed the case and put it in his bag with the ledger. The three coin bags matched tonight's collected entries. He left them on the desk.

Outside, someone blew a horn.

Ikaros answered from above with a sharp cry. Kassandra looked toward Theron.

"Four coming from the road," she said.

"We have what we came for. East wall."

He pulled the rope holding the rack. The top lowered another foot, forcing the remaining guards back. Kassandra ran up the slanted frame, crossed two shelves, and jumped down beside him.

Theron opened the drain cover with the warped office spring. It was too narrow for Kassandra and too obvious for Phoibe, which made it useful for exactly one thing.

He fed the grapnel through, caught the outside bar, and pulled. The old mortar around the drain cracked. Kassandra put one heel against the wall and hauled the grate inward.

They left through an opening wide enough for both of them.

The four reinforcements reached the front gate and found the release lever disconnected. By the time they climbed over it, Kassandra and Theron were halfway into the olive grove.

Glaux flew above them with a strip of stolen sausage in her claws.

Theron had not assigned that part of the plan.

____

The Cyclops's local network failed before breakfast.

Phoibe carried copies of the collection routes to three villages. Markos sent two workers with the debt tablets and collateral lists. Theron used the eye seal to mark every named account paid, then broke the seal across the filed notch.

Kassandra returned confiscated tools and grain from the warehouse while its guards argued over who had let her inside. The fishermen of Kranioi reclaimed four boats before the Cyclops's men understood that the warehouse ledger was gone.

By midday, the island knew the Cyclops had lost his accounts, his seal, and the eye he had bought to prove he could afford one.

Fear depended on looking expensive and organized. He now looked neither.

One account remained unfinished.

Duris kept a pottery stall in Sami and owed Markos one hundred drachmae for a shipment of oil. Markos's tablet demanded two hundred. The second hundred had come from Markos rather than the Cyclops, which was almost impressive.

Kassandra stood beside a display of painted amphorae while Theron set the original tablet on Duris's counter.

Duris looked at the figure, then at her.

"Are you here to break my wares?"

"Do you plan to pay?" she asked.

"The original hundred, yes. I never agreed to two."

Theron placed Markos's altered copy beside the first. "The wax is newer around the second figure. He doubled it after you signed. Pay the hundred and we close it."

Duris blinked. "Markos sent you to collect less money?"

"Markos sent us to collect," Theron said. "The number became our problem."

Kassandra picked up a small cup painted with two badly proportioned dolphins. "How much for this?"

"Two obols."

"One. The dolphin looks ill."

"It is swimming."

"Poorly."

They settled on one obol and a promise that Duris would have the hundred drachmae delivered to the vineyard before sunset.

Theron paid for the cup from his purse rather than the System balance. Kassandra carried it home and told Phoibe she had won it in difficult negotiations.

Markos complained for six full minutes when they gave him one hundred drachmae instead of two hundred.

Then Theron showed him the Cyclops's original tablet, the false interest, and the warehouse ledger listing bribes paid to a harbor clerk who approved Markos's vineyard purchase.

Markos stopped complaining.

"He could take the land back," he said.

"He could have," Theron replied. "Now the clerk knows we have his name. The Cyclops knows we have the eye. Neither can move without giving us a reason to release the ledger."

Markos looked toward the locked chest beneath Theron's workbench. "How much is the eye worth?"

"Two thousand four hundred drachmae to a buyer who can protect the purchase. Less to anyone local."

"I know buyers."

Kassandra put a hand on the chest. "No."

"I know discreet buyers."

"No."

"One discreet conversation?"

"Markos."

He raised both hands. "It remains capital. Untouched capital. A wise reserve. I agree completely."

Duris's payment arrived before sunset. Markos had promised them one quarter of any debt they recovered. He tried to remember a different arrangement until Phoibe produced the wax tablet on which Theron had made him mark it.

They received twenty-five drachmae, split evenly with the spare obol going to Phoibe for delivering the copies.

`[ Balance: 314 dr 1 ob + 12 dr 3 ob = 326 dr 4 ob ]`

Phoibe held up her obol. "I should negotiate all your contracts."

"You would charge more than Markos," Theron said.

"Yes."

Kassandra drank from the badly painted dolphin cup and looked at the vineyard below them. The workers had returned. Nobody had burned the vines. Markos was already explaining to one of them that the day's trouble proved the land had become more valuable.

"The Cyclops will come himself," she said.

Theron knew exactly where that ended. A dead strongman, a freed captain, and a ship they would need for the rest of Greece.

"He will," Theron replied. "But first he has to work out how much we took from him."

A rider appeared on the southern road before the Cyclops found the answer. He wore a clean traveling cloak and carried a sealed message from the mainland.

Markos saw him first.

His opportunity face returned.
