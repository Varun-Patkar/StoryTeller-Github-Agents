# Chapter 7: The Edge of the Mountain

Nikolaos chose to meet them above the fort.

Of course he did.

Below the climbing path, Spartans moved Geraneia's captured grain toward the villages. Stentor led with six guards and demanded their weapons.

"No," Kassandra said.

She tossed him Elpenor's token. "Someone paid us three hundred drachmae to put a blade near the Wolf. Six guards should manage two misthioi."

Theron persuaded her to surrender the sword. The Spear of Leonidas stayed on her back.

Ikaros circled over the open summit while Glaux crossed the trees. Through the owl, Theron saw Nikolaos by the cliff, grey cloth behind a command tent, and an archer hidden on an eastern shelf.

"Two intruders," he told Kassandra. "You speak to Nikolaos. I stop them."

"Alive, if possible."

"Agreed. Ikaros watches for a third."

Their risks were stated. The old quarry rule still held.

At the last turn, Theron opened the shop out of sight.

`Bronze wire, 20 cubits x2 ... 5 dr 2 ob`

`Rope, 10 cubits x2 ........ 1 dr 0 ob`

`[ Purchase total: 6 dr 2 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 546 dr 2 ob ]`

The coils appeared inside his field bag. He closed the menu before Stentor could turn again.

Six drachmae and two obols was expensive for rope and wire. It was cheap for keeping a family argument from becoming a murder arranged by someone hiding in Phokis.

____

Nikolaos waited beside the cliff, grey in his beard and old cuts across both hands. He looked at Kassandra's Taygetos scar.

"You live," Nikolaos said.

"I noticed."

Stentor reported the fort's fall and Elpenor's contract.

"Take the guard below," Nikolaos said.

Stentor objected, but an order sent him and the guards below the last turn.

Nikolaos looked at Theron. "You as well."

"He stays," Kassandra said.

"This concerns my family."

"So does he."

Nikolaos accepted the boundary.

Kassandra looked at Theron. "Give us room."

Theron moved twenty paces away. His Eagle Vision picked out dark wool behind the tent and the metal cap of an arrow on the eastern shelf. Glaux waited in a split pine above it.

Kassandra walked toward Nikolaos.

"You knew me when Stentor said my name," she said. "You still made me fight for an audience."

"I command an army."

"You left me on a mountain for less."

"I watched you fall. I believed you dead."

"Then you returned to Sparta. Do you want pity for the empty house?"

"No."

"The oracle said Alexios would destroy Sparta," he continued. "The ephors demanded the law. I believed a Spartan obeyed."

"He was a baby."

"Yes."

"I tried to save him."

"Yes."

"You picked me up and threw me after him."

Nikolaos faced her. "Yes."

The concealed men waited for a condition.

Kassandra pulled the Spear from her back. Bronze caught the morning light.

"You taught me to protect the person beside me. When Sparta asked for Alexios, you gave him to them. When I acted on your lesson, you gave them me too."

Nikolaos looked at the Spear that had belonged to Leonidas. "I was the Wolf before I was your father. I used duty to make that sound honorable."

"Was it?"

"No."

The man behind the tent moved.

Theron let his field bag slide and caught one rope coil behind his leg. The intruder came around the canvas with a knife held low.

"Drop it," Theron said.

The man lunged. Theron moved outside the blade, caught his wrist, and drove it against the tent pole. The knife fell. A rope loop went over the attacker's other elbow and pulled both arms across his chest.

The man kicked Theron's thigh. Theron used the pole to hold him, tightened the rope, and struck once behind the jaw.

Kassandra had taught him the hold years ago. His first attempt had tied him to a fig tree. This one ended better.

He bound the man's ankles and found two knives, seventeen drachmae, lamp oil, and a folded strip of parchment.

The message contained one line.

*If the woman lowers her spear, kill the Wolf.*

No signature. No seal. No name.

The order was meant to force the ending. If Kassandra chose mercy, the hidden bow would kill Nikolaos and leave her standing over the body with the Spear in hand. Stentor would see a murderer. Kassandra would see another choice taken from her.

Elpenor might have written it. Another Cult member might have prepared for his failure. Theron had suspicion, motive, and no evidence he could use in public.

That was enough to set the next destination.

On the cliff, Nikolaos removed his helmet.

"Elpenor asked for this," he said.

He placed it on the stone between them.

"Take it. Finish what you came to do."

Kassandra stared at the helmet. "You want death to settle the account."

"I owe more than I can pay."

"A dead man pays nothing."

She raised the Spear.

The archer on the eastern shelf raised his bow with her.

Theron fixed bronze wire to the campaign table and ran it through the low brush to a loose stone above the shelf. Through Glaux's eyes, he saw the archer aim at Nikolaos.

Theron pulled. The stone struck the man's elbow, and his arrow cut through the command tent instead.

Glaux hit his bow hand. Theron reached the shelf as the man drew a short sword, caught its guard with the wire, and dragged the point aside. One strike to the throat and a sweep put the archer face-first in the scrub.

Theron bound him with the second rope. He carried twenty-three drachmae, five arrows, and no name.

Stentor's guards had heard the shout. Two started up from the lower turn.

"Stay there," Nikolaos ordered.

They stopped.

Kassandra had followed the attack through Ikaros. Theron saw her glance toward the bound archer, confirm he was handled, and return to Nikolaos.

The Spear still rested against his throat.

Theron remembered the dialogue choices that had once appeared at this moment. Revenge or mercy reduced to two prompts and a timed pause. The game had made the choice his because he held the controller.

Here, it belonged to Kassandra.

He gathered the captured weapons and stayed where he was.

Nikolaos met her eyes. "You deserved a father who chose you before Sparta."

"I deserved Alexios alive. I deserved my mother."

"Yes."

"Agreement does not repair it."

The Spear touched his skin. A thin line of red appeared beneath its point.

Then Kassandra lowered it.

"I will not kill you," she said. "That is my choice. It is not forgiveness."

Nikolaos looked at the helmet on the ground. "I understand."

"You live with what you did. If you repair any part of it, you do the work without asking me to forgive you."

Nikolaos bowed his head once. "Then I accept it."

Kassandra put the Spear away.

For the first time since they reached the summit, Nikolaos looked uncertain about the next words.

"There is another truth," he said. "I should have told you when you were a child."

Kassandra waited.

"I am not your birth father."

Kassandra stared at him. Anger gave way to a blank, sharp attention.

"Say that again."

"I am not the man who fathered you."

"You waited until I had a spear at your throat to mention this?"

Nikolaos looked toward the gulf. "I should have told you when you were old enough to ask."

"I asked why I looked like Mother's family. I asked why your friends called me Leonidas's little shadow."

"You were Leonidas's granddaughter. That was answer enough for a child."

"It was answer enough for you."

He accepted the correction.

Kassandra paced away from the cliff, then turned back. Her hand touched the Spear again, more for something solid than as a threat.

"Did you know before you married her?"

"Yes. Myrrine carried you when she became my wife."

"And you raised me."

"As my daughter."

"Until Sparta told you I was not worth the trouble."

Nikolaos's mouth tightened. "Blood had nothing to do with Taygetos."

"That does not improve it."

"No."

Theron stayed near the prisoners and let them speak. The game had delivered this revelation in a few lines because quests needed to move. Kassandra had lived inside the missing years. She deserved every question she could make Nikolaos answer.

"Who was my father?" she asked.

"Myrrine never told me his name."

"You never demanded it?"

"Once. Before we married. She told me the man was gone and you were hers. I told her that made you ours."

The answer caught Kassandra badly. For one moment she looked like the girl who had wanted Nikolaos to be better, rather than the woman deciding what remained of him.

Then she looked at the cut on his throat.

"You knew how to choose me once," she said.

"Yes."

"What changed?"

"I let Sparta decide what kind of man I was."

"Sparta did not carry me to the edge."

"I did."

The direct answer left her nowhere easy to put the anger. Nikolaos did not defend himself, and shouting at agreement would only make her repeat what he already knew.

She walked to the campaign table and put both hands on its edge. The wax map showed roads, forts, and units arranged into neat problems. Families did not fit on military maps. Nikolaos had apparently spent years proving that.

"You said Alexios was your son by blood," she said.

"He was."

Theron knew that claim was false. Pythagoras had fathered both children, though Nikolaos had no reason to doubt Alexios was his. Correcting him would steal Myrrine's truth and expose knowledge no Kephallonian misthios should possess.

"So you obeyed the oracle even when it asked for your blood," Kassandra said.

"Yes."

"I almost wish blood explained it."

Nikolaos flinched. It was the first blow she landed that morning without touching him.

"Where is my mother?" Kassandra asked.

"After Taygetos, Myrrine searched below the mountain. She questioned the priests, the guards, and every traveler who used the southern road. Then she searched for Alexios."

"You went with her."

It was not a question.

Nikolaos answered anyway. "For three days. The army recalled me."

"And the Wolf obeyed."

"The Wolf always obeyed when obedience was easier."

Kassandra looked toward the Spartan camps. Thousands of men below would have called that discipline. Theron could see what she called it.

"Myrrine left Sparta," Nikolaos continued. "She had no family left there, and she would no longer share a house with me. I heard she traveled east. After that, nothing I trusted."

"Did you follow?"

Nikolaos paused.

Kassandra nodded once. "There is the answer."

"I used the war to avoid the house I destroyed."

"You keep saying what you did as if naming it makes you brave."

"It only makes the account honest."

"Then keep counting."

Nikolaos took that without reply.

The silence lasted until a bound prisoner tested his rope. Theron put a foot against the knot, and the man stopped trying.

"Find Myrrine," Nikolaos said. "She can tell you who your father was. She may also know more about Alexios."

Kassandra turned from the table. "Alexios fell from Taygetos."

"So did you. No body was recovered from the mountain. Not yours. Not his."

"You think he lived?"

"I think certainty has cost this family enough."

That was dangerously close to hope. Kassandra handled it like an unfamiliar weapon, careful of every edge.

Kassandra turned toward the gulf. She had arrived for one answer and received a road full of them.

Theron carried the captured bow back to the command tent.

"We find her," he said.

Kassandra looked at him.

"Elpenor brought us here. These men came to decide the outcome. He knows something about your family, or the people behind him do. Phokis is a place to start."

She looked at the unsigned instruction in his hand. "Show me."

Theron passed it over.

She read the sentence twice. "They wanted him dead if I spared him."

"And wanted Stentor to blame you."

Kassandra folded the parchment and put it beneath her bracer. "Then we ask Elpenor about my mother."

"He still owes us a thousand drachmae."

"He can answer before we discuss the contract."

"A generous order of operations."

"I am in a generous mood."

Stentor returned with the guards and saw the blood at Nikolaos's throat.

"One prisoner carried an order to kill him if Kassandra showed mercy," Theron said. "No name."

"You spared him," Stentor said.

Nikolaos stepped between them. "Return to Geraneia. Hold the road, distribute the grain, and command the campaign. The Wolf is dead."

"You leave when she arrives?"

"Your sister spared my life."

"She is no sister of mine."

Kassandra accepted her sword belt from a guard. "Good. I have enough family problems today."

Stentor ordered the prisoners taken to the fort. His next order sent two men to check the grain wagons before he followed them downhill.

____

Kassandra started downhill. Theron stayed long enough to recover his wire.

"Stentor will chase your approval into every war Sparta offers him," Theron told Nikolaos. "Stay close. One day his anger will put him across a battlefield from Kassandra. Be there before either draws blood."

"And if she wants no family from Sparta?"

"Respect it. Repair is work you offer, not another duty you place on her."

Nikolaos looked down the path. "I will watch Stentor. When that day comes, I will stand between them."

That was his first payment.

____

Kassandra waited where the path joined the western road. A driver had paid her with bread for helping free a grain wagon.

"What did you say to Nikolaos?" she asked, handing Theron half.

"I gave him useful work."

They walked toward the anchorage with Ikaros above and Glaux ahead.

Kassandra ate without tasting much of it. She looked back once, though the summit had already disappeared behind the pines.

"He called me his daughter," she said. "Then he told me I was not his."

"Both can be true."

"That sounds like something Sokrates would say before charging for wine."

"I think he gets other people to buy the wine."

The small joke failed to move her. Theron let it fail.

A grain wagon passed them on its way west. One of the guards walking beside it recognized Kassandra and watched her until the road turned. News would travel through the camp before the Adrestia cleared the gulf. By midday, half the army would believe she had killed Nikolaos. The other half would believe she had married him, robbed him, or challenged Stentor for command.

The truth would be less popular. It usually was.

"Myrrine may be alive," Kassandra said.

"Yes."

"You believe she is."

"I believe Elpenor chose you because of Nikolaos, and those men knew you might spare him. Someone has studied your family."

"That was not my question."

"It is the answer I can prove."

Kassandra stopped at a bend above the water. The Adrestia waited in the anchorage below, small enough from this height to hide the scrape on her ram.

"You do that," she said. "You give me the part you can prove and keep the rest behind your teeth."

Theron looked toward the ship. Fifteen years of careful truths had built the life around them. The System remained impossible to explain, and knowledge of Pythagoras, Deimos, and the Cult's sanctuary could get people killed if spoken at the wrong time.

"Yes," he said.

Kassandra seemed more surprised by the admission than an excuse would have made her.

"Is the rest dangerous?"

"Some of it. Some would sound like madness. Some belongs to people we have not met yet."

"Does any of it change where we go now?"

"No. Elpenor first. The Pythia after him. Both are in Phokis, and both know pieces of your mother's road."

She considered that, then started walking again.

"You will tell me when it changes the plan," she said.

"Before it changes the plan."

"Good. I have had enough surprises for one mountain."

The answer was not forgiveness either. Theron did not need it to be. Their rule remained intact, and Kassandra still chose the road.

At the beach, two Spartan sailors were arguing with Barnabas about anchorage fees. Barnabas insisted rescuing a captain from torture entitled his ship to free harbor in every Greek city. The Spartans wanted to know which law established that right.

"The law of gratitude," Barnabas said.

"This is Megaris," one sailor replied.

"Then your laws need work."

Kassandra walked past them toward the boarding boat. "Put the fee on Elpenor's account."

The Spartan sailor looked at Barnabas. "Who is Elpenor?"

"A man about to have a difficult day," Theron said.

Barnabas accepted this as sufficient financial security.

Theron checked the System before they boarded.

No quest completion appeared. No reward marked Nikolaos alive. The System remained very interested in rope and remarkably quiet about families.

Barnabas called down when he saw them. "Did the Wolf fall?"

Kassandra stepped into the boat. "The Wolf is dead."

"Ah," he said. "One of those deaths."

"Take us to Phokis."

"Across the gulf and into the arms of Apollo. The sea is calm, though I may have ruined that by saying so."

The crew lowered the oars. Fort Geraneia and the Spartan camps receded as the Adrestia turned west, then north toward Phokis.

Kassandra stood at the bow with Elpenor's unsigned order tucked against her arm.

Glaux settled beneath the mast while Ikaros followed over open water. Behind them, the grain wagons kept moving toward Megara's villages. At least one useful thing had survived the Wolf's campaign, even if the Wolf himself had become a matter of wording.

Ahead, a merchant waited for a helmet.

They were bringing him questions instead.
