# Chapter 9: Beneath Delphi

One mask was enough to enter the Cult of Kosmos.

It was not enough for two people.

Theron stood in the western preparation room of Apollo's temple and studied the bronze brazier that hid the sanctuary stair. Priests had removed the day's ashes, but one coal still glowed beneath the grate. The room smelled of old smoke, lamp oil, and expensive lies.

Kassandra held Elpenor's mask by its leather straps. "I wear it."

"Yes."

"You dislike that answer."

"I dislike answers that are correct before I finish objecting."

Herodotos crouched beside the brazier and compared its carved base to a charcoal rubbing from his scroll case. The historian had produced six months of temple notes from three different pockets. Theron respected the organization. He worried about the pockets.

"The stair is the only entrance used by the masked visitors," Herodotos said. "There is an older foundation passage here, but the priests sealed it two generations ago."

He pointed to a narrow line on his rubbing. It ran behind the western wall and ended beneath the sacred vault.

Theron touched the Forge Key to the stone.

His concentrated Eagle Vision followed mortar, bronze clamps, and a faint energy route under the floor. The temple had been built over an Isu structure, then repaired by Greeks who had treated every inconvenient gap as somewhere to put more stone.

One route still continued past the sealed passage.

"The old passage has a maintenance channel," Theron said. "Too narrow for Kassandra. Wide enough for me if the builders were feeling charitable."

Kassandra looked at his shoulders. "The drain was charitable too."

"The drain and I remember that differently."

Herodotos glanced between them. "Do your plans often depend upon Theron entering holes?"

"More often than he admits."

The first masked visitor would arrive after moonrise. They had less than an hour.

Theron opened the System shop while Herodotos watched the temple court through the door.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 1,536 dr 2 ob ]`

`Fine charcoal tracing sheets x12 ... 3 dr 4 ob`

`Copper contact thread, 30 cubits .... 8 dr 2 ob`

`Sensory focus tonic, 1 dose ........ 18 dr 0 ob`

`[ Purchase total: 30 dr 0 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 1,506 dr 2 ob ]`

The items appeared inside his field bag. Thirty drachmae was a month's skilled pay for an hour of sharper perception and materials fine enough to catch weak electrical patterns.

The permanent version remained less encouraging.

`Permanent +1 PER ................... 250,000 dr`

Theron closed the shop. His eyes could earn the expensive improvement the slow way.

He took out the copper thread and tracing sheets. To Kassandra, they were more evidence that his bag had been packed by a man expecting to crawl beneath a temple and inspect an ancient machine.

That was close enough to the truth.

"Risks," Kassandra said.

"Your mask is valid, but Elpenor may have been expected to speak to someone. The invitation says no names, which helps. Stay close to the outer wall until you understand their ritual. If they discover you, move toward the Pyramid. I will be behind its western housing."

"And you?"

"The maintenance path may be broken, trapped, or occupied. The Key can open compatible machinery, but it cannot make collapsed stone polite. I drink the tonic only after I reach the lower chamber. If the Pyramid reacts to the Key, I disconnect first and learn second."

"Your escape?"

Theron showed her the remaining smoke pot from the Pythia's house. "Back through the old passage if it stays open. Through your route if it does not."

Kassandra tied Elpenor's invitation inside her bracer. "I enter first. Tap the wall twice when you are in position. Three times if you need me."

"If Deimos appears, do not fight him unless he forces it."

Her hand stopped on the mask. "You know this Deimos?"

"I know the Cult keeps a weapon close, and Praxithea said they fear the Pyramid. Elpenor's invitation called blood a memory. Those facts fit together badly."

It was evidence. It was also an answer shaped around fifteen years of impossible knowledge.

Kassandra accepted it for now. "Then we see what they fear."

Herodotos rolled up his rubbing. "I will remain above and watch the preparation room. If the priests close the stair, I shall reopen it."

"How?" Theron asked.

"I have spent my life asking religious officials questions they dislike. I expect something will occur to me."

It was not a plan Theron would have written down. Herodotos looked pleased with it.

The first visitor crossed the temple court.

Kassandra put on Elpenor's face.

____

The brazier turned a quarter circle when she pressed the bronze token into its base.

Stone moved beneath it. A stair descended into yellow lamplight.

Kassandra looked once at Theron. The mask hid her expression, but he knew the small tilt of her head.

Ready.

She went down alone.

Theron waited until the brazier closed, then moved to the sealed foundation passage. Herodotos helped him lift a marble offering table away from the wall. Behind it, newer blocks covered an arch no wider than a man's shoulders.

The Forge Key found a bronze service plate behind the mortar. Theron pressed two contacts in the sequence recorded beneath the quarry.

Nothing happened.

He tried the sequence in reverse.

A lock released inside the wall.

"History rewards persistence," Herodotos whispered.

"History rewards trying the latch both ways."

Theron removed three loose blocks and slid into the gap. The passage beyond had lost half its ceiling. He crawled over broken stone, turned sideways between two support ribs, and reached a smooth black panel set beneath the Greek foundation.

The Key translated one damaged line.

`[ SERVICE ACCESS: MEMORY ASSEMBLY ]`

That was useful.

The line below it remained unreadable.

That was normal.

Theron connected the Key. The panel opened on a shaft lined with metal grips. Warm air rose from below, carrying voices.

He climbed down.

____

The sanctuary had been designed for machinery and renovated for theatre.

Black stone formed a circular chamber beneath the temple. Greek lamps stood between metal ribs that had survived centuries without rust. Masked figures gathered in groups around a central platform. At its center sat the Pyramid, a dark four-sided artifact tall enough to reach Kassandra's chest.

Theron watched through a service grille behind the western wall.

Kassandra stood among more than twenty Cultists in identical robes. Elpenor's mask had a fixed mouth and heavy brows. It made her look permanently disappointed, which suited the man better than it suited her.

The gathering had rules. Members used titles when they had to address each other. Most avoided even that. Each wore a small branch token at the throat, though the masks hid every face.

Theron counted six designs.

An eye cut by a knife. A coiled serpent. A silver vein beneath a pick. A ship's ram. A bull above broken grain. A helmet surrounded by waves.

Six branches, each reporting through one masked Sage.

The game menu had once displayed them as clean rows of portraits. Here they were merchants, priests, soldiers, mine owners, ship captains, and politicians trying not to recognize each other's voices.

They did not trust one another.

Good. Distrust produced gaps.

Theron fixed the first charcoal sheet against the grille and ran copper thread from the Forge Key to the Pyramid's western housing. He kept the contact loose enough to tear free with one pull.

The Key recorded active routes through the floor. Energy moved from the Pyramid into six buried channels, then branched toward recesses around the chamber. Each recess contained clay tablets, wax records, and sealed chests.

The Cult had organized itself around the machine.

Kassandra moved through the groups while servants poured watered wine. Her broad Eagle Vision could map every body, door, and weapon in the room. Theron could not share her sight, but he knew how she worked. She would build the room in her head before she listened to a single word.

A Cultist with the silver-vein token spoke near her.

"The Laurion output is short again," the man said. "The slaves are dying faster than replacements arrive."

"Then buy more," replied a woman wearing the eye token.

"Athens inspects the roads."

"Athens inspects whatever Kleon tells it to inspect. Remind our friend that silver pays for speeches."

Kassandra turned her cup as if bored. Her thumb rubbed soot from its rim and marked a small line inside her sleeve.

One resource route. One Athenian pressure point. One reference to Kleon without proof of his exact place.

Enough to investigate. Not enough to accuse a public man and survive the attempt.

Across the room, a Cultist with a ship token argued with the helmeted branch.

"The blockade in Megaris was supposed to starve both armies into dependence," the shipmaster said. "The Wolf took the western road, and someone preserved the grain."

"Elpenor chose poor tools."

"Elpenor has not arrived."

Three masks turned toward Kassandra.

She raised her cup. "Elpenor chose a poor courier."

Her voice came out lower than usual and roughened by the mask.

The shipmaster laughed. "At least the merchant learned something before he died."

Kassandra did not react.

Theron wrote the exchange onto a tracing sheet. Elpenor's death was already suspected, but no branch had confirmed it. The Cult had poor internal reporting by design. Their secrecy protected them from enemies and from each other.

That fracture mattered more than a single name.

An older man bearing the bull-and-grain token approached the Pyramid. "The war must continue. Sparta and Athens are spending men faster than they can replace them. The shortages will make every city listen."

The eye-token woman replied, "A city that cannot eat does not listen. It tears down doors."

"Then stand behind the correct door."

Several members approved. Several did not.

The Cult shared a method, not an outcome. One branch wanted wealth, another wanted military crisis, and another wanted an orderly Greece built after both great powers broke themselves.

Theron copied their branch tokens and the locations of the six record recesses. The Key captured the energy address attached to each one.

Names remained behind masks. Routes and habits did not.

Two quiet taps crossed the western housing.

Kassandra had reached the Pyramid.

Theron drank the focus tonic.

It tasted like bitter figs soaked in copper.

`[ Sensory focus: +4 PER ]`

`[ Duration: 59 minutes 59 seconds ]`

Edges sharpened. Fine scratches appeared across the Pyramid's surface, thousands of them, each following a repeated hand position. Dark residue filled the lowest cuts.

Blood.

____

A masked Sage called the gathering to order.

"Kosmos remembers through us," the Sage said. "Blood enters. Purpose returns."

Each member had to place a blooded hand against the Pyramid.

Theron had remembered the ritual, but memory from a game did not include the exact contact points, energy draw, or order of response. Those were the parts that could turn a prophecy stone into a machine for repairing Alexios.

Kassandra drew a pin from Elpenor's mask strap and cut her thumb.

She pressed it to the black surface.

The Pyramid answered.

White lines spread beneath her hand. The buried channels lit one after another, six branches accepting one blood signature. The Forge Key grew hot against Theron's arm.

His tracing sheet filled with fine grey tracks where the copper thread touched it.

Then the feedback returned.

Kassandra went still.

The Pyramid pulled memory through blood and sent selected pieces back through the same path. Theron saw the sequence in the Key: recognition, retrieval, pressure, response. It was not reading a soul. It was stimulating patterns already present and binding them to stored records.

The Cult called that prophecy.

Theron called it the first useful mistake they had made.

The Key opened a capture prompt.

`[ Compatible signal detected ]`

`[ Capture external architecture? ]`

`Estimated storage required: 3 blueprint fragments`

`Estimated comprehension: 0%`

He accepted.

Three empty fragment slots opened. The Key copied routes, timings, and material responses without explaining any of them.

`[ Captured blueprint fragments: 30 ]`

`[ Understood fragments: 4 ]`

That was honest progress. Theron had acquired three new ways to be confused.

Kassandra pulled her hand away.

Inside the mask, her breathing had changed.

Theron tapped twice. He was present. He had the data. She could move.

Before she answered, every lamp in the sanctuary dimmed.

The Cultists turned toward the northern passage.

A man entered without a mask.

He was younger than Kassandra, broad across the shoulders, and dressed in dark armor worked with gold. A heavy sword hung at his hip. The Sword of Damokles looked ceremonial until Theron's Eagle Vision caught the energy moving through its guard.

Alexios walked past the assembled Cult without acknowledging any of them.

Kassandra stared.

Fifteen years had changed the infant she remembered into a stranger. The shape of Myrrine's face remained around his eyes. Nikolaos appeared in the way he carried his weight, though Nikolaos had never fathered him. Leonidas's blood showed most clearly in the fact that every armed person in the chamber made room.

Theron had known this moment was coming.

Knowing did nothing useful to Kassandra's hands. One tightened around the edge of her robe. The other still bled from the Pyramid cut.

The masked Sage bowed. "Deimos."

Alexios looked over the gathering. "One of you failed in Megaris. One failed in Phokis. Now Elpenor hides behind his mask and hopes I will not notice."

His gaze moved across the room.

Kassandra held still.

"The Eagle Bearer lives," the shipmaster said. "Elpenor's plan can still draw her into the war."

"She was already in the war," Alexios replied. "You were too slow to see it."

He put one hand on the Pyramid.

The machine reacted more strongly than it had for any Cultist. White filled every channel at once. The Forge Key's heat crossed into pain.

Theron pulled the copper contact free.

The signal had already returned.

For one sharp instant, the Pyramid joined three sets of memory.

Theron saw a baby's hand reaching toward Myrrine.

He saw stone, sky, and Nikolaos above Taygetos.

He saw a dark room where masked adults taught a child that pain meant weakness leaving the body.

Then he saw Kassandra at twelve, holding an iron knife out to a smaller boy and telling him to stop watching her feet.

That memory belonged to Theron.

The Pyramid had found him through the Key.

Alexios turned toward the western wall.

"There," he said.

Kassandra moved before anyone else understood.

She drove her shoulder into the shipmaster, sent him into two other Cultists, and kicked a lamp across the central platform. Oil spread beneath the Pyramid. Flame followed it.

Theron tore the tracing sheets from the grille and shoved them into his bag.

Alexios drew the Sword of Damokles and struck the western housing.

Black stone split. The service grille crossed the space beside Theron's head and broke against the shaft wall.

Kassandra pulled off Elpenor's mask.

Alexios saw her face.

The chamber stopped being a meeting and became a family disaster with twenty armed witnesses.

"Alexios," Kassandra said.

His expression changed for less than a second. The name reached somewhere the Cult had tried to bury.

Then anger covered it.

"My name is Deimos."

Three Cult guards rushed Kassandra. She caught the first by his robe and threw him across the platform. Her Spear struck the second man's sword hand. The third stopped when she put the broken point beneath his chin.

"You survived Taygetos," she said.

Alexios stepped through the burning oil. "You threw me from that mountain. Do not speak to me of survival."

"I tried to save you."

"The Cult saved me."

The words sounded practiced. Theron had seen the room where they were taught.

The Pyramid's feedback had carried memories in both directions. Stored pain could be triggered. It could also be reached.

Not tonight. Tonight they needed to leave with the first lead intact.

Theron struck the service plate with the Forge Key. The western maintenance door opened behind the cracked housing.

"Kassandra. West wall. Now."

Alexios looked toward his voice. "Another one."

Kassandra backed toward the opening. "He is with me."

"Then he can die with you."

Alexios crossed the platform fast enough to clear the overturned lamp in one step. Kassandra met his sword with the Spear shaft.

The impact put her against the Pyramid.

She did not try to overpower him. She turned with the blow, cut the strap on his right pauldron, and drove her heel into his knee. It bought one pace.

Theron threw the smoke pot.

It broke between them. Grey smoke covered the western side of the sanctuary.

Kassandra's broad Eagle Vision kept every body marked inside it. She caught Theron's wrist and pulled him through the maintenance door as Alexios's sword cut the smoke behind them.

Theron pressed the service plate again.

The door began to close.

Alexios caught its edge with one hand.

Metal stopped.

Kassandra struck his fingers with the flat of the Spear. He released the door, and several tons of old Isu construction sealed him inside the sanctuary.

For perhaps five seconds.

"Climb," Theron said.

They climbed.

____

The service shaft shook beneath the first strike from below.

Theron went up first because Kassandra could hold the lower grips with one hand and punch anyone who reached them. The charcoal sheets dug into his ribs inside the field bag. The Forge Key showed its captured architecture intact.

Another strike bent the maintenance door.

"He is strong," Kassandra said.

"That is the polite version."

"I am considering several impolite ones."

They reached the collapsed passage. Theron squeezed between the support ribs and heard metal tear below.

Three Cult guards entered the shaft.

Kassandra pulled herself into the passage, turned, and kicked loose the stone Theron had marked on the way in. A section of Greek repair work fell across the opening. The guards survived, but removing the blocks would cost them time.

The old path ended at the temple wall.

Herodotos had already removed the loose stones. "You are early."

"Close it," Kassandra said.

Herodotos saw her face and stopped asking questions.

They replaced the blocks and pushed the offering table back into place. Voices rose from the preparation room as masked Cultists came up the main stair.

Herodotos led them through a linen store, across the rear colonnade, and into a group of pilgrims leaving after the final hymn. Kassandra covered the Spear with a folded temple cloth. Theron turned the Forge Key inward beneath his sleeve.

Behind them, a priest shouted that someone had spilled oil below the sacred vault.

Herodotos looked offended. "They keep oil beside an ancient artifact?"

"It was a poor decision before we arrived," Theron said.

"History should note that."

They left Apollo's court at walking speed.

No one remembered a normal departure, Barnabas had said.

Theron hoped the Cult was too busy remembering the fire.

____

They stopped before dawn in an abandoned shepherd's shelter above Kirrha.

Herodotos lit one lamp and spread the tracing sheets across a flat stone. Six branch symbols surrounded a rough plan of the sanctuary. Copper marks showed energy addresses for each record recess and the Pyramid's contact sequence.

Kassandra sat by the entrance with Elpenor's mask beside her.

She had not spoken since Delphi.

Theron gave Herodotos the public portion first. "Six branches. Each has a Sage. Silver from Laurion reaches an Athenian political contact. Naval members manage blockades and shipping. The Peloponnesian branch interfered in Megaris. Another branch controls grain and land pressure."

Herodotos studied the symbols. "Names?"

"References. Kleon may be connected to the silver route. That is not proof that he wears a mask. Elpenor's death exposed poor communication between branches. They suspect one another and disagree about the purpose of the war."

"A secret society divided by ambition," Herodotos said. "How reassuringly ordinary."

"Ordinary people built it. The Pyramid makes them look more certain than they are."

Kassandra finally spoke. "It showed me him."

Herodotos looked toward her. "Deimos?"

"Alexios."

She said the name with care, testing whether it still belonged to the man below the temple.

Theron sat across from her. "Yes."

"You knew."

It was not a question.

"I knew there was a real chance he survived. I knew the Cult had taken a child from Taygetos and raised Deimos. Tonight proved they were the same person."

"You told me Elpenor and the Pythia had pieces of my mother's road. You said nothing about my brother waiting beneath Delphi."

"Because I could not prove he would be there. If I had told you Deimos might be Alexios before we entered, you would still have gone down. You would have gone down carrying a hope the Cult could use."

Kassandra looked at him over the lamp. "You decided what I could carry."

"Yes."

The answer deserved no defense.

Herodotos became interested in arranging the tracing sheets by size.

Kassandra picked up Elpenor's mask. One edge had cracked during the escape.

"The rule is that you tell me the risks in your plans," she said. "Alexios was a risk."

"He was. I should have named the possibility before you put on the mask."

"Yes."

Theron waited.

Kassandra set the mask down. "Do it again and I will throw you from something lower than Taygetos. Low enough that you survive to hear me complain."

"That is a carefully measured threat."

"I learned from the man who prices rope before family arguments."

The anger remained. So did the partnership. Both were allowed to exist.

Theron turned one tracing sheet toward her. "The Pyramid connected to memories already inside Alexios. I saw Taygetos. I saw Myrrine. I saw a room where the Cult hurt him and taught him what to call it."

Kassandra's attention fixed on the copper lines.

"Can you undo it?"

"I can say the machine has a repeatable process. It recognizes blood, retrieves memory, applies pressure, and returns a response. The Key captured three fragments of its architecture. I understand none of them yet."

He opened the status screen where only he could see it.

`[ STATUS | Theron, Age 24 ]`

`STR 17    AGI 19    END 18`

`INT 20    PER 24    CHA 14`

`WIL 18    MEM 16`

`Forge Key integrity: 46.0%`

`Captured blueprint fragments: 30`

`Understood fragments: 4`

`Balance: 1,506 dr 2 ob`

The focus tonic had expired during the walk from Delphi. His perception was back to its trained limit. The new fragments remained expensive questions.

"I cannot cure him now," Theron said. "I can begin learning how."

Kassandra put both forearms on her knees. "He believes I threw him from the mountain."

"The Cult built his life around that belief."

"Then we break it."

There was no speech about saving everyone. No promise that the work would be easy. Alexios had nearly torn an Isu door from its housing with one hand.

Theron pointed to the southern energy route on the sanctuary plan. "Herodotos said the ancient forge on Andros responds to the Spear. The Pyramid and Spear used related access patterns. The Forge Key recorded both. Andros is where we learn what those patterns mean."

Herodotos nodded. "The island lies across the Aegean. Its oldest accounts describe a door opened by Leonidas's bloodline."

Kassandra stood and tied the cracked mask to her belt. "Then we return to the Adrestia."

"You should sleep," Theron said.

"You should learn when advice is wasted."

"I am collecting evidence."

She stepped into the first grey light outside the shelter.

Below them, Kirrha's harbor began its morning work. The Adrestia waited at the quay, and Barnabas was already arguing with someone beside the gangplank. From this distance, the dispute looked energetic and probably concerned money.

Behind them, the Cult of Kosmos still had its masks, its branches, and most of its names.

Theron had its shape now, and the Pyramid had shown him that Alexios was still inside Deimos. On Andros, he could begin working out how to reach him.
