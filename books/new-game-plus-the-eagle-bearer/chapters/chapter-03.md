# Chapter 3: The Owl Beneath the Hill

The wall had waited longer than Theron had been alive twice over. It could wait three more days while he bought a hammer.

He sat behind the barred storeroom door with his purse open beside him. Five drachmae and one obol rested in a clay bowl. The knife lay across his knees, the rope was coiled by the grain sacks, and the remains of the bandage roll fit inside his tunic.

The shop listed what he still needed.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 5 dr 1 ob ]`

`Bronze chisel ............ 1 dr 1 ob`

`Hand hammer .............. 1 dr 3 ob`

`Oil lamp, filled ......... 5 ob`

He could have borrowed tools from Markos. Markos owned a hammer with a split handle, two chisels softened by bad sharpening, and an oil lamp that leaked. Ancient machinery deserved better than the household collection of objects waiting to injure someone.

Theron selected all three.

`[ Purchase total: 3 dr 3 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 1 dr 4 ob ]`

Twenty-one obols vanished from the bowl. The tools appeared on the sack in front of him, plain and clean. He wrapped the hammer and chisel in cloth, hung the lamp from his belt, and opened the door.

Kassandra was in the yard with a long iron bar balanced across one shoulder.

Theron looked at it. "Where did you get that?"

"The dockmaster."

"He lent it to you?"

"He will have lent it to me when I return it."

That was close enough to borrowing by Kephallonian standards.

She set the bar against the wall. "Plan. All of it this time."

Theron spread a scrap of old sailcloth on the ground and drew the quarry with charcoal. The cart path. The western goat track. The drainage channel and the dark course of fitted blocks beneath the limestone.

"We clear the channel first," he said. "You work the loose stone. I check the joints. If we find a door, we open it from the side, not in front. Rope around my waist if I go into any gap you cannot fit through."

"Risks."

"Loose rock. Bad air. A floor over an empty space. A door that closes after it opens. Old defenses if the builders were Isu. Snakes."

"You put snakes last."

"I dislike them. That does not make them the largest problem."

Kassandra tapped the charcoal mark for the buried wall. "What do you do if something starts moving?"

"Tell you what I see. Then we leave by the route we agreed on."

"And if you think staying will get us something useful?"

"I tell you that too."

She studied him for a moment. The silence was deliberate rather than doubtful.

"Good," she said. "Now my risks. I move the stone. I stand nearest the opening because I am stronger. If something needs holding, I hold it only while you tell me how long. If I say run, you run."

"Agreed."

"Say the part you don't like."

Theron looked at the iron bar. "You do not put yourself under several tons of ancient masonry because you think you can win an argument with it."

"Fine."

They rolled up the sailcloth. It wasn't a perfect plan. Perfect plans required knowing what was behind the wall, and that was the current difficulty. It was at least a plan both of them had heard.

____

The old limestone cut was empty when they arrived. The abandoned cart was gone, the cold fire had been scattered, and someone had taken the thieves' dropped knives. Kephallonia wasted little.

They spent the first hour clearing the drainage channel. Kassandra lifted blocks that Theron had expected them to roll together. Theron used the chisel on packed seams and stopped twice to check whether the wall above had moved.

The dark course extended beneath six paces of the quarry face. Each block was the same height. Thin vertical lines divided them, but there was no mortar and no room for the knife point between them.

Near the western end, the chisel struck metal.

Theron cleared the dirt with his fingers. A plate the size of his palm sat flush with the dark stone. Its surface had been blackened by age, except for five narrow grooves arranged around a shallow handprint.

Kassandra crouched beside him. "A lock?"

"Probably."

"Can you open it?"

"Ask me after I know how it works."

He pressed the chisel gently into the first groove. A faint blue line ran beneath the black surface and went out.

The System gave a quiet ping.

`[ Compatible Isu access mechanism detected. ]`

`[ Genetic authorization present: 1%. ]`

One percent was apparently enough for the lock to notice him. It was not enough for the lock to be impressed.

Theron put his palm into the print.

Five points touched his skin. The plate warmed. Somewhere behind the fitted blocks, a mechanism struck once and stopped.

"It tried," he said.

Kassandra put her hand against the wall. "Something moved on the left."

They cleared another arm's length. A narrow vertical seam turned across the top and down the far side, outlining a door. The lowest corner had sunk into the earth.

Theron showed Kassandra where the hidden mechanism had pulled. She drove the iron bar beneath the sunken edge while he held his palm to the plate. The mechanism struck again. Kassandra leaned down on the bar.

Stone scraped over stone.

The door moved inward the width of two fingers, caught, then shifted another handspan as Kassandra changed her grip. Cold air came through the gap.

They waited.

The lamp flame bent toward the opening but remained steady. Air moved inside. That removed one problem from the list.

Kassandra worked the bar again until the opening was wide enough for her shoulders. Theron tied the rope around his waist. She checked the knot herself and tied the other end around the iron bar.

"I go first," she said.

"The access plate answered me."

"And if something inside answers with a spear?"

"Then you going first improves our position."

She gave him a look. "You agreed too quickly."

"I am capable of learning."

Kassandra took the lamp and slipped through.

The passage descended for eight paces and opened into a square chamber. Theron followed, paying out the rope behind him. The walls were dark composite blocks like the outer course, smooth where the quarry stone had been rough. Dust covered the floor. A line of footprints would have been obvious.

There were none.

At the center stood a waist-high frame with a metal bracer held upright between two clamps. Behind it, an oval case rested against the wall. Three bronze bars and a roll of green-black copper mesh lay on a workbench to the right. Most of the remaining shelves had collapsed.

Limited salvage. One artifact. One container. No convenient mound of ancient gold waiting for two children with a cart.

Theron had expected less and hoped for more.

Kassandra swept the lamp across the corners. "No spears."

"Promising."

The outer door dropped.

Kassandra moved before the first impact finished. She shoved the iron bar beneath the descending slab and pulled Theron clear. Stone struck metal at knee height. The bar bent, but held the doorway open by less than a cubit.

Then part of the ceiling lowered with a slow grind.

Not the whole ceiling. Four square blocks descended on metal shafts around the central frame. Dust fell from their edges. Whatever the chamber had once done to intruders, age had changed it into a machine for crushing its own contents.

"The door was a risk," Kassandra said.

"It was."

"How long?"

Theron looked at the bent iron bar. Each scrape of the door forced it lower.

"Thirty breaths before we lose that gap. Less if the bar slips."

"Then move."

He did.

Three channels ran from the access frame to the descending blocks. Two still carried blue light in broken sections. The third was dark. A bank of white ceramic pins stood beside the bracer, several snapped at their bases.

Theron opened his tool wrap.

"I am going to bridge the dark channel," he said. "If it speeds up, pull the bar and get through the door. I will follow through the gap."

"You will be on the wrong side of four falling stones."

"Yes. Assigned risk, disclosed before I do it."

"I hate this rule."

"It has been three days."

Kassandra planted both feet against the floor and took hold of the iron bar. "Do it."

Theron chipped the base from one broken ceramic pin. A metal filament ran through its center. He cut the filament free with his knife, flattened it against the chisel, and fitted it between the two contacts in the dead channel.

Blue light crossed the bridge.

The descending blocks stopped.

The door pressed harder against the bar.

"Different problem," Kassandra said.

"I noticed."

The metal bracer released from its clamps and fell forward. Theron caught it against his chest. Hinged bands opened along its inner surface and closed around his left forearm before he could decide whether wearing unknown machinery was sensible.

The answer was no. The decision had already been made.

Lines appeared across the System interface.

`[ Isu Forge Key identified. ]`

`[ Integrity: 17.4% ]`

`[ Stored charge: 2.1% ]`

`[ Material analysis: unstable ]`

`[ Energy-path tracing: unstable ]`

`[ Isu translation: partial ]`

`[ Blueprint capture: available ]`

`[ Compatible machinery access: available ]`

`[ Repair service: unavailable. ]`

The Key had survived thousands of years to become a damaged tool with a list of maintenance problems. Theron liked it immediately.

Pain pressed behind his eyes.

The chamber changed without changing color. Fine seams stood out in the walls. He saw the ceiling blocks as layers of material, each with a fracture line and a metal shaft behind it. The blue channels resolved into separate routes, one carrying power to the door, another to the security blocks, a third toward the oval case.

His attention narrowed until the chamber outside those routes became difficult to hold in his head.

`[ PER +2 ]`

`[ Isu Sixth Sense awakened. ]`

`[ Expression: concentrated analysis. ]`

"Theron."

Kassandra's voice pulled his focus back. The iron bar had bent another finger's width.

He followed the door's energy route to a cracked switch behind the left wall. The wall showed a square seam he had missed before.

"Panel beside your knee," he said. "Hit the lower right corner."

Kassandra kept one hand on the bar and struck with the heel of the other. The fitted panel jumped inward.

"Again."

The second blow broke the corroded catch. Theron reached into the opening and found three levers. The center lever was connected to the door by a straight metal linkage.

"Pull the middle one toward you."

Kassandra pulled.

The slab rose. She kicked the bent bar free and stepped away as the doorway opened to its original height.

Neither of them moved for two breaths.

"That," Kassandra said, "was thirty breaths?"

"I estimated badly."

"You counted twenty-three."

"Then I estimated well and disliked the answer."

The oval case clicked behind them.

Theron turned. Its front had split along a curved seam. Inside lay a small owl with mottled brown feathers, her wings folded tight and her eyes closed. A metal collar no wider than cord circled her neck. Thin leads ran from the collar into the case.

The System identified her.

`[ Glaux | Isu reconnaissance owl ]`

`[ Condition: critical ]`

`[ Stasis charge: 0.3% ]`

`[ Neural link: offline ]`

`[ Automated restoration: unavailable. ]`

Kassandra leaned close without touching the bird. "Alive?"

"Barely. The case has power for minutes. Maybe less."

The energy route into the case broke at two places. One was the bridge Theron had already made. The other ran through a cracked copper strip beneath the owl's collar lead.

"I can repair the path," he said. "Risk is that the Key has almost no charge and I do not know what the collar will do when it wakes."

Kassandra looked at the owl. "Will leaving it kill her?"

"Yes."

"Repair it."

Theron removed a clean strand from the copper mesh and folded it three times. The Forge Key marked the break with a line his eyes could follow. It did not tell him how to fix it. He fitted the strand beneath the cracked strip, pressed it flat with the chisel, and used a ceramic pin to hold it in place.

The Key released most of its remaining charge.

The collar flashed once. Glaux's body jerked. Her claws closed around the cloth beneath her, and one yellow eye opened.

She stared at Theron.

Then she bit his thumb.

He pulled his hand back. "That seems healthy."

Kassandra laughed once and offered the owl the leather guard from her forearm. Glaux stepped onto it, unsteady but upright.

"She likes me," Kassandra said.

"You have not been bitten yet."

"A strong beginning."

Theron checked the chamber through the new sense. Every object demanded close attention before it gave him detail. He could follow the path from the Forge Key to the broken channels, isolate stress in the ceiling, and see the difference between copper alloys on the bench. The range ended a few paces past the doorway.

Kassandra noticed him staring. "You are doing it again."

"This time I can see where the stone will break."

Her expression lost its humor. "Show me."

He tried to explain the first sensation and failed. It wasn't sight alone. His mind took ordinary details and arranged them by use, danger, and connection. The Key had forced the sense open, but the sense belonged to his Isu blood.

"Stop naming what you see," he said. "Hold the whole room at once. Then choose one thing that matters and let the rest fall back."

Kassandra looked around. "That sounds like looking."

"It is looking with extra steps. Close your eyes first. Listen to where I move."

He stepped left. She pointed at him. He moved behind her. She turned before his sandal touched the floor.

"Again," he said. "Do not follow me. Follow every place I could come from."

Kassandra went still.

When she opened her eyes, she looked through the doorway, then up at the quarry above them.

"There are two goats on the western path," she said. "A man on the cart road. He is walking away. Three birds above the hill."

Theron could see none of those things from inside the chamber.

She turned toward him. "And the block over your head is loose. Move."

He moved. A fist-sized piece of old composite dropped where he had been standing.

The System confirmed what they already knew.

`[ Isu Sixth Sense awakened: Kassandra. ]`

`[ Expression: broad combat awareness. ]`

Kassandra rubbed one temple. "Too much."

"Mine is narrow. Yours seems to cover the quarry."

"Useful in a fight."

"Useful before one too."

She gave him a short nod. Different tools. Same work.

They took the bronze bars, copper mesh, two intact ceramic pins, and three plates the Forge Key marked as blueprint fragments. The Key captured line diagrams from the plates. It supplied no explanation for them.

Glaux rode out on Kassandra's arm. Once they cleared the passage, she climbed to Kassandra's shoulder and stayed there with the stubborn balance of a bird refusing to admit weakness.

Theron used the inner lever to close the door. They packed limestone chips over the access plate and dragged brush across the drainage channel.

The cache had given them a damaged bracer, a sick owl, scrap metal, and diagrams Theron could not read. It was the best haul of his life.

____

Markos accepted the story because most of it was true.

They had found an old workshop beneath the quarry. The chamber was unstable. They had taken a few metal fittings before closing it again. The owl had nested inside and become trapped.

He looked at Glaux, who had moved from Kassandra's shoulder to the yard wall and was tearing apart a mouse with professional focus.

"A trapped owl," Markos said.

"Very trapped," Theron replied.

"And the thing on your arm?"

Theron had wrapped the Forge Key in linen until it resembled a heavy forearm brace. "Part of the old workshop. It holds small tools."

Markos brightened. "An artisan's bracer. Valuable. I know a man in Sami who buys antiques."

"It is broken," Kassandra said.

"Then I know a man who buys broken antiques for slightly less."

"We are keeping it."

Markos looked from her to Theron and recognized a closed negotiation. "Fine. The metal, however, should be sold by someone with experience."

They sold one bronze bar and a damaged plate to a smith for two drachmae and four obols. Kassandra split the money evenly before Markos could explain brokerage.

`[ Sale share recorded: +1 dr 2 ob ]`

`[ Balance: 3 dr 0 ob ]`

Three drachmae was not wealth. It covered food, lamp oil, and replacement tools. The remaining salvage was more useful on Theron's bench than in his purse.

That evening, he opened the shop and checked whether possession of the Key had changed anything.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 3 dr 0 ob ]`

`Isu Blueprint ........... LOCKED`

`Requirement: translate one complete Isu blueprint or barter compatible artifact data.`

Progress had acquired a condition. That was better than a blank lock and much worse than a free answer.

Theron closed the shop and began cleaning three thousand years of corrosion from the Forge Key.

____

Glaux recovered over the next month. She ate anything small enough to carry, slept above Theron's workbench, and bit him whenever he touched the collar without warning. The collar remained fused shut, so he learned to announce maintenance to an owl.

The Forge Key improved more slowly.

Its material analysis could identify differences in density, stress, and composition within arm's reach. Its translation function recognized Isu characters but often returned three possible meanings and no grammar. Blueprint capture preserved diagrams exactly while leaving Theron to determine whether a line represented power, material, or an instruction to avoid touching something.

It was a tool built for an engineer trained by the Isu. Theron was a former office worker with perfect memory, a modern education, and a severe shortage of reference books.

He started with hinges.

Kassandra tested every hinge he made by forcing it until it broke. This was useful data delivered with unnecessary enthusiasm.

Two years passed.

Phoibe arrived at Markos's gate thin, dirty, and prepared to steal breakfast if hospitality failed. Markos saw a child who needed feeding and immediately described taking her in as an investment in future errands.

"What is the return on a person?" Phoibe asked around a mouthful of bread.

"Loyalty," Markos said.

"Can you spend it?"

"Sometimes."

Kassandra laughed. Theron moved the cheese closer to Phoibe before Markos could give her a lecture on commerce.

The household gained another bedroll, another opinion, and a rule that Glaux was not allowed to leave dead mice where anyone slept.

Phoibe broke the rule twice. Glaux broke it more often.

Three more years passed.

Theron learned to share Glaux's sight for several breaths at a time. The first attempt put him on the floor with a bruised shoulder because an owl's idea of level flight did not agree with a human inner ear. By the tenth attempt he could watch a goat path from above while sitting at his bench. By the hundredth, Glaux could scout a dark ruin and show him the route back.

Kassandra's Eagle Vision grew with use. She could walk into a crowded harbor and identify every armed person before Theron finished counting exits. Theron could inspect one of those weapons and see the crack below its hilt. Her awareness made the map. His focus found where to cut it.

They worked.

Markos found debts, lost cargo, angry partners, and opportunities with suspicious timing. Kassandra became the person Sami sent when a problem required force. Theron became the person sent when the problem had locks, ledgers, walls, or people clever enough to avoid standing in front of Kassandra.

They returned with results often enough that the names began traveling together.

Kassandra stopped using the pine staff. She unwrapped the broken Spear of Leonidas she had carried from Sparta and trained until the old weapon moved as naturally as her hand. The spear answered her blood in brief flashes during hard fights. Theron recorded each response with the Forge Key and understood almost none of it.

Ikaros came from the mountains one summer morning and refused to leave.

The eagle watched Kassandra from a cedar for half a day. At noon she held out her arm. He landed hard enough to make her shift one foot, then accepted a piece of fish as if the entire meeting had been his decision.

Glaux disliked him for a week.

Their argument remained mostly silent and involved ownership of the roof.

Ten more years passed through contracts, training, and repair attempts that worked often enough to justify the ones that smoked.

Theron built compact pulleys, folding grapnels, stronger lamp housings, hidden blade catches without the blades, and a dozen tools whose modern names had no useful Greek translation. Kassandra carried the good ones and broke the bad ones. Phoibe learned every route across Kephallonia and developed a talent for hearing news before the people involved knew it had become news.

The Forge Key reached forty-six percent integrity. Its basic analysis and energy tracing became reliable. Translation remained partial. It held twenty-seven captured blueprint fragments and only four Theron understood well enough to build.

That was progress. Ancient technology did not owe him convenience.

On a clear morning in 431 BCE, Theron stood outside the house above Ktimene Beach and opened his status.

`[ STATUS | Theron, Age 24 ]`

`STR 17    AGI 19    END 18`

`INT 20    PER 24    CHA 14`

`WIL 18    MEM 16`

`Trait: Enhanced Talent, active.`

`Isu blood: 1%.`

`Balance: 318 dr 4 ob.`

The physical numbers had crossed an ordinary adult's years ago. None had arrived for free. Kassandra had made sure of that with dawn runs, staff drills, spear drills, climbing, swimming, and the firm belief that Theron could think after training just as well as before it.

He closed the screen.

Kassandra waited in the yard with the Spear of Leonidas at her hip. At twenty-seven she was taller, stronger, and known across the island as a misthios who finished contracts. Ikaros circled above the beach. Glaux watched from the shade beneath the roof, better suited to ruins and night than the open morning sky.

Kassandra tossed Theron a wooden practice knife.

"You were staring."

"I was counting."

"You always say that."

She attacked before he finished answering.

Theron stepped outside the first cut, caught her wrist with both hands, and used the motion to turn away. Kassandra planted one foot and stopped him. Strength remained her argument and she was very good at making it.

He released her wrist before she could throw him, kicked a wooden wedge beside the water jar, and pulled a cord hidden under the dust.

A loop dropped from the yard wall toward her shoulder.

Kassandra saw it through Eagle Vision and cut the cord without looking. The distraction still gave Theron time to put the practice knife against her ribs.

Her wooden blade rested under his chin.

They stopped.

"Draw," Theron said.

"You are dead."

"You are bleeding badly."

"Then I kill you and find a healer."

"Reasonable."

Kassandra lowered her knife. "The loop was new."

"Third version."

"The second was faster."

"The second caught Markos."

"That was its best feature."

Theron picked up the cut cord. She reset the water jar he had used as an anchor. Neither had won cleanly. That was normal now.

From the ridge above the beach, Phoibe shouted their names.

She came down the path at a run, waving one arm and carrying news urgent enough to skip breakfast.

The quiet part of the morning was over.
