# Chapter 10: The Ancient Forge

Andros appeared after sunrise as a hard line of brown rock above the Aegean.

Barnabas stood at the Adrestia's bow and pointed as if he had personally arranged the island. "There! The place where Hephaistos taught mortals to ruin perfectly good metal by making swords."

Herodotos lowered the clay cup he had been using for breakfast. "No account says Hephaistos ever visited Andros."

"Then the account was written by a man who arrived late."

Theron sat beside the mast with the Forge Key open across his forearm. Three copper contacts had darkened during the escape from Delphi. He cleaned them with vinegar, a strip of wool, and more patience than the job deserved.

Kassandra watched the island from the rail. She had slept for four hours after they returned to the ship. It was four more than Theron expected and probably four fewer than Barnabas had ordered.

Ikaros circled above the northern cliffs. Glaux stayed beneath the sail, where daylight offended her professional standards.

Herodotos unrolled one of his copied accounts on a deck chest. "The oldest reference calls the place a chamber for returning a weapon to its first purpose. Later writers turned that into a shrine. One claims Leonidas visited before Thermopylae."

"Did he?" Kassandra asked.

"The same writer claims Leonidas wrestled a sea serpent on the voyage home."

Barnabas looked pleased. "A reliable man."

Theron finished the last contact and closed the Key's outer plate. "The forge is inside the eastern ridge. The entrance faces a dry river cut and responds to Leonidas's bloodline. There is a second opening above the chamber, used for heat or maintenance."

Herodotos looked at him. "Which account mentioned the second opening?"

"The mountain."

That answer earned him a long stare.

Theron tapped the Forge Key. "The energy route Herodotos traced from old descriptions matches the route beneath Delphi. If the main chamber still works, the exhaust has to go somewhere."

"Ah," Herodotos said. "A conclusion."

"One with a mountain attached."

Kassandra turned from the rail. "And Alexios?"

There was the real reason none of them cared much about breakfast.

Theron put the wool away. "The Cult knows we escaped. Alexios knows the Key interfered with the Pyramid, and he heard us name Andros before the western door closed. He may follow. I expect him to."

Kassandra's face remained still.

Theron continued before silence became another decision made for her. "Ikaros watches the open approaches. Glaux watches the upper vent and the passage behind us. Barnabas holds the Adrestia beyond the northern cape once we land. A skiff waits in the eastern cove. I will mark two routes from the forge before we activate anything."

"If he comes inside?"

"The forge may answer his blood and the Sword of Damokles. We leave the machinery between him and us. If he waits outside, we speak from ground we have already measured."

"And if I can reach him?"

"You decide how long we stay."

She studied him for a moment. "Every risk this time?"

"Every one I know. The Pyramid showed him one of my memories. The forge could connect the Spear, the Sword, and the Key more strongly. I may receive another memory. He may receive mine. Either could hurt him, you, or me."

"Can it kill him?"

"I do not know. I will break contact before I test that answer."

Kassandra nodded once. "Good."

It was not forgiveness for Delphi. It was the rule working again.

Barnabas called for the sail to come in. The Adrestia turned toward a narrow beach below the eastern ridge.

____

The forge entrance stood behind a fall of loose stone and thorn brush.

Ikaros had found the dry river cut from above. Glaux found the upper vent ten minutes later, a square shaft hidden beneath a shelf of rock. Her shared sight showed Theron a vertical drop, metal braces, and dust disturbed by warm air from below.

The chamber was awake enough to heat the mountain.

Kassandra placed her palm against a smooth panel beside the buried door. Nothing happened.

She drew the Spear.

White lines appeared in the stone.

The lines followed the broken weapon, stopped at the blood on her knuckles, and continued around the door. Stone sections withdrew into the ridge with a series of heavy impacts.

Herodotos stared into the opening. "I have spent twenty years reading claims that the old gods built halls inside mountains."

"And?" Kassandra asked.

"I preferred being correct when the evidence was less likely to crush me."

The main passage descended in a straight line. Theron marked the wall every twenty paces with chalk and checked each side seam through his concentrated Eagle Vision. The construction resolved into layers: black stone facing, pale ceramic insulation, bronze-colored supports, and narrow channels carrying heat deeper into the ridge.

The Key translated scattered labels.

`[ MATERIAL INTAKE ]`

`[ POWER DISTRIBUTION ]`

`[ AUTHORIZED GENETIC LINE: PARTIAL MATCH ]`

The last line appeared when Kassandra passed beneath it.

Theron stopped. "The door read your blood. It recognizes enough of Leonidas's line to open the forge, but the match is partial."

"Because I am his granddaughter?"

"Or because humans and Isu were never using the same measure of family."

Herodotos copied the translated words onto a wax tablet. "The builders restricted tools through ancestry."

"Through genetic traits," Theron said. "Ancestry was how those traits moved."

Kassandra looked back. "Explain it without inventing a new word."

"The forge knows what your blood can do. It does not care what your family calls itself."

"Better."

The passage opened into a chamber built around a circular platform. Six metal arms hung above it. Each ended in a different tool, though only two retained complete working heads. A ring of white light ran beneath the floor and crossed to a waist-high control block.

The floor vibrated under Theron's sandals. Heat came from vents around the platform in measured bursts.

This was not the damaged cache beneath Kephallonia. The forge had lost pieces, but its power routes still completed their circuits.

The System recognized the difference.

`[ ACTIVE ISU FABRICATION SITE DETECTED ]`

`[ Site access: inherited authorization required ]`

`[ User access: 1% compatibility, service functions only ]`

`[ Compatible damaged tool detected: Isu Forge Key ]`

Kassandra stepped onto the platform with the Spear in hand.

The machine arms lowered.

One passed a line of white light over her from head to foot. Another stopped above the Spear. The broken blade lifted from her palm and remained suspended between them.

Kassandra reached for it.

"Wait," Theron said. "It is holding the metal. It has not begun cutting."

"You say that as if the distinction improves matters."

"It improves them slightly."

Theron connected the Forge Key to the control block. The fit was imperfect. Two of the Key's contacts aligned, three failed to reach, and one touched a port that had melted centuries ago.

The Key began recording anyway.

Energy entered through the floor ring, split beneath the platform, and rose into the arms. One route read Kassandra's blood. A second examined the Spear's internal channels. A third compared both against a stored pattern.

The forge was not adding power from nothing. It was repairing access to power already trapped inside the broken artifact.

Theron's Key filled six fragment slots.

`[ Machinery architecture captured: 4 fragments ]`

`[ Genetic access architecture captured: 2 fragments ]`

`[ Captured blueprint fragments: 36 ]`

`[ Understood fragments: 4 ]`

A new shop entry appeared beneath the capture notice.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 1,506 dr 2 ob ]`

`Forge interface adapter, one use .... 1,200 dr 0 ob`

`Requires: active fabrication-site scan ... MET`

`Function: bridge one damaged service circuit and open one interpretation slot`

`[ Remaining balance after purchase: 306 dr 2 ob ]`

Twelve hundred drachmae could buy land, armor, or enough grain to make Markos call himself a civic benefactor until someone checked the bill.

It could also turn one pile of copied Isu routes into something Theron understood.

He bought it.

`[ Purchase confirmed: -1,200 dr ]`

`[ Balance: 306 dr 2 ob ]`

A flat packet appeared inside his field bag. It held a mesh of silver contacts, six ceramic pins, and a strip of material thin enough to show the lines of his fingers through it.

Theron opened the Key's outer plate and set the mesh across the burned service circuit. The first ceramic pin locked it against an intact contact. The second joined the forge port. The third broke when the machine sent power through it.

He replaced it and changed the angle.

"How expensive was that?" Kassandra asked from the platform.

"Painfully."

"A number, Theron."

"Twelve hundred drachmae."

Herodotos lowered his tablet. "You carried twelve hundred drachmae in that bag?"

"Not anymore."

Kassandra gave Theron a flat look. "If this burns your arm, I am charging you for the bandage."

"That seems fair."

The replacement pin held.

The Key's dead circuit received power for the first time since Theron had found it. Its inner plate warmed against his skin. White lines passed through the new mesh and connected to the forge control.

The machine supplied a complete route. The Key compared it to thirty-six incomplete records, discarded five poor matches, and found one pattern close enough to translate.

`[ Forge Key integrity: 46.0% -> 51.6% ]`

`[ Interpretation slot opened: 1 ]`

`[ Power-routing fragment understood ]`

`[ Captured blueprint fragments: 36 ]`

`[ Understood fragments: 5 ]`

One of the machine arms moved.

Its tool head opened above the Spear and directed a narrow beam into the broken shaft. Kassandra's blood lit the access route. The forge adjusted the weapon around her response, restoring links between the blade and the damaged core.

The process lasted less than a minute.

The Spear dropped into Kassandra's hand.

She caught it and turned toward the empty side of the chamber. Her next movement was faster than the eye expected. The Spear crossed her body, reversed, and stopped against a metal post with the point a finger's width from contact.

White light covered the blade. A shallow cut appeared in the post anyway.

Kassandra looked at it. "Useful."

Herodotos stepped closer to the mark. "That is your complete judgment?"

"It cuts better."

Theron disconnected the Key before the forge could offer a second expensive idea. The new circuit remained dark without external power, but its repaired path held.

He had spent four fifths of his available money to understand one route.

It was a terrible financial decision.

He was already thinking about the next one.

____

They ate bread, olives, and hard cheese outside the forge.

Herodotos filled two wax tablets before he remembered the food. Kassandra tested the Spear's balance against a dead tree. Theron checked the eastern path and found every chalk mark untouched.

Glaux returned from the upper vent and settled on a rock beside him.

Ikaros called from above.

Kassandra stopped moving.

The eagle circled once over the southern ridge, then broke east toward the beach.

One person approached from the landing place.

Theron closed the Key's plate. "Alexios."

Herodotos gathered his tablets. "You expected him."

"Yes. Take the upper path. Glaux will lead you to the skiff if the main route closes."

"And you?"

"We use the main route until we choose otherwise."

Herodotos looked at Kassandra. "History records many meetings between divided families. It usually improves the account by omitting the weapons."

Kassandra put the Spear across her back. "History was not raised in mine. Go."

Herodotos went up the marked trail with Glaux flying between the rocks ahead of him.

Theron and Kassandra walked down to the dry river cut.

Alexios waited where the path narrowed between two stone faces. He had come alone. Dark armor covered his shoulders and chest, repaired where Kassandra had cut the strap beneath Delphi. The Sword of Damokles hung in his right hand.

"You led me to the forge," he said.

"You followed us," Kassandra replied.

"The Cult searched for this place for years. You opened it in a morning."

Theron watched the Sword through his concentrated sight. Energy gathered inside the guard and followed three channels into the blade. The forge had taught the Key enough to resolve their order.

One route amplified force. One protected the user from the return. The third reached toward nearby compatible artifacts.

The Sword was already touching the Spear's signal.

"Risks changed," Theron said quietly. "The Sword is linking to the Spear. Keep the point away from his chest."

Kassandra heard him. Alexios did too.

"You study me like one of your machines," Alexios said.

"Machines are easier."

Alexios looked at the Forge Key. "You hid behind a wall in Delphi."

"The wall was useful."

"Will it save you now?"

Kassandra stepped between them. "You came for me. Speak to me."

His attention moved to her. "You wear our grandfather's weapon and claim our mother's name. That does not make you family."

"Myrrine searched for you. The Cult kept her away."

"The Cult raised me."

"They hurt you until you called it kindness."

Alexios attacked.

Kassandra caught the Sword on the Spear. White light crossed both weapons, and the impact drove her sandals through loose gravel. She turned the blade aside and struck his armored ribs with the Spear shaft.

He barely moved.

Theron pulled the Key's new service contact open. The understood power route showed him the Sword's next build half a second before Alexios used it.

"Ground strike," he said.

Kassandra jumped back.

The Sword hit stone. Force crossed the river cut and broke the surface into sharp pieces. Theron moved behind the left rock face before the wave reached him. Stone fragments passed through the space where he had stood.

Kassandra landed close to Alexios and used the moment after the discharge. The Spear point cut his thigh below the armor. She kept the wound shallow.

Alexios looked down at the blood.

"You hold back," he said.

"You are my brother."

He drove forward harder.

Kassandra gave ground by choice, turning each strike and refusing the openings that would put the Spear through his throat or eye. Alexios's strength made every block expensive. The Sword made every mistake final.

She could fight him. She could not safely beat sense into him.

Theron connected the Key to the metal strip he had fixed beneath the rock face before entering the forge. Copper thread ran from it across the dry riverbed and ended beneath Kassandra's marked retreat point.

The Sword struck the Spear there.

The contact route opened.

The Key caught a fragment of Alexios's response and returned the simplest compatible memory available through Kassandra's weapon.

A small Kassandra stood beside Myrrine on a Spartan terrace. Myrrine held an infant against her shoulder and sang while Kassandra made faces at him.

The image lasted one heartbeat.

Alexios stopped.

"Mother," he said.

Kassandra lowered the Spear a fraction. "Yes."

The Sword's third channel forced itself wider.

Pain hit Theron behind the eyes. The Key filled with broken pieces from the Cult's training room: a child's wrist tied to a chair, a masked teacher, the same lie repeated after every scream.

The return signal was feeding pressure with memory, exactly as the Pyramid had.

Theron tore the copper thread free.

Alexios shouted and swept the Sword across the river cut. The strike missed Kassandra and split the marked rock face.

That was their limit.

"East route," Theron said.

Kassandra struck the Sword aside, kicked Alexios back one pace, and moved.

Theron pulled the release pin beside the path. The cracked rock face settled across the river cut in a controlled fall. It would not hold Alexios long. It only had to block his next clean strike.

They ran up the eastern trail.

Behind them, the Sword broke the first stone.

The trail ended above the cove. Herodotos already had the skiff in the water. Glaux flew back to Theron while Ikaros crossed the ridge above Kassandra.

They pushed off as Alexios reached the cliff.

He stood above the broken route with the Sword at his side. Kassandra remained at the stern, watching him until distance hid his face.

No one called it victory.

It was information bought with blood, stone, and twelve hundred drachmae.

For Theron, that counted as an expensive beginning.

____

Barnabas brought the Adrestia around the northern cape after the skiff cleared the cove.

Once they were aboard, Kassandra sat beside the mast and cleaned the cut on her forearm. It was shallow. Alexios had still come close enough to draw blood.

Theron set the Forge Key beside her. Its status remained unchanged after the brief contact.

`[ STATUS | Theron, Age 24 ]`

`STR 17    AGI 19    END 18`

`INT 20    PER 24    CHA 14`

`WIL 18    MEM 16`

`Forge Key integrity: 51.6%`

`Captured blueprint fragments: 36`

`Understood fragments: 5`

`Balance: 306 dr 2 ob`

Kassandra tied off the bandage. "I could have hurt him badly."

"Yes."

"I could not make him stop without doing it."

"No."

She looked toward Andros. "But he remembered her."

Theron turned the copied forge route toward the Pyramid tracing. The structures were different, but both used blood to identify a mind and both returned stored patterns through an artifact connection.

"The memory reached him," he said. "The Cult's conditioning reached back through the same path. I can build protection against that now. I cannot remove it yet."

"Then we learn more."

Herodotos sat across from them with three tablets on his knees. "Athens holds records from every city in the alliance, physicians from across Greece, and politicians who believe secrets become respectable when filed properly."

Barnabas leaned over from the steering deck. "Athens also has excellent wine. I mention this for the completeness of the plan."

Theron looked east, where the route to Attika lay beyond the open water.

The Cult had money in Laurion, influence around Kleon, and enough interest in Kassandra to send Alexios after them. Athens offered names, records, and a city full of arguments that could hide several useful questions.

"Set course for Athens," Kassandra said.

Barnabas raised one hand to the crew. "Athens! City of wisdom, terrible parking, and wine good enough to forgive both."

The sail filled.

Andros fell behind them. Theron kept the forge map open across his knees and started marking every place where the Pyramid's memory route matched the Sword's response.

For the first time, five understood fragments felt like the beginning of a machine instead of a better-organized collection of problems.
