# Chapter 2: The Better Tool

The iron knife arrived without ceremony.

Theron sat alone in Markos's storeroom with the door barred, his six drachmae and five obols counted into a clay bowl beside him. The room held sacks of grain, two cracked amphorae, and enough dust to prove that Markos considered sweeping a seasonal task.

He opened the shop.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 6 dr 5 ob ]`

`Iron knife .............. 4 dr 0 ob`

`Bandages, roll .......... 5 ob`

`Rope, 10 cubits ......... 3 ob`

He selected the knife.

`[ Purchase: Iron knife ]`

`[ Cost: 4 dr 0 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 2 dr 5 ob ]`

Four silver coins vanished from the bowl. The knife appeared on top of a grain sack in a plain leather sheath, as if someone had placed it there while he looked away.

Theron picked it up and checked the room for witnesses despite the barred door. Some habits deserved to survive even when they looked ridiculous.

The blade was a little shorter than his forearm. Iron, straight-backed, sharp on one edge, with a wooden grip that fit his hand. The System description contained one word: knife. Four drachmae bought him a tool that could cut rope, clean fish, open sacks, and discourage someone from grabbing him.

The last purpose required knowing how to use it.

He belted the sheath beneath his tunic, poured the remaining coins into his purse, and opened the door.

Kassandra was waiting outside.

She had wrapped the grip of her pine staff with a strip of dark leather. The work was tight and even. She looked at Theron's belt, then at his face.

"You bought it."

"That was the plan."

"Show me."

Theron drew the knife and offered it hilt first. Kassandra turned it over, checked the edge with her thumb, and tested the balance across one finger.

"Good blade," she said. "Grip is dry. It'll rub your hand raw."

"Good morning to you too."

She handed it back. "Hold it."

He did.

"Not like you're cutting bread."

She moved his thumb down and turned his wrist until the point lined up with his forearm. Then she stepped away and raised the staff.

Theron looked at the staff. "Are we training or settling an argument I don't remember having?"

"Training. If it becomes an argument, you'll know."

Kassandra tapped the staff against his wrist. The knife dropped into the dirt.

"Again."

He picked it up.

The second tap caught his knuckles. He kept hold of the knife and nearly cut his own tunic when he pulled his hand back.

"Better," Kassandra said.

"Your standards are generous."

"My standards are that you still have all your fingers."

She came at him slowly after that. The staff moved at half speed, which was still enough to make every bad choice obvious. When he watched the end nearest him, the other end struck his ankle. When he backed away in a straight line, she walked him into the wall. When he tried to rush inside the staff's reach, she turned her hands and put the leather-wrapped grip against his chest.

He landed on his back beside the water jar.

Kassandra looked down at him. "You think before you move."

"Usually considered a virtue."

"Not when someone is hitting you."

Theron sat up and rubbed his chest. "Thinking after they hit me seems worse."

"Think before the fight. During it, move."

That was simple advice. It was also the sort of simple advice that took years of bruises to follow.

He got up and tried again.

This time he stopped treating the staff like a puzzle that would wait for him. Kassandra's shoulders moved before her hands. Her front foot turned before a sweep. He still missed most of it, but he saw enough to step around the first strike and catch the shaft with his empty hand.

Kassandra pulled it free and stopped the next thrust an inch from his ribs.

"That was better."

"I lasted almost three breaths."

"Four. I counted."

Markos came through the gate carrying a wax tablet and wearing his opportunity face.

"Excellent," he said. "You're both awake, armed, and already dirty. Saves time."

Kassandra lowered the staff. "What happened."

"Nothing happened. A minor delay. A misunderstanding involving a cart, six jars of good oil, and three men who believe unattended goods are gifts from Hermes."

"Someone stole from you," Theron said.

"Borrowed. Without asking. Or leaving names." Markos held up the tablet. "The cart driver saw them take the north path toward the old limestone cut above Sami. He came to tell me after deciding that fighting three men was outside the price I paid him. A reasonable business judgment, though inconvenient."

Kassandra rested the staff across her shoulders. "How much."

"The oil is worth twenty-four drachmae. Bring back all six jars and I will pay three drachmae each."

"Four," she said.

"Three."

"The cart driver decided three men weren't worth fighting."

"The cart driver is not you."

"Four."

Markos put a hand to his chest. "You would profit from my misfortune?"

"Yes."

He looked at Theron for support.

Theron said, "Twenty-four drachmae of stock recovered for eight is still better than losing twenty-four."

Markos pointed at him. "This is what happens when children learn accounts. Three drachmae and four obols each. Final offer."

Kassandra looked at Theron.

He ran the figures. The difference between three drachmae four obols and four drachmae was two obols. Markos would also feed them whether they succeeded or not, a detail that belonged in every honest calculation.

"Done," Theron said.

"Done," Kassandra agreed.

Markos brightened immediately. "Good. I knew I could trust you. Try not to break the jars."

"Anything else?" she asked.

"Try not to break yourselves. Jars first, of course."

Kassandra made a quiet sound through her nose.

Markos left before either of them could revise the price.

---

The old limestone cut sat less than an hour north of Sami, where a low hill had been opened years ago for building stone and abandoned when the clean layer ran out. The cart driver had seen three men take the path. That did not mean the men were still there, or that there were only three, or that they had not posted someone where the path narrowed.

Kassandra wanted to leave immediately.

Theron wanted ten minutes.

"They have six oil jars," he said. "Each one weighs enough that they won't carry them far uphill. The quarry has one cart path and a goat track around the western side. If they're staying there, they'll put one man where he can see the cart path."

"And if they didn't?"

"Then we wasted ten minutes and Markos gets to complain before supper instead of before midday."

He went into the storeroom, barred the door, and opened the shop again.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 2 dr 5 ob ]`

`Rope, 10 cubits ......... 3 ob`

`Bandages, roll .......... 5 ob`

`[ Purchase total: 1 dr 2 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 1 dr 3 ob ]`

The rope and a linen roll appeared beside the grain sacks. Eight obols was more than he wanted to spend. It was also less than the price of finding out they needed either item after someone started bleeding.

He put the bandage roll under his tunic and carried the rope openly. Kassandra looked at it when he came out.

"Markos had rope left?"

"Behind the grain."

There had been rope behind the grain now. The best lies were facts with carefully trimmed edges.

They took the goat track.

The climb was rough enough that Theron used both hands twice. Kassandra moved ahead, slowed when the track narrowed, and never asked whether he could keep up. He appreciated that more than help he did not need.

Near the top, she crouched and touched two fingers to the dirt. A sandal print crossed the track toward the quarry. Fresh edges. A second print lay partly over it.

She pointed uphill.

Theron nodded.

They left the track and moved through knee-high brush until the quarry opened below them. It was a shallow limestone cut, perhaps sixty paces across, with worked shelves on three sides and a dirt ramp descending from the east. The stolen cart stood near the ramp. Four oil jars remained aboard. Two more sat beside a cold fire under a scrap of cloth.

Three men.

One sat on the cart with a short spear across his knees. Another slept near the fire. The third was using a stone to sharpen a knife, though the angle suggested he was doing more damage to the edge than good.

Kassandra watched them for several breaths, then leaned close enough to speak quietly.

"I take the one with the spear. You stay here."

"If you run straight down, the man by the fire reaches the goat track and comes behind you."

"Then I hit him first."

"The spear reaches farther."

"Then what?"

Theron studied the quarry again.

The ramp was the easy exit. The goat track met the western lip behind them. A narrow drainage channel had been cut through the rock along the north wall, deep enough to hide a child if the child had no respect for his tunic. Loose chips covered the floor near it.

He had one rope, one knife, and a partner who could beat any of the three men if they arrived one at a time.

"We make them arrive one at a time," he said.

Kassandra followed his finger as he pointed out the channel and the two stone blocks flanking the ramp.

"That's your plan?"

"Most of it."

"What's the rest?"

"You hit them."

She gave him a short nod. "Good plan."

---

They tied the rope low across the ramp between the two blocks, then dragged dust over it. Theron hated using a new rope as a tripline. Rope did not improve from being stepped on by thieves. The thought was petty enough to keep him calm.

Kassandra went back around to the western side. Theron crawled through the drainage channel until he was below the quarry lip and twenty paces from the cart.

The stone was cool against his palms. The channel had straight sides and a steady slope toward a crack lower in the hill. Tool marks crossed the bottom in regular rows. He filed that away. There were three armed men nearby, which made archaeology a later problem.

He picked up a limestone chip and threw it against the far wall.

The man sharpening his knife looked up.

Theron threw another, lower this time. It struck near the mouth of the channel.

The man stood. "Rat?"

The spear carrier laughed. "Go catch it. We can sell the hide."

"Sell your mother."

He walked toward the channel with the knife held away from his body. Theron backed along the cut, keeping below the edge. At the last turn he stopped, drew his own knife, and waited.

A sandal appeared above him. Then a shin.

Theron sliced the leather strap behind the man's ankle.

The sandal came apart under his next step. The man pitched forward, caught himself on both hands, and shouted something rude about Theron's ancestry.

Theron was already moving.

He came out of the channel behind the cart as Kassandra dropped from the western shelf. She landed beside the man near the cold fire and struck once with the middle of her staff. The blow caught his forearm before he could reach his weapon. Her return swing hit the side of his knee.

He went down swearing.

The spear carrier jumped from the cart and thrust at her. Kassandra turned past the point and knocked the shaft aside, but the first man grabbed for her ankle from the ground.

Theron climbed onto the back of the cart and kicked one of the empty wooden chocks loose. It struck the grabbing hand. The man pulled back.

"Behind you," Theron said.

Kassandra ducked. The damaged knife went over her head.

She drove the end of her staff backward into the attacker's stomach. He folded around it. She pulled the staff free, turned, and struck his wrist. His knife landed in the dust.

The spear carrier had enough sense to back away.

He looked from Kassandra to the man holding his stomach, then to Theron on the cart. His expression changed as the numbers stopped favoring him.

"Children," he said. "Markos sends children."

"Markos keeps the expensive people for difficult work," Theron said.

The man charged him.

That was the point of being visible on the cart.

Theron jumped down the far side. The man followed around the front, spear level, choosing the shortest route out of habit. Kassandra shouted behind him and gave chase.

He reached the ramp first.

His front foot caught the rope.

The spear point struck dirt. The shaft drove back into his ribs as he fell, and Kassandra reached him before he could work out which injury to complain about. She planted one foot on the spear and set the end of her staff against his throat.

"Stay down," she said.

He stayed down.

The fight had taken perhaps twenty seconds. Theron's heart was trying to fit several minutes into them.

The man with the cut sandal came out of the drainage channel holding his knife. He saw the first man on his knees, the second on his stomach, and the spear carrier under Kassandra's staff.

He looked at Theron.

Theron held the iron knife properly this time, point in line with his forearm. His hand still shook. The shaking mattered less than the edge.

"You can leave," Theron said. "Without the oil."

The man considered the offer.

He dropped his knife.

Kassandra took their weapons and sent all three down the ramp. The spear carrier limped over the rope he had missed the first time. None of them looked back.

Theron waited until they cleared the turn below the hill before putting his knife away.

"Your hand is shaking," Kassandra said.

"It has excellent reasons."

She checked his arms, chest, and face with quick looks. "You're not cut."

"Neither are you."

"I wasn't worried about me."

The words were matter-of-fact. She turned away and began checking the oil jars before they could become a discussion.

Theron looked at the three abandoned weapons. Two knives and a short spear. The System did not issue a ping for surviving poor decisions. It probably would have been overworked if it did.

He went to untie the rope.

One of the knots had pulled tight under the man's weight. Theron worked the point of his knife beneath it and eased the fibers loose without cutting them. Four drachmae had purchased a blade. The useful part was learning where to put it.

---

The cart was intact. The mule had been tied in the shade beyond the ramp and looked offended by the entire interruption. Kassandra brought it back while Theron reset the jars and checked each clay seal.

One seal had cracked. Oil had not leaked yet.

He used a strip from the bandage roll to bind cloth over the mouth, then tied it below the handles. Five obols spent on bandages to protect four drachmae of oil was not their intended medical purpose, but cloth did not care.

While Kassandra fitted the mule to the traces, Theron returned to the drainage channel.

"What are you doing?" she asked.

"Looking."

"At a ditch."

"At a ditch someone cut through solid stone and sloped to the same depth for thirty paces."

Kassandra came over. She studied the channel, then the quarry walls.

"Quarrymen cut stone."

"These marks run under the quarry face. The channel was here first."

He cleared loose chips from one wall with his fingers. Beneath them, the stone changed. The quarry was pale limestone. The buried course was darker and fitted in blocks with gaps too fine for his fingernail.

Theron knew what ancient construction looked like in this world. He also knew that not every good wall came from the Isu. Humans had built clever things for thousands of years and deserved the credit.

Still, the straight channel, the fitted blocks, and the direction into the hill gave him a reason to return with better tools.

"There's something under this cut," he said.

Kassandra crouched beside him and pressed her palm to the darker stone. "A wall."

"Probably."

"You sound disappointed."

"Walls usually have something on the other side."

She stood. "Oil first. Buried wall later."

"Agreed."

They led the cart back toward Sami.

The trip downhill was slow because six jars of oil punished confidence. Kassandra walked beside the mule. Theron stayed behind the cart and watched the cracked seal. Neither of them spoke for the first half of the journey.

Near the town, Kassandra said, "You didn't stay where I put you."

"No."

"The plan worked."

"Mostly."

"You cut his sandal."

"It seemed less difficult than cutting him."

She thought about that. "Next time, tell me the part where you're bait."

"Would you have agreed?"

"No."

"Then it would have made the plan harder to discuss."

Kassandra stopped walking.

Theron took two more steps before noticing. He turned.

She pointed the staff at him. "Next time, tell me."

Her voice carried none of Markos's theatrical outrage. She meant it. Theron had built a plan around what Kassandra could do and omitted what it required from him because he had assumed the result justified the choice. That was the kind of habit that became dangerous when people trusted you.

"Next time, I tell you," he said.

She lowered the staff. "Good."

Then she started walking again, and the matter was closed for now.

---

Markos met them at the gate and counted the jars before asking whether anyone was hurt. Kassandra noticed. Theron noticed her noticing.

"All six," Markos said. "Seals mostly intact. Beautiful. I knew those men would see reason."

"They saw Kassandra," Theron said. "Close enough."

Markos paid them three drachmae and four obols each. He did it without trying to renegotiate, which suggested the oil had already been promised to a buyer for more than twenty-four drachmae.

Theron put the coins in his purse.

`[ Balance: 1 dr 3 ob + 3 dr 4 ob = 5 dr 1 ob ]`

He had spent more than half of what he owned that morning and ended the day one obol short of where he began. In exchange, he had a knife, rope, most of a bandage roll, and a better idea of how quickly a real fight went wrong.

That was a good trade.

After supper, Kassandra made him hold the knife again.

She tapped his wrist with the staff. He kept the blade.

The second strike hit his knuckles and he dropped it.

"Again," she said.

Theron picked it up.

Tomorrow they would practice feet. After that, distance. When he could survive five breaths, they would try for six. The numbers would move when the work deserved it.

Under the limestone cut north of town, a fitted wall waited in the dark. Theron had time to learn what stood behind it.
