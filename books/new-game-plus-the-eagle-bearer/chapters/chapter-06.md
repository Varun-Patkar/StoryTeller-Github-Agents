# Chapter 6: The Wolf's War

The Spartan blockade found them before Megaris did.

Three triremes cut across the Adrestia's course at dawn, their red sails bright against a pale sea. Bronze rams pointed toward Barnabas's hull. Archers lined the nearest deck while a signalman raised two yellow flags and waited for an answer.

Barnabas squinted at the flags. "They want our name, port, cargo, patron, and reason for approaching. They also question my judgment, though that may be a separate flag."

"Tell them we carry misthioi for the Wolf," Kassandra said.

"A short answer. Very Spartan."

He ordered the matching flags raised. Theron stood beside the mast and watched the coast beyond the warships. Megaris lay between Attika and the Peloponnese, a small region being squeezed by two much larger powers. Smoke rose from Megara's walls. Spartan camps covered the shore outside the city, and Athenian ships waited farther east near the port of Nisaia.

The game had rendered the war as forts, supply crates, and a nation-power meter.

The people on the coast had rendered it as empty fishing boats and fields harvested before the grain was ripe.

One Spartan trireme came alongside. Its captain checked Elpenor's military token, looked at Kassandra's broken Spear, then studied Theron's bracer without recognizing it.

"Nikolaos receives no visitors during a campaign," he said.

Kassandra took the token back. "He will receive us."

"Speak to Stentor at the beach camp. If he finds a use for you, the Wolf may spare the time."

The ships separated. Barnabas waited until the Spartan captain was out of easy hearing.

"A friendly welcome," he said. "Nobody fired."

"Your standards are improving," Theron replied.

"Near Athens, that counts as affection."

The Adrestia entered a narrow anchorage west of Megara. Glaux left her shaded perch near the stern and flew toward the broken hills inland. Ikaros climbed above her into open sky.

Barnabas lowered the boarding boat. "We can stay two days before the Spartans ask what we're carrying and discover the answer is mostly hungry rowers."

"Buy grain if anyone will sell it," Theron said. "Do not take from the villages."

Barnabas nodded. His humor left for a moment as he looked at the stripped farms. "My crew will fish. Poseidon owes us after the Cyclops."

"Does he know that?"

"He will. I pray loudly."

Kassandra stepped into the boat. Her hand stayed near the pouch holding Nikolaos's token.

They rowed ashore beneath the Spartan flags.

____

Stentor had Nikolaos's posture and none of his patience.

He stood over a campaign table beneath a red awning, broad through the shoulders, his bronze cuirass scratched from use. Three officers moved markers across a wax map while runners entered and left with reports. Stentor dismissed the officers when Kassandra gave her name.

His attention settled on her face.

"Kassandra of Sparta," he said. "The child who killed Nikolaos's son."

Kassandra's expression went still. "You know one version of that day."

"I know my brother fell from Taygetos. I know you were cast after him. I know the Wolf spent years paying for both."

Theron remembered Stentor from the game as a grievance wearing Spartan armor. In person, he was worse at hiding the grief beneath it.

Kassandra set Elpenor's token on the table. "We came to speak with Nikolaos."

Stentor barely glanced at it. "A merchant's token does not command a Spartan general."

"Then consider it proof that someone is paying for his death."

That earned his full attention.

"Who?"

"A merchant named Elpenor hired us to kill the Wolf," Theron said. "He paid three hundred drachmae in advance and promised a thousand after."

Stentor looked at him for the first time. "And you are?"

"Theron. Her partner."

"Second spear?"

"First plan."

Kassandra's mouth moved slightly. Stentor noticed and liked it even less.

"Two island mercenaries come into my camp carrying a murder contract, then ask for access to the target," he said. "You expect trust?"

"No," Kassandra replied. "We expect work."

That answer reached him. He turned the wax map toward them.

Athens held Megara and Nisaia. Sparta controlled most of the countryside but had failed twice to secure Fort Geraneia, a fortified position above the western road. The fort protected an Athenian supply yard and a signal tower. Any Spartan force moving toward the city would be marked before it crossed the first ridge.

Six wooden tablets sat beside the map. Each described a separate task: kill an Athenian captain, burn military supplies, steal treasury coin, destroy weapon racks, free prisoners, and lower the nation's ability to resist.

It was the regional quest list with the names removed.

Theron read all six and set five aside.

Stentor's jaw tightened. "You discard Spartan orders quickly."

"These are six ways to spend men for the same result," Theron said. He tapped Fort Geraneia. "Take the signal tower and the supply yard. Athens loses warning, food, weapons, and the road in one action. You can move before they replace any of it."

"We tried. The south gate has two towers. The east wall stands over a ravine. The north approach is visible for half a stade."

"Then we do not use those."

Stentor gave him a hard look. "You have never seen the fort."

"I will before sunset."

Kassandra leaned over the map. "Give us until dawn. Hold your men beyond the western ridge. When the signal tower goes dark, advance."

"You plan to take a fort with two people?"

"No," she said. "We plan to open it. Your soldiers can do the walking."

Stentor looked between them. His first judgment of Theron as an attached blade had lasted less than a minute. The second, attached nuisance, appeared stronger.

"You have until dawn," he said. "Fail, and you leave my camp. Succeed, and I bring your contract to the Wolf."

Kassandra took the token from the table. "Have your men ready."

"They are Spartans."

"Then they should manage it."

They left before the argument could become recreational.

____

Fort Geraneia had been repaired by people who trusted stone more than water.

Theron lay beneath a thorn bush on the ridge west of the fort and watched Ikaros circle above it. Kassandra shared the eagle's wider view in brief turns, counting forty-three soldiers inside, seven on the walls, and two patrols covering the western road.

The signal tower stood at the fort's highest point. A polished bronze shield hung beneath its fire basket. In daylight, the Athenians used reflected sunlight. At night, they used flame. Fast, cheap, and difficult to silence from outside.

Glaux found the other route.

She flew low through an abandoned olive grove below the north wall, vanished into a roofed channel, and returned a few minutes later with damp feathers. The images she shared came in quick pieces: fitted limestone, shallow water, a bronze grate, then darkness beneath the supply yard.

"Cistern overflow," Theron said. "The channel predates the fort."

Kassandra looked at the dry slope. "Will you fit?"

"With less dignity than usual."

"A terrible loss."

They moved down the ridge after sunset. Kassandra watched patrols through Ikaros until the eagle returned to a pine above the western path. Glaux waited beside the channel entrance and clicked her beak at Theron's delay.

The bronze grate had green corrosion around three bars. Theron touched the Forge Key to the center joint.

Thin lines crossed his sight, showing the mix of copper and tin, the harder repair pins, and a crack hidden beneath mineral deposits. The Key did not tell him what to do. It gave him facts and expected competence afterward.

He could work with that.

"The lower left bar comes out," he said. "Quietly, if the pin moves. Loudly, if it breaks."

Kassandra took position behind a low wall where she could see both the channel and the northern patrol.

"Risks," she said.

"The channel may narrow under the fort. The cistern may have an inner grate. If the Athenians close the sluice, I get wet and annoyed. If I miss the dawn signal, you leave the ridge and return to Stentor."

"No. If you miss the signal, I come through the south gate."

"That gate has two towers."

"Then you should not miss the signal."

Theron accepted the correction. "Your risks?"

"Two patrols on the road, archers on both towers, and Stentor may advance early to prove he does not need us. I stay hidden until the signal. If fighting starts inside before then, I use the west wall where the stones have settled."

They had both seen the shallow sag beneath the parapet. Her broad sight found the weakest defended route. His concentrated sight found why it was weak.

Theron opened the System shop once he was below the wall and hidden from Kassandra.

The supplies appeared in the dry section of the channel. He packed the bandages and salve away, tied one coil of rope to the grate, and worked the bronze wire around the cracked pin.

Kassandra pulled when he lifted two fingers.

The pin came free. The lower bar shifted enough for Theron to pass with his field bag above the water.

"Dignity survived," he whispered.

"I disagree."

He entered the channel.

...

The second grate was iron.

Theron reached it after crawling beneath the north wall through water that smelled of old copper. The grate stood under a square opening in the supply yard. Lamplight crossed overhead every time a sentry walked past.

The Forge Key traced an iron latch on the far side, well beyond his fingers. Theron fed bronze wire through the gap, bent it around the latch, and waited for the next patrol to pass.

The wire pulled. The latch rose.

He caught the grate before it fell inward.

Glaux slipped through first. She crossed the supply yard and entered the storehouse through a smoke vent. Theron followed, lowered the grate behind him, and rolled beneath a cart.

The yard held more than military supplies. Grain jars stood beside shield bundles and arrow crates. Three locked wagons carried sealed sacks marked for Megara's civic granary. The Athenian garrison had taken the city's food into the fort, then issued it back under guard.

Burning the yard would hurt Athens for a week and Megarians for a month.

Theron changed the plan.

He crossed to the storehouse while the sentry faced the wall. The door lock was ordinary iron. His knife lifted the worn catch in seconds.

Inside, amphorae of lamp oil filled two racks. Spare bowstrings, spear shafts, and signal fuel occupied the back wall. A thick rope ran through the ceiling toward the western gate's counterweight.

Concentrated Eagle Vision narrowed the room to useful paths. Oil residue marked the floor beneath the left rack. The gate rope carried fresh wear where it crossed a rough stone guide. A line of pale dust beside the rear wall showed where the foundation had moved.

The old retaining wall below the supply yard was taking more weight than its builders intended. Rain had washed soil through the foundation. The Athenians had braced the storehouse floor and called the problem solved.

It was not solved.

Theron wrapped bronze wire around the gate rope above the frayed section. He fixed the other end to a shelf brace, creating a crude secondary line. Then he cut halfway through the damaged rope.

When the gate dropped, the old line would break. His wire would catch the counterweight long enough to stop it from slamming shut again.

He moved to the oil racks. Rather than spill them, he pulled the sealing cords from twelve amphorae and pushed the stoppers deep into the necks. The garrison would need tools and time to open them. He took the signal tower's reserve oil and poured it into a covered drainage jar.

The bowstrings went into the cistern.

The grain stayed dry.

A footstep stopped outside the door.

Theron slid behind the oil rack. The door opened, and an Athenian quartermaster entered carrying a wax tablet. He checked the spear bundles, counted two oil rows, and noticed the missing cords.

His hand went to his sword.

Glaux dropped from the rafters and struck the lamp out of his hand.

The room went dark. Theron caught the quartermaster's wrist before the sword cleared its sheath, turned it against the doorframe, and pressed his knife below the man's ear.

"Quiet," he said.

The quartermaster drove backward instead.

The shelf brace cracked under their combined weight.

Theron heard stone grind beneath the floor.

That was new information. It arrived late, loudly, and with poor manners.

He released the man and jumped aside. The left oil rack leaned as the floor dropped beneath one leg. Amphorae struck each other. One broke, spreading oil across the stones.

The quartermaster shouted.

Theron kicked the door closed, struck him once in the stomach, then pulled the first rope from his bag. He looped it around the leaning rack and anchored it to the gate-rope guide.

The floor sank another finger's width.

The old retaining wall was failing now. If it went at once, the supply yard would collapse into the channel and bury the grain with Theron under it.

Backup plans were useful. Leaving early was often the best one.

He tied off the rope, climbed the stair to the signal tower, and met the first guard halfway up.

The guard thrust with a spear. Theron saw the worn leather under his forward hand and caught the shaft there. He pulled, stepped past the point, and drove his shoulder into the man's chest.

The guard fell down six steps and struck the quartermaster coming out of the storehouse.

Theron kept climbing.

At the top, a signalman reached for the bronze shield. Theron threw the second coil of wire. It wrapped the man's forearm and dragged the shield sideways. Sunlight flashed once across the western ridge, too short to carry a coded warning.

Theron cut the shield cord. Bronze hit stone.

Ikaros cried above the fort.

Kassandra saw the signal tower go dark.

The western wall erupted.

____

Kassandra hit the settled stones where Theron had marked them with chalk.

The first block shifted. The second went inward. On the third strike, a section of parapet collapsed into the yard and took one surprised archer with it.

She came through the gap before the dust cleared.

The nearest soldier raised his shield. Kassandra hit its rim with the Spear of Leonidas and drove it into his face. She caught the second man's sword arm, turned him across her path, and used him to block a spear from the stairs.

Theron reached the western gate mechanism as Stentor's soldiers appeared on the ridge.

The quartermaster had recovered enough to understand the plan. He ran for the gate rope with a knife.

Theron threw his iron blade.

It struck the wax tablet tied at the man's belt and pinned the tablet to the storehouse door. The quartermaster stopped from pure confusion.

Theron hit him with the bronze signal shield.

It was not an elegant weapon. It made a satisfying noise.

He pulled the gate release.

The counterweight dropped. The damaged rope snapped exactly where he had cut it. Bronze wire caught the mechanism, stretched, and held.

The western gate opened halfway.

Stentor's first rank came through under locked shields. Athenian arrows struck bronze. Most deflected. Two found gaps, and the men behind dragged the wounded clear without breaking the advance.

The fort commander shouted for the inner yard to form around the supply wagons.

Kassandra's awareness spread across the fight. She saw the shield line building before Theron could see past the gatehouse. She also saw three archers climbing the south tower and pointed with the broken Spear.

"Tower!"

Theron followed the immediate problem. The Forge Key traced the tower's wooden stair supports through the plaster facing. His Eagle Vision picked out two old joints and one new brace.

He cut the brace rope.

The stair twisted away from the wall. It dropped less than a man's height, enough to throw all three archers into each other and block anyone behind them.

Kassandra reached the forming shield line.

She did not charge its center. She ran onto the low watering trough, stepped across the end of a grain wagon, and landed behind the captain calling cadence. The Spear struck the back of his knee. When he fell, she took the crest of his helmet and pulled him away from his men.

The line turned to follow her.

Stentor's Spartans hit its exposed side.

The fight broke into short, ugly pieces. Shields collided. Men slipped on spilled grain from a torn sack. One Athenian tried to light the oil outside the storehouse and discovered the nearest amphora had no accessible stopper.

Theron reached him before he found the broken one.

The man swung a torch. Theron caught his wrist with both hands and drove it against the edge of a cart. The torch dropped into the dirt. Theron stamped it out, then used the cart wheel to trap the man's ankle until a Spartan disarmed him.

"Do not burn the grain," Theron told the Spartan.

The soldier stared at him as if mercenaries did not normally protect enemy supplies.

"The city needs it," Theron added.

That explanation worked better.

Stone cracked beneath the storehouse.

The retaining wall finally gave way.

The outer face dropped into the abandoned olive grove. Half the storehouse floor tilted after it. Theron's rope pulled tight around the oil rack, and the gate guide groaned under the added load.

The bronze wire holding the gate began to stretch.

"Clear the entrance!" Theron shouted.

Stentor heard him from the inner yard. He ordered his rear rank through, then moved the wounded beyond the gate. The last Spartan crossed as the wire snapped.

The counterweight fell the remaining distance. The gate slammed down behind them.

Athenians on the western road reached it seconds too late.

Inside, their commander saw the Spartans between him and every exit. He looked at Kassandra, who had put his captain on the ground and taken his sword. Then he looked at Stentor's shield line.

He dropped his weapon.

The remaining soldiers followed.

The fort had taken less than a quarter hour to fall.

Theron sat on an intact grain jar and checked the cut across his left palm. The broken bronze wire had opened it when the gate dropped. Three stitches would close it.

Kassandra arrived with blood on one shoulder that belonged to someone else.

"You missed the signal," she said.

"I removed the signal."

"Late."

"The building objected."

She looked at the collapsed storehouse floor. "You fought a building?"

"It had poor foundations and a numerical advantage."

Kassandra took the bandages from his field bag and wrapped his palm with more force than necessary.

Stentor approached while she tied the knot.

He looked from the dark signal tower to the open grain wagons, then toward the western gate where his soldiers had trapped an Athenian patrol outside.

"You left their food intact," he said.

"Megara's food," Theron replied. "The military oil and bowstrings are unusable. Your men can distribute the grain under guard when you take the road."

"You changed the objective after entering."

"The facts changed."

Stentor looked at Kassandra. "And you trusted him to do that?"

"He trusts me to change mine."

The answer seemed to bother Stentor because he understood it.

He called for his quartermaster and ordered two wagons released to villages west of Megara. The rest would follow under Spartan guard before noon. It was a practical order rather than a kind one, but hungry people would eat either way.

Then he faced them again.

"The Wolf will inspect this position at sunrise," he said. "The fort's signal will remain dark. Their western supplies will not arrive. You earned a place before him."

Kassandra stood. "Both of us."

Stentor looked at Theron. "The plan was yours. The breach was hers. The gate belonged to both. Yes, both of you."

It was the first time he had included Theron without being reminded.

The Spartans paid for results even when they disliked the people producing them.

Stentor's quartermaster counted two hundred drachmae onto a captured shield, one hundred for each misthios. He also replaced Theron's lost bronze wire from the fort stores, though the local coil was heavier and less flexible than the System-made material.

Theron accepted the difference.

The obsidian eye remained aboard the Adrestia. Elpenor still owed them a thousand drachmae. For one day of work, the numbers had moved in the right direction.

The important part was camped beyond the next ridge.

Barnabas met them at the beach after dark with grilled fish, flatbread, and a complaint about Spartan harbor fees. Glaux took a piece of fish from his plate while he described the injustice.

"That owl is a pirate," he said.

Theron watched her eat from the mast beam. "You recruited the crew."

Kassandra sat at the rail with Nikolaos's token in one hand. She had said little since leaving the fort.

Theron carried two cups of watered wine over and set one beside her.

"Stentor calls him father," she said.

"Nikolaos adopted him."

"You knew."

"Yes."

She turned the token over. "He looked at me like I came back to take something."

"Tomorrow, he may learn you did not."

"I came for answers."

"Those are different."

Kassandra drank and made a face. Barnabas had been generous with the water.

Beyond the anchorage, Spartan campfires climbed the dark coast toward Megara. Torches moved along the western road as the Wolf's guard shifted toward the captured fort.

Nikolaos would be there at sunrise.

Kassandra put the token away. "Tomorrow I speak to the Wolf."

Theron lifted his cup. "And after that?"

"After that, I decide for myself."

It was the same answer she had given when Kephallonia disappeared behind them.

This time, Nikolaos was close enough to hear it.
