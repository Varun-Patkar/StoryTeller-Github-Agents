# Chapter 8: The False Oracle

Elpenor had left a man at the dock to collect Nikolaos's helmet.

The man stood beneath a faded awning at Kirrha, the port below Delphi, holding a wax tablet and wearing the patient expression of someone waiting to be paid. He watched the Adrestia tie off, counted Kassandra and Theron as they came down the gangplank, then looked behind them.

"Where is the proof?" he asked.

Kassandra stopped in front of him. "The Wolf lives."

The man's stylus paused above the wax.

Theron watched his eyes move toward the road north. It was quick, but quick reactions still counted.

"Then the contract failed," the man said.

"The contract changed," Kassandra replied. "Elpenor can explain why he sent men to kill Nikolaos after I spared him."

"I know nothing about that."

"You knew enough to look for help."

The man dropped the tablet and ran.

He made it six steps. Kassandra caught the back of his belt and put him against a warehouse post hard enough to remove his interest in a seventh.

Barnabas leaned over the Adrestia's rail. "Should I prepare the ship to leave quickly?"

"Prepare it to leave normally," Theron said.

"That is harder. Nobody remembers a normal departure."

The courier carried twelve obols, a bronze key, and a delivery slip for an estate outside the port. The slip named three amphorae of Chian wine, two baskets of figs, and enough lamp oil to keep a large house bright for a month. It also bore a merchant's mark Theron had seen on Elpenor's contract.

Kassandra turned the courier around. "Is Elpenor at the estate?"

"He left yesterday."

"Where?"

"He did not tell me."

Theron picked up the wax tablet. A single line had been scratched across it.

*Wolf unconfirmed. Prepare the second account.*

"He told you enough," Theron said.

The courier looked at the road again.

Ikaros had already taken the open sky above it. Glaux waited beneath the warehouse roof, watching the spaces between stacked cargo.

Kassandra released the man. "Go tell Elpenor we arrived."

He stared at her.

"You want me to run?"

"Very much."

He ran north.

Barnabas came down the gangplank carrying a coil of ship's rope. "You let him go."

"Ikaros will follow him," Kassandra said.

"Ah. A normal arrival, then."

Theron gave Barnabas the delivery slip. "Keep the ship ready. Buy food for three days, water for five. Pay no one claiming to collect a sacred harbor fee."

Barnabas looked offended. "I know a false sacred fee when I hear one."

"You paid two in Megaris."

"One may have been real. The man had a very religious hat."

Kassandra was already walking toward the road. Theron followed before Barnabas could defend the hat.

____

Elpenor's estate had been emptied badly.

The couches, painted pottery, and bronze dining lamps remained. The household slaves had gone. So had the account chests, bedroom linens, and every document from the writing room.

Someone had tried to make the place look occupied from the road. A cooking fire burned behind the house, but the pot above it held water and three stones.

"Poor stew," Kassandra said.

"Needs salt."

The courier had entered through the front gate ten minutes earlier. Ikaros saw him leave through the rear with two armed men. They headed northeast toward the hills below Delphi.

Theron could have followed immediately. Elpenor expected that. A merchant who survived by arranging other people's deaths would leave a clean trail and prepare something unpleasant at the end.

The useful trail would be the one he had tried to erase.

Glaux entered through a high window. Her shared sight gave Theron quick pieces of the upper floor: bare sleeping mats, an open cedar chest, dust around a missing statue, and a writing room with one table leg shorter than the others.

He went upstairs.

Kassandra checked the courtyard and joined him at the table. "You think he left a confession under it?"

"I think a rich man tolerated a crooked table in the room where he counted money."

The short leg rested on a thin bronze plate. Theron touched the Forge Key to it and narrowed his sight.

The bracer traced the metal through the floor. A rod ran beneath the leg, crossed under two tiles, and ended at the wall. Theron pressed the plate. A section of plaster beside the table released with a quiet click.

Behind it sat a narrow compartment.

Kassandra looked at the Key. "Forty-six percent still finds better furniture than Markos buys."

"Markos would call the crooked leg a negotiation feature."

The compartment held six clay seals, a bundle of blank military orders, and a strip of black cloth wrapped around a silver triangle. The triangle carried a branching mark cut into its face.

Theron knew the symbol. The Cult of Kosmos used it on messages meant for members who had not yet earned a mask.

He kept that knowledge behind his teeth and set the object on the table.

Kassandra held it beside the unsigned order from Geraneia. The parchment fibers matched. So did the black wax pressed into one corner.

"Same writer?" she asked.

"Same supply of parchment and sealing wax. The letter on the mountain came through this house."

One clay seal carried the image of Apollo's lyre. Another bore the name of a priest attached to the Temple of Apollo at Delphi. Three seals belonged to merchants in Lokris. The last showed a snake wound around a short blade.

Theron turned that one over. Fine grey dust filled a chip along its rim.

Limestone. Freshly cut.

Kassandra opened the blank orders. "These can send soldiers anywhere."

"Or move armed men past city gates."

"The Pythia serves Apollo."

"And somebody in this house expected to issue orders in a priest's name."

Kassandra put the papers into her bag. "Then we find Elpenor before he issues another."

Theron opened the System shop while she searched the shelves. Elpenor liked poison in the game. Theron preferred not to test whether the man had improved his habits.

`[ SHOP | Balance: 546 dr 2 ob ]`

`Antidote, broad plant toxins x2 ... 2 dr 4 ob`

`Smoke pots x2 ..................... 3 dr 0 ob`

`Fine bronze picks ................. 4 dr 2 ob`

`[ Purchase total: 10 dr 0 ob ]`

`[ Remaining balance: 536 dr 2 ob ]`

The purchases appeared inside his field bag. He passed one antidote vial to Kassandra without explaining where it had come from. She had long ago stopped asking why his bag produced exactly the tool he wanted. Her working theory involved compulsive preparation and a private problem with money.

He had packed the same medicine before leaving Kephallonia. Replacing used supplies through the shop kept the System hidden inside an ordinary habit.

"Drink half if a blade cuts you," he said. "The rest if your fingers go numb."

"His knives are poisoned?"

"A merchant who sends other people to fight should invest in unfair knives."

Kassandra tucked the vial under her belt. "Risks?"

"The trail is prepared. He has at least the courier and two guards. The seal came from a limestone cave or quarry. Ikaros stays high and watches the exits. Glaux checks the enclosed spaces. If Elpenor runs, we take him alive until he answers about Myrrine and the Pythia."

"If he fights?"

"He answers while he still can."

"Your risks?"

"Poison, narrow ground, and whatever he placed at the obvious entrance. I stay behind you until we know the shape of it."

Kassandra nodded. "I take the first attack. You stop anyone leaving."

They left through the back gate and followed Ikaros into the hills.

____

Elpenor had chosen a snake cave.

Theron remembered that detail from the game. In person, the cave was a limestone cut above the road to Delphi, screened by cypress and old quarry spoil. Fresh boot marks crossed the lower path. A thin copper wire ran between two stones at ankle height.

The prepared trail ended in a warning bell.

Theron cut the wire and fixed it around the clapper instead. Anyone pulling the alarm from inside would silence it.

Glaux entered through a crack above the cave mouth. She found two guards near the first bend, the courier beside a supply chest, and Elpenor waiting in a larger chamber beyond them. A second passage climbed toward daylight behind him.

Ikaros marked the upper exit for Kassandra.

"Three in front," Theron said. "Elpenor has a rear path. Ikaros sees where it opens."

Kassandra checked the Spear at her back. "Smoke?"

"Save it for the house in Delphi."

"You bought smoke for a house we have not seen."

"I have faith in architecture."

She stepped over the disabled wire and walked into the cave.

The first guard challenged her from behind a round shield. "Elpenor receives no one."

"He received three hundred drachmae worth of work," Kassandra said. "He still owes a thousand."

The second guard drew his sword.

Kassandra hit the shield with her shoulder. The guard expected her to bounce away. Instead, he struck the wall and lost his footing. She trapped the shield against him, caught the second man's sword wrist with the Spear shaft, and drove her knee into his stomach.

The courier reached for the alarm cord.

He pulled. The bound clapper made a small scrape.

Theron came around the bend and showed him the cut wire.

"The gods dislike repetition," he said.

The courier grabbed a knife. Theron struck his forearm against the supply chest, took the blade, and pushed him down beside it.

Kassandra had both guards on the floor. One tried to rise. She tapped the Spear against the stone beside his hand.

He reconsidered.

Elpenor applauded from the next chamber.

He wore a white merchant's robe, clean sandals, and the same superior smile he had used on Kephallonia. A short sword hung at his side. Two dead snakes lay near his feet, placed there recently enough that flies had not found them.

"The mighty Eagle Bearer returns," he said. "Without the Wolf's helmet."

Kassandra walked into the chamber. Theron moved along the outer wall where he could see the upper passage.

"Nikolaos lives," she said.

"Then you failed."

"Your men failed too."

Elpenor's smile held, though his eyes moved to Theron.

Theron took out the mountain order and the silver triangle. "Your parchment. Your wax. Your compartment."

"Items stolen from my house prove someone robbed me."

"Your courier led us there. Then here."

The courier looked down at the cave floor.

Elpenor sighed. "Competent servants are rarer than honest merchants."

Kassandra stopped a spear's length from him. "Why did Nikolaos have to die if I spared him?"

"Because Sparta needs a war, and the Wolf had begun to win his. A dead general creates uncertainty. A daughter accused of killing him creates more."

That was an answer. Elpenor gave it because he believed they would die with it.

"Who needs the war?" Kassandra asked.

"Every sensible man who earns from it."

"Who sent you?"

"You mistake business for service. I choose profitable work."

Theron focused his Eagle Vision on Elpenor. The merchant's pulse beat hard at his throat. His left hand stayed loose near his belt, while his right remained close to the sword. A faint green smear marked the inside of his left cuff.

Poison.

"He has something in his left sleeve," Theron said.

Elpenor threw it at Kassandra.

The clay vial broke against the Spear shaft. Yellow powder filled the space between them.

Kassandra turned her face and drove forward. Elpenor drew his sword, caught one strike, and retreated toward the upper passage.

Theron moved first. He kicked the dead snakes into Elpenor's path.

The merchant stepped on one, slipped, and struck the wall with his shoulder.

It was an undignified use of local wildlife. It worked.

Elpenor slashed at Theron. The poisoned edge passed close enough for Theron to smell bitter herbs on the metal. Theron caught Elpenor's wrist with the Forge Key bracer, turned the blade away, and held him for Kassandra.

She put the broken Spear through his side.

Elpenor folded around it. His sword hit the floor.

Kassandra held him upright against the wall. "Myrrine. Where is she?"

Blood darkened his robe. He smiled anyway, smaller now and far less convincing.

"Ask the oracle. She told Sparta to throw your brother from a mountain. Perhaps she has improved with age."

"Who controls her?"

"Men with better questions than yours."

Theron picked up the poisoned sword and set it out of reach. "The invitation in your room. The mask you are wearing beneath that robe. They meet under the temple."

Elpenor's eyes fixed on him.

That reaction was the proof Theron needed for Kassandra. It also told Elpenor exactly how much trouble had reached his cave.

"What are you?" Elpenor asked.

"A man who checks crooked tables."

Kassandra pushed the Spear deeper. "When do they meet?"

"Soon."

"How soon?"

"You should hurry."

His left hand moved again. A narrow blade slid from his cuff toward Kassandra's ribs.

Theron caught his elbow. Kassandra saw the blade and ended the fight.

Elpenor dropped beside the dead snakes.

For a moment, the cave held only the courier's rough breathing from the outer chamber.

Kassandra wiped the Spear on Elpenor's robe. "He talked too much for a man with so little time."

"He thought every answer made him important."

They searched him. Beneath his robe, Elpenor wore a dark leather mask shaped into a fixed, severe face. A bronze token hung behind it, marked with the same branching symbol as the silver triangle.

His belt pouch held a folded invitation.

*To the chosen of Kosmos. Enter beneath Apollo's gaze. Wear the face granted to you. Speak no name. The Pyramid remembers blood.*

Below the words, a diagram showed a side chamber of the Temple of Apollo and a stair hidden under a bronze brazier.

Kassandra read it once. "Kosmos."

"A group large enough to move priests, merchants, soldiers, and hired blades," Theron said. "Elpenor belonged to it."

"And they know about my family."

"They know enough to fear what happens if it reunites."

The upper passage led to Elpenor's private camp. Theron found a locked iron chest behind a sleeping mat. The new bronze picks opened it after the third attempt.

Inside were ten sealed bags, each holding one hundred drachmae. The contract's unpaid balance, prepared for a death Elpenor had never expected to purchase honestly.

Kassandra looked at the money. "He planned to pay us?"

"He planned to show us money. Payment may have involved another knife."

Theron fed the coins into the System inventory one bag at a time.

`[ Contract balance recovered: +1,000 dr ]`

`[ Balance: 536 dr 2 ob + 1,000 dr = 1,536 dr 2 ob ]`

No reward appeared for killing Elpenor. The mask, invitation, and frightened men in the outer chamber were enough.

Kassandra tied the two guards and courier with Elpenor's own supply rope. "They can explain the body to whoever comes next."

"They may know where the Pythia sleeps."

The courier answered before Kassandra asked. "A house east of the sanctuary. Red door. Laurel trees in the yard. Two guards at the street and priests inside."

"How many priests?" Theron asked.

"Three. Sometimes four."

"Warning route?"

"A bell in the rear court. The lower temple can hear it."

Elpenor had used the man to carry food and messages. He knew more than his employer had valued.

Kassandra took the mask. "We go to the oracle."

____

Delphi sold certainty to people who had climbed too far to leave without it.

Pilgrims filled the Sacred Way, carrying goats, wine, coins, and questions toward the Temple of Apollo. Treasuries lined the rising path. Priests sorted offerings with the speed of dock clerks.

Theron and Kassandra entered among a group from Thebes. Elpenor's mask stayed wrapped in Kassandra's bag.

The Pythia's house stood east of the sanctuary, exactly where the courier had placed it. The red door faced a busy lane. Two armed guards watched the front while petitioners passed within twenty paces.

Glaux found the rear court from above. A bronze bell hung beside the kitchen door. A narrow covered drain ran under the outer wall and opened near a public fountain.

Theron studied the house through the Forge Key. The bell cord crossed the kitchen rafters and continued into an inner hall. Pulling either end would warn the sanctuary. The rear door had a bronze bar. The upper shutters were wood, recently replaced.

"I take the drain," he said. "You enter when the rear door opens."

Kassandra looked at the opening, then at his shoulders. "You have become larger since the fort."

"The drain has not become smaller."

"That is a hopeful interpretation."

"Risks?"

"The front guards may follow. Ikaros watches them. I keep the priests alive if they surrender."

"The cord may have another branch. I disable the bell before opening the door. If it rings, we leave with the Pythia through the rear and use smoke on the lane."

Theron crossed behind the fountain and lowered himself into the drain.

The fit was worse than Fort Geraneia. He crawled six cubits through cold water, removed the inner grate with the bronze picks, and came up beneath a kitchen bench.

A priest stood at the table cutting bread.

Theron waited until the man turned, then rose and pressed the iron knife against his back.

"Hands on the table," he said. "I am here for the bell, not you."

The priest obeyed.

Theron's concentrated sight followed the cord through the rafters. One line ended at the courtyard bell. A second crossed the inner hall and entered the Pythia's room. The Cult had given their captive a way to summon her own guards, then made sure the signal also warned the temple.

He cut both lines and tied them around the roof beam.

The rear bar lifted. Kassandra entered and caught a second priest coming down the hall. He reached for a knife. She put him against the wall and took it away.

The front guards heard the impact. One entered through the red door while the other ran toward the sanctuary.

Ikaros cried once.

Kassandra met the first guard in the hall. Theron went through the kitchen, crossed the courtyard, and threw a smoke pot into the lane ahead of the runner.

The clay shell broke. Thick grey smoke covered the intersection. The guard stopped, turned toward the rear court, and found Glaux striking at his face.

Theron caught him from behind and pulled him into the yard. Kassandra arrived with the other guard's sword in one hand.

"Two pots," she said.

"One remains."

"You sound disappointed."

"I enjoy prepared exits."

The Pythia opened her own door.

Praxithea was older than Theron remembered from the game, or perhaps fear had made her look older there. Grey ran through her dark hair. Her white ceremonial dress was plain away from the temple, and red marks circled one wrist where a cord had rubbed the skin.

She looked at the bound priests, the guards in the courtyard, and Kassandra's Spear.

"You came after Elpenor," she said.

Kassandra entered the room. Theron stayed by the door where he could watch the hall.

"You know him," Kassandra said.

"I know the mask he wears."

Kassandra set it on the table.

Praxithea sat down.

"Then he is dead."

"Yes."

"They will replace him."

"Who are they?"

The Pythia looked toward the cut bell cord. "The Cult of Kosmos. They decide which questions reach me and which answers leave the temple. They threaten my family when Apollo proves inconvenient."

Kassandra put both hands on the table. "Did they order the oracle that condemned Alexios?"

"I was not the Pythia then. The woman before me died years ago. But the Cult keeps records. I have seen the order. A child of Leonidas would threaten their design for Greece. The oracle came from men beneath the temple."

Kassandra's fingers pressed into the wood. "Nikolaos obeyed a merchant in a mask."

"He obeyed Sparta," Praxithea said. "The Cult made certain Sparta asked."

That distinction made the crime larger. It did nothing to make Nikolaos smaller inside it.

"My mother survived Taygetos," Kassandra said. "Where did she go?"

Praxithea studied her face. "Myrrine came to Delphi years ago. She demanded answers about both children. The Cult's men tried to hold her, but she escaped."

Kassandra leaned closer. "Where did the roads take her?"

"The men below seized every report. One complained that she had survived long enough to become a problem. Their records may still be in the sanctuary."

Theron knew the route ended much farther east, with Myrrine ruling Naxos. The Pythia had given Kassandra proof that her mother survived Delphi. The next answer waited below them.

"Where do the Cult meet?" he asked.

Praxithea looked at him for the first time. Her attention went to the Forge Key, then to the knife in his hand.

"Beneath the Temple of Apollo. There is an old chamber below the sacred vault. Members enter masked through a stair in the western preparation room. Their next gathering begins tonight."

"How many?"

"Dozens pass through Delphi. Fewer attend at once. Guards remain above because masks are their protection below."

"What is the Pyramid?"

Her face tightened. "A black stone that shows them things when blood touches it. They fear it. They obey it. I have never entered the chamber."

Theron's modern memory supplied the rest: a Piece of Eden used for visions, memory feedback, and control. In chapter nine it would show Kassandra that Deimos was Alexios. For now, the important fact was simpler.

Elpenor's mask could take them inside.

Kassandra straightened. "Come with us."

"My family remains in Delphi. Running would make them targets."

"Staying makes you one."

"Staying lets me hear what they ask. Now that you know the bell, I can change which warnings reach them."

The Pythia had spent years surviving under control. Theron had no right to turn rescue into another order.

He placed Elpenor's clay seal beside her. "This priest's name was in his house. Is he one of them?"

Praxithea looked at it. "He carries messages. He does not enter the chamber. Remove him, and they will know someone spoke. Leave him, and I can feed him an answer."

Kassandra understood first. "Tell him Elpenor lives. Tell him the Eagle Bearer sailed west."

"And the meeting?"

"Continues as planned," Theron said.

Praxithea nodded. "I can do that."

Theron freed the kitchen priest after Praxithea identified him as a servant with no Cult tie. The other priest and two guards stayed bound in a storage room. Praxithea would claim thieves had entered for temple silver and fled when challenged.

It was an ordinary crime. Delphi had plenty.

____

Herodotos was waiting outside the Temple of Apollo.

He stood beside a column with a scroll case over one shoulder, watching priests replace a bronze brazier in the western preparation room. He was near fifty, with a scholar's beard, a traveler's sandals, and the alert expression of a man who had just found a better question than the one that brought him there.

His gaze went to Kassandra's Spear.

"That weapon belonged to Leonidas," he said.

Kassandra kept walking. "Many spears belonged to Leonidas. He was a soldier."

"That one was carried at Thermopylae. I have studied every account I could find, including two written by men who watched him use it."

Theron stopped.

Herodotos noticed the Forge Key next. Curiosity won over caution with impressive speed.

"And that," he continued, "was never made by any bronze worker in Greece."

"You make quick judgments," Theron said.

"I revise them quickly too. It is the only honest arrangement."

Kassandra looked toward the temple entrance. Pilgrims still moved along the Sacred Way. The priests had accepted Praxithea's false story, and the sanctuary showed no sign that Elpenor's death had reached it.

"Who are you?" she asked.

"Herodotos of Halikarnassos. I collect histories before powerful men improve them."

Theron knew the name carried weight, though the most famous books still lay ahead of him.

"Theron. This is Kassandra."

"The Eagle Bearer. Yes, the eagle offered a clue."

Ikaros called from the temple roof as if accepting credit.

Herodotos stepped closer to the Spear but kept his hands away. "May I see it?"

Kassandra looked at Theron.

He touched the Forge Key to the stone column beside them and opened the fragment it had captured when the Spear passed the temple's western wall. A narrow set of energy paths appeared across the Key's surface, pale lines following a pattern beneath the sanctuary floor.

Herodotos stared.

"The Spear woke something below us," Theron said. "My bracer recorded the route. It did not identify the source."

"A mechanism under Apollo's temple," Herodotos said. "Older than the temple, if the foundation accounts are true. The oldest stones were already here when the first priests arrived."

There it was. A public bridge made from records, masonry, and a scholar willing to say the strange part aloud.

Theron pointed to the energy pattern. "The same construction appears in a quarry cache on Kephallonia. Different function, related method."

"Then the builders worked across the Greek world. Perhaps before the Greeks."

"That is my conclusion."

Herodotos looked delighted. "History, my friend, is stranger than any myth, though myth complains when history takes the credit."

Kassandra drew the Spear and held it flat across both palms. Herodotos examined the broken shaft, the metal edge, and the lines that brightened faintly near the Forge Key.

"Leonidas carried a weapon made by the same people who built what lies beneath Delphi," he said. "His blood allowed him to use it. Yours does the same."

Kassandra watched the pale lines. "My blood has caused enough trouble today."

Herodotos's curiosity eased. "Then let knowledge make some of it useful. There is an ancient forge on Andros connected to objects like this. I have spent years collecting references to it."

"And you tell strangers?" she asked.

"I tell the woman holding Leonidas's Spear and the man wearing a device that answers it. I would look foolish pretending this is an ordinary meeting."

Theron liked him immediately. That was inconvenient, but manageable.

Kassandra put the Spear away. "We have business beneath the temple first."

Herodotos looked toward the western preparation room. "The masked visitors?"

Both of them went still.

He raised one hand. "I have watched men enter that room for six months. Merchants, generals, priests, and one Athenian who publicly claims he has never visited Delphi. They arrive separately. They leave after midnight. The temple records omit every name."

"You kept notes," Theron said.

"Naturally."

"How would you like to become useful to dangerous people?"

Herodotos considered the question. "I had hoped to become useful to history. Dangerous people appear to control the schedule."

Kassandra took Elpenor's mask from her bag and unwrapped it.

For once, Herodotos had no immediate words.

Theron checked the System as the evening shadow crossed the temple steps.
