# Story Summary: Assassin's Creed: The Eclipse

## Arc I: The First Rebellion

- **Chapter 1 (The Weight of the Apple)**: Near the Isu towers on the plain of Eden (c. 75,000 BCE), the modern superfan's consciousness awakens inside Cain one heartbeat too late, his brother Abel already dead by his hand and Abel's Apple of Eden clutched in his fist. Reeling with guilt and flooded with foreknowledge of Assassin's Creed history, Cain discovers his awakened mind can resist the Isu neurotransmitter leash: he refuses the command to carry the Apple to his masters, is noticed by a watching Isu, and flees. Realizing the Apple commands only the living and can never undo Abel's death, he makes his first true choice, keeping the stolen Apple from the Isu and setting out north to find the two humans who defied them and lived: Adam and Eve.
- **Chapter 2 (A Better Class of Master)**: Three days later, a colder, hardened Cain, merged with the original Cain's ruthlessness and unbothered by grief, hunts the rebellion as his best bet to survive the coming catastrophe. When Isu-driven human thralls corner him, he uses the Apple for the first time deliberately, tearing their leashes from the Isu's grip (at the cost of a nosebleed and near-blackout; he can win only at close range, not long) and keeping one thrall as a tool. He lets the rebellion capture him, is brought before Adam and Eve, and refuses to hand over his foreknowledge cheaply. When a planted Isu thrall lunges to kill the immune Eve, Cain seizes the Apple and severs the Isu's control mid-strike, saving her. Eve reads him accurately, a self-interested wolf, not an ally to her cause, and Cain openly admits he is in it for himself. He gives his name as Cain, embracing the ugly legend as the perfect disguise, and is accepted into the rebellion on suspicion.
- **Chapter 3 (The Knife)**: A season on, Cain is the rebellion's decisive weapon, "the Knife," his mass leash-cutting turning Isu thrall-armies against themselves (ceiling ~40 leashes for ~6 seconds, brutal physical cost; freed thralls sometimes die in the chaos, which he coldly files away). His aide is Nara, the hunter he first freed, who stays with him by pure arithmetic ("You're the thing they can't hold. I pick you."). Cain reads the real situation, the Isu are abandoning the war because they have secretly calculated the coming Catastrophe, and steers Adam and Eve into marching on a specific, lightly-held Isu city, framed as the war-winning blow. Challenged by Eve, he openly admits he is steering them for his own gain and that their survival is merely a happy side effect. Only the reader learns the truth: the war is a sideshow, the sky is weeks away, and he has chosen that city because of the consciousness-preserving tech buried beneath it. He is not marching to win a war. He is marching to disappear.
