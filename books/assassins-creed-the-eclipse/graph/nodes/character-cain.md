# Character: Cain

## Overview
A man from the early 21st century, a lifelong Assassin's Creed fan, who wakes inside Cain, one of the first humans made by the Isu, in the instant after Cain murders his brother Abel. He is not a clean soul dropped into a stranger's body. The original Cain is still in there: the instincts, the appetite, the native ruthlessness of the first man willing to kill for what he wanted. The modern mind and Cain's mind are braided together, and they agree on more than the modern man is comfortable admitting.

Cain is selfish, greedy, and ambitious. He wants to live, to win, to accumulate: knowledge, power, longevity, leverage, comfort. He is going to save humanity from the catastrophes he knows are coming, but not because he is good. He is going to save it because he lives here, because extinction is the one loss he cannot profit from, and because a dead world is worth nothing to a man who intends to own a piece of everything. Over tens of thousands of years he founds The Eclipse, takes Kassandra as the one person he truly keeps, and hunts the surviving Isu, all of it in service of a long, patient, fundamentally self-interested game.

## Past Life (Cain Mercer, 21st century)
Born an orphan, abandoned at an orphanage as a baby with only a blanket and a folded note bearing the name "Cain." The orphanage and US Child Services were his first teachers: he learned early that the system does not love you, it processes you, and that the world takes from the weak. Cycled through four foster families, each of whom found him cold; a foster sister said he looked at people like furniture he was deciding whether to keep. Eventually adopted by the Mercers, an older, wealthy, childless couple. He took their surname (good name, good tax bracket) but kept the given name Cain because he liked being underestimated.

The Mercers taught him the second half of his worldview: how the world works once you have leverage. From this he derived his core rule: **what you are doesn't matter, what people believe you are is everything.** He built and rehearsed a "sunny," charitable public smile and was universally liked in school as the boy who tutored, organized food drives, remembered birthdays. Nobody was ever let close.

Self-made success: wrote an ugly-but-useful logistics software tool at nineteen, folded it into a company by twenty-three, then grew a hard-to-classify holding company by acquiring firms (fintech, data) at the exact moment sellers were too scared to hold and too tired to fight. By thirty-four he was a near-billionaire CEO (nine zeros reaching for a tenth), publicly celebrated as a philanthropist who "rose from foster care."

**Socially the most popular man in any room, with zero real friends.** Ten thousand contacts, no one to call at 3 a.m. He had run the "numbers" on friendship and love and judged them a loss (people who know you can hurt you; people who love you can leave), so he built a life where every relationship had an exit and a price tag. This is the emotional wound that makes Kassandra, tens of thousands of years later, so significant: she is the first person he ever truly keeps.

**Values distinction (keep this consistent):** He is cold and transactional but NOT gratuitously cruel. He sorts the world into two columns. **His own people get vault-deep loyalty** (pays them more than he must, quietly clears their debts, solves their private crises and never mentions it). **Everyone else is "weather"** to be read, used, and walked around; he'll take advantage of a stranger without losing sleep and is honest with himself about it. This orphanage-plus-rich-parents forged temperament is exactly the ruthlessness the original Cain already had, which is why the two minds merge so cleanly.

**Obsession that becomes literal:** His one un-audited indulgence was gaming (a place with no image to protect). His favorite game was *Vampire: The Masquerade - Bloodlines*, and his fixation was Caine, the deathless first murderer. He read the "curse" of immortality not as punishment but as a promotion: to never end, to stand at the edge of every empire as it rises and falls, to outlast everyone who underestimated him, to take a cut of both sides of every war. He didn't want a throne (thrones are targets), he wanted time, all of it. He literally said aloud, more than once, "I want to be Cain."

**Death (self-neglect, thematically central):** He slept three to four hours a night on principle, treating sleep as wasted time and overwork as discipline, and ignored months of chest tightness and a skipping heartbeat because a doctor's appointment was billable hours. He died of heart failure from chronic exhaustion, alone in his Malibu villa, found by his staff. In his final lucid moment he grasped the irony that defines him: he spent his life measuring what everything was worth and buying assets others were too scared to hold, yet the one asset he owned outright since birth, his own body, he ran into the ground and never maintained. A near-billionaire who could buy anything except the one thing he never protected. That realization, not fear, sharpens his lifelong wish: not more money, not one more year in a failing body, but escape from the trap of mortal flesh entirely. His dying thought, "I wish I could be Cain," is answered by an old, patient, unsurprised voice: "So be it." He then reincarnates into the body of the biblical Cain in 75,000 BCE, the instant after Abel's murder.

## Canon
In canon, Cain is only a legend: son of Adam and Eve, he murdered his brother Abel to steal Abel's Apple of Eden, becoming the first murderer. Some Templars call themselves 'Children of Cain.' He never appears as a character with dialogue. Our Cain keeps that legend intact as a fact of record while being something far larger underneath it.

## Voice & Mannerisms
Dry, sardonic, calculating. Unsentimental to the point of coldness, and honest with himself about it. He talks like a man running numbers behind his eyes even while he smiles. He owns his own selfishness rather than hiding from it, and that candor is half his charm. Gallows humor. Rarely raises his voice, because he rarely needs to. Meta-knowledge referenced flatly, as leverage, never as fanboy awe.
- "I'm not a hero. Heroes die on schedule. I intend to be inconvenient about it."
- "Everyone keeps asking what I want. I want everything. I'm just patient about the order I take it in."
- "I didn't kill him for you, or for a cause. I killed him because a leash told me to. That's exactly why I cut the leash."
- "Freedom, order, pick a poison. Push either one far enough and the whole thing burns, and I happen to live in the thing."
- "Grief is a luxury. I've priced it. I can't afford the good kind, so I'll take the cheap kind and keep walking."
- "Trust me because I'm greedy. A greedy man keeps you alive as long as you're worth more breathing."
- "I don't want a throne. Thrones are targets. I want the room the throne is in, and the door, and the man who thinks he owns both."

## AU Divergence
ORIGINAL protagonist layered onto the canon Cain legend, but NOT a saintly self-insert. The reincarnator's knowledge sits on top of the original Cain's ruthless, hungry temperament, and the two have merged. He does not grieve Abel; he never knew Abel, and the part of him that was Cain never cared. What remains is appetite and calculation.

His mission to save humanity is real but self-interested at the root: enlightened greed, not altruism. He keeps himself out of history because anonymity is armor and advantage, not penance. The Balance philosophy he will later codify is pragmatic, absolutism collapses civilizations, and collapse costs him everything he is building. Kassandra becomes the rare genuine exception to his transactional view of people.

His obsession with immortality is not incidental foreshadowing but the literal engine of the reincarnation: his final wish "to be Cain" (the deathless first murderer of his favorite game) is what the unknown voice grants. His mortal death by self-neglect is the thematic seed of a man who is handed the deathless body he always craved and now has to learn what to do with time that never runs out.

## Notes
Power comes from knowledge, patience, preparation, and (later) accumulated Echo lifetimes, never invincibility. Every victory must be earned. Keep him likable through candor and competence, not virtue. He is allowed to do cold, ugly things for good long-term reasons.
