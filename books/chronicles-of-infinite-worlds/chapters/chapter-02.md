# Clear the Road

## Outline

1. Alaric hides from two bandits and finds the child they are hunting.
2. He uses the terrain and his limited skills to defeat the searchers and rescue the child.
3. Alaric scouts the nearby camp, forms a practical plan, and clears it through traps, patience, and a stolen gun.
4. The system completes his first quest and grants its exact rewards.
5. Joel and Tess arrive during a delivery, find the aftermath, and confront Alaric.

**Arc beats advanced:** A bandit-chain quest leads Alaric into Joel and Tess's path.
**Foreshadowing planted:** Alaric's plans work best when he gathers information first; Joel notices that Alaric understands weapons.
**Foreshadowing paid off:** The Clear the Road quest and its listed rewards.

## Chapter Text

The voices were getting closer.

Alaric stayed behind the rusted car and held the folding knife against his leg so the blade would not catch the light.

One man walked along the road. The other moved through the trees to Alaric's right. They were spreading out.

That was bad. If he kept hiding, one of them would eventually see him.

He looked under the car. The road sloped down toward a drainage ditch. Thick weeds covered most of it, and a concrete pipe ran beneath the road.

Something moved inside the pipe.

A small face appeared in the darkness. A boy, perhaps nine or ten, pressed one finger to his lips.

Alaric nodded.

The boy vanished back into the pipe.

"Come on out!" the man on the road called. "We already have your bag. We won't hurt you."

His friend snorted. "Yeah. We only want to talk."

"Shut up," the first man said.

The mockery made their lie obvious. They had stolen the boy's bag, and now they wanted to kill the witness.

He picked up a loose piece of concrete and threw it toward the far side of the road.

It struck the door of an abandoned truck with a loud bang.

"There!"

The man on the road hurried toward the sound. The second man changed direction inside the trees.

Alaric moved.

He slid down the slope and crawled into the drainage pipe. The smell inside was awful. Mud soaked through his trousers, and cold water ran over his hands.

The boy had pressed himself against the wall. He held a broken bottle in both hands.

"I'm not with them," Alaric whispered.

"Stay back."

"Okay. Is there another way out?"

The boy pointed behind him. "Other side."

"Can you run?"

A quick nod.

"Then go. Stay low and don't stop until you reach the trees."

"What about you?"

"I'm going to make them follow me instead."

The boy stared at him as if that was the stupidest thing he had ever heard.

Alaric could not blame him.

"Go," he said.

The boy crawled away. A few seconds later, leaves shook at the far end of the pipe.

Boots scraped against the road above.

"Check the ditch," one of the men said.

Alaric gripped the knife tighter.

He had never stabbed anyone. He had never been in a real fight. His new body knew a little about firearms and fieldcraft, but Close Combat was still Untrained.

The system had given him a chance. It had not given him experience he had not earned.

A shadow blocked the pipe entrance.

The first bandit crouched and leaned inside. He carried a revolver in his right hand.

For half a second, neither of them moved.

Then Alaric kicked him in the face.

The man fell backward with a shout. The revolver fired into the dirt.

Alaric lunged out of the pipe and grabbed his wrist with both hands. The bandit was stronger. He slammed Alaric against the concrete wall and tried to bring the gun around.

Pain spread across Alaric's back.

**HP: 94/100**

He ignored the number and bit down on the man's hand.

The bandit screamed and loosened his grip. Alaric knocked the revolver away, but the man punched him across the cheek.

His vision flashed white.

**HP: 89/100**

The second bandit shouted from the road.

Alaric drove his knee into the first man's stomach. When the man bent forward, Alaric shoved him headfirst into the edge of the drainage pipe.

There was a hard crack.

The bandit dropped and did not move.

Alaric stared at him.

Blood ran from the man's temple into the muddy water.

"Dale?"

The second bandit was coming down the slope.

Alaric grabbed the revolver.

He rolled behind the concrete wall just as a bullet struck it. Dust hit his face.

"You little bastard!"

Alaric checked the revolver. Five chambers. One spent round. Four left.

His hands were shaking.

Alaric hated that weakness and promised himself he would handle it better next time.

Then the other man called the body Dale, and the name bothered him. It turned the dead bandit into someone who had friends and a life before this road.

Alaric looked toward the pipe. Ben had a name too. Dale had still hunted him like an animal and planned to kill him.

That ended the conflict in Alaric's mind. Dale had earned his death.

The second bandit fired again.

Alaric waited. The man had been somewhere near the top of the slope. He was angry, moving quickly, and expecting a frightened child.

A boot appeared beside the concrete wall.

Alaric fired once.

The bullet struck the man's thigh. He fell with a scream and lost his pistol in the weeds.

Alaric stood and aimed the revolver at him.

"Don't move."

The man clutched his leg. He was in his thirties, with a dirty beard and a red strip of cloth tied around one arm.

"You shot me!"

"You shot first."

"Help me, kid. I won't do anything."

Alaric looked toward the trees where the boy had escaped. "How many people are in your camp?"

The man stopped begging.

"Five? Ten?"

No answer.

Alaric moved closer and kicked the fallen pistol away.

"Put both hands on your head," Alaric said. "Do it, and I won't shoot again."

The bandit slowly raised his hands.

Alaric lowered the revolver a little.

The man's right hand dropped to his jacket. A tiny pistol appeared from inside it.

He fired.

The bullet cut across Alaric's ribs and knocked him sideways.

**HP: 80/100**

Alaric fired back.

The man went still.

For several seconds, the only sound was the water running through the ditch.

Alaric lowered the revolver.

His side burned, and blood was already soaking into his shirt. He had offered mercy, and the man had used it to shoot him.

Alaric would remember that lesson when he reached the camp.

"System, status."

**HP: 80/100**  
**Stamina: 55/100**  
**Condition: Bruised back, swollen cheek, shallow gunshot wound across the ribs**

The pain was manageable. His hands were already becoming steadier.

Gamer's Body was helping, but it did not erase what had happened.

"Can I store their weapons?"

**Yes. Items you can lift and physically possess may be placed in the inventory.**

Alaric cleaned and bandaged the graze with his first-aid supplies. Then he collected both guns. The first was an old revolver with two rounds left. The second was a worn 9mm pistol with seven rounds in the magazine. He also found a knife, a half-empty canteen, six loose revolver rounds, and an old FEDRA gas mask hanging from the second man's pack. The mask had an intact filter and no visible damage around the seal.

He stored everything except the pistol.

The boy stepped out from behind a tree.

Alaric almost pointed the gun at him. He stopped himself in time.

"I told you to run."

The boy lifted his chin. "I did. Then I came back."

"That isn't how running away works."

"Are they dead?"

Alaric looked at the ditch. "Yeah."

The boy swallowed. He did not look sorry.

"What's your name?" Alaric asked.

"Ben."

"I'm Alaric. Were you alone?"

Ben shook his head. "My aunt's hiding in a house. They shot her yesterday. We tried to get around their camp, but they saw us."

"They took our food and medicine," he added. "It was all in the bag."

"How bad is she hurt?"

"I don't know. There's a lot of blood."

Alaric put the pistol into his waistband and followed Ben.

The house was less than ten minutes away. Half of its roof had collapsed, and vines covered the front door. Ben led him through a broken window.

A woman lay behind an overturned table with a blanket wrapped around her left arm. She raised a kitchen knife when Alaric entered.

"Ben, get away from him."

"He saved me."

"He has their scarf."

Alaric looked down. He had used one of the red cloth strips to wipe mud from the pistol and forgotten it in his hand.

He dropped it at once. "I'm not with them. I have a first-aid kit. May I look at your arm?"

The woman kept the knife raised for several seconds. Then she looked at Ben and lowered it.

The bullet had passed through the flesh of her upper arm. The wound was dirty and swollen, but she could still move her fingers. Alaric cleaned it, used the last of the disinfectant, and wrapped it with fresh bandages.

Academic Knowledge was Expert. That did not make him a doctor, but it helped him remember everything he had read about wound care.

"You need antibiotics," he said.

"Do you have any?"

"No."

The woman gave a tired laugh. "Then I guess I don't need them that badly."

Ben sat beside her and leaned against her good arm. He tried to act calm, but his fingers would not let go of her sleeve.

"There's an old maintenance trail west of the road," she said. "It leads toward a farm where people trade. We were trying to reach it."

"The bandit camp watches the road?"

"Every day. Six or seven of them. More come and go."

Her face hardened. "They take children and women when they can. Men usually get shot. Don't believe anything they say if they surrender."

Alaric opened his map. It was only a blank gray field around his current position, but the places he had seen appeared as rough shapes.

"Show me where the camp is."

She traced a route with one dirty finger.

A service station stood beside the eastern road. The bandits had blocked both approaches with cars. They slept inside the shop and used the garage as storage. A lookout watched from the roof during daylight.

Alaric listened and asked simple questions. Where did they get water? When did they change guards? Did they have dogs? Had she seen infected nearby?

Ben answered the last one.

"There's a loud one in the old grocery store. They don't go near it."

"A clicker?"

Ben frowned. "What's a clicker?"

Alaric remembered that survivor names were not always the same as game terms. "The one with fungus covering its face."

"Yeah. That."

An idea formed.

It was dangerous. It also gave him a way to avoid fighting seven armed adults at once.

"You two should leave now," he said. "Use the maintenance trail."

The woman studied him. "What are you going to do?"

"Clear the road."

"You're a kid."

Alaric almost smiled. He had heard that recently.

"So is Ben. They still tried to kill him."

She did not have an answer to that.

Before they left, Ben pressed a small metal whistle into Alaric's hand.

"My uncle gave it to me," he said. "It's loud."

"You should keep it."

"I have another one."

The lie was obvious. Ben's empty pockets did not even have room for another one.

Alaric accepted it anyway. "Thanks."

Ben nodded once, then hurried after his aunt.

Alaric watched them disappear between the trees.

His first quest had become personal.

---

The grocery store was full of spores.

Alaric saw them floating in the sunlight through a broken window. Holding his breath or covering his mouth with cloth would do nothing against spores. He put on the gas mask, tightened every strap, and checked the seal before entering through the broken window.

The filter made each breath louder, but no air leaked around the edges. He crossed the dark store and found the chained delivery door.

Something struck the metal door from the other side.

Click. Click. Click.

Alaric stepped back.

"Good. You're still here."

The infected slammed into the door again.

He spent the next hour preparing the route. A trail of empty cans and broken glass led from the grocery store to the service station. He tied fishing line across two narrow gaps and piled loose metal near the road. He found an old hand cart and placed it on the slope above the station.

None of the traps would kill anyone. They were alarms and distractions.

The clicker would handle the dangerous part.

By sunset, Alaric had counted six bandits.

One watched the roof with a rifle. Two sat near a barrel fire. Another worked in the garage. A woman carried food between the shop and a small office. The last two returned from the western road with stolen bags.

Six people. Five visible firearms. The lookout was the main problem.

Alaric waited until dark.

His stomach hurt from hunger, so he ate another ration bar. His stamina rose slowly as he rested.

**HP: 84/100**  
**Stamina: 76/100**

His regeneration had restored four HP in several hours. Useful, but not enough to depend on during a fight.

The lookout climbed down from the roof to eat.

Alaric moved to the grocery store.

He took cover behind a delivery van and fired one shot through the lock.

The chain fell.

He blew the whistle as hard as he could.

The clicker burst through the door.

Alaric ran.

It followed the sound, striking shelves and walls as it charged through the dark store. Alaric blew the whistle again near the first pile of cans, then ran toward the next position.

He threw two empty cans toward the service station. They bounced across the road with a series of loud clangs while he kept the whistle in his hand.

The clicker turned toward the noise.

"What was that?" someone shouted from the station.

A flashlight swept across the road.

The clicker shrieked and ran toward the light.

Gunfire started.

Alaric stayed low behind the delivery van. He counted shots and watched the flashes.

The rifle fired first. Then two pistols. The clicker crossed the road before they could bring it down.

A man screamed.

Then a second scream came from beside the barrel fire.

Someone yelled for the others to get inside.

Alaric waited.

That was the hardest part. Every part of him wanted to move while they were confused, but he did not know who was alive or where they had gone.

The clicker made a wet choking sound. A final rifle shot ended it.

Then a bandit tripped the fishing line.

Metal crashed beside the garage.

"There's someone out there!"

Two flashlights turned toward the noise.

Alaric fired at the barrel beside the roof ladder. The bullet missed the bandit but struck metal, making him duck.

Alaric changed position at once.

The rifle fired where he had been.

He moved behind a wrecked pickup, circled left, and reached the back of the garage. The side door stood open.

The man inside was trying to load a shotgun with shaking hands.

Alaric remembered the hidden pistol and fired twice into the man's chest.

The man dropped the shotgun and fell behind a workbench. He did not move again.

The sound inside the garage hurt his ears. He took the shotgun, checked the chamber, and stored it.

Three bandits remained.

A flashlight appeared at the rear door.

Alaric ducked behind the workbench. A bullet tore through a wooden cabinet above him.

**HP: 83/100**

A splinter had cut his forehead. One point of damage.

The woman entered with a pistol held in both hands.

Alaric threw a wrench across the garage.

She turned toward the sound.

He stepped out and fired twice into her chest before she could turn back.

She fell beside the doorway.

Alaric moved on without waiting. The first fight had taught him what mercy meant to this gang.

The remaining bandits tried to escape in a truck. Alaric heard the engine struggle outside. He ran to the hand cart and cut the rope holding it.

The cart rolled down the slope, gathered speed, and crashed into the truck's front wheel as it turned onto the road.

The driver lost control. The truck struck a concrete divider and stopped.

One man crawled out through the passenger window. His left leg bent at the wrong angle.

The other stumbled from the driver's side with blood running down his face. He raised a pistol.

Alaric fired twice.

The driver dropped.

The injured passenger lifted both hands.

"I surrender."

Alaric kept the pistol aimed at him. "Throw your weapons away."

The man tossed a knife and a small revolver onto the road.

"How many more?"

"That's all. I swear."

"People come and go. Where are they?"

"Two went north this morning. They won't be back for days."

The quest window remained open in the corner of Alaric's vision.

The camp still controlled the road while one member remained willing to return to it.

"You hunted travelers," Alaric said.

"I only watched the road. I never killed anyone."

"Ben said you shot his aunt."

The man looked away.

That was enough of an answer.

Alaric thought about letting him crawl away. Then he imagined the man returning with the two bandits who had gone north, or finding a larger gang and leading them to Ben and his aunt.

He would not leave that loose end behind.

Alaric shot him once in the head.

Then he collected the discarded weapons.

The quest did not complete.

"He can't control the road like that," Alaric said.

**Correct.**

"Then what am I missing?"

**The camp remains operational.**

Alaric looked at the service station.

The bandits had supplies, barricades, weapons, and marked routes on a wall inside the office. If he simply left, the two men who had gone north could return and start again.

He spent the next hour emptying the camp.

Food, medicine, ammunition, tools, fuel, blankets, clothing, maps, and weapons vanished into his inventory. He disabled the truck, broke the water pump handle, removed the barricade chains, and burned the road maps in the barrel.

When the last crate entered his inventory, a blue window appeared.

**OPTIONAL QUEST COMPLETE: CLEAR THE ROAD**

**Objective:** Break the bandit camp's control of the eastern service road.  
**Completion Time:** 11 hours, 24 minutes

**Rewards Granted:**

- Firearms Aptitude
- 1 Endurance Point
- Basic Firearm Cache: one 9mm pistol, three 15-round magazines, 60 rounds of 9mm ammunition, one belt holster, and one pistol-cleaning kit

Warmth spread through Alaric's arms and hands. His grip shifted without conscious thought. He suddenly understood how to line up sights, control his breathing, and press a trigger without pulling the gun off target.

The system gave him safe handling and maintenance knowledge. It also made it easier for him to learn from every shot, but it did not give him experience he had not earned.

**Firearms Aptitude (Basic) acquired.**  
**Effect:** Firearms training speed increased by 25%.  
**Firearms Skill:** Novice

**Endurance: Developing, 0/10 → Developing, 1/10**

**Current HP:** 83/100  
**Current Stamina:** 44/100

The firearm cache appeared in his inventory exactly as promised.

Alaric smiled.

He had earned his first rewards. They felt good.

A second window opened.

**CHAIN QUEST UNLOCKED: ROAD HUNTER I**

**Objective:** Kill 10 hostile bandits.  
**Progress:** 6/10  
**Deadline:** None  
**Failure Penalty:** None

**Rewards:**

- 2 Physique Points
- Skill Book: Basic Close Combat (raises Close Combat from Untrained to Novice after use)
- 100 rounds of 9mm ammunition

Six. The two bandits killed by the clicker did not count.

Alaric could accept that.

He equipped the new pistol and holster. The weapon fit his hand better than the stolen one, and the magazine was full.

For the first time since waking in this world, he felt prepared.

Then a woman's voice came from behind him.

"Put the gun down."

Alaric stopped smiling.

---

He lowered the pistol and turned slowly.

A man and woman stood near the ruined gas pumps.

The woman held a handgun aimed at his chest. She was lean, dark-haired, and looked tired in the way only people who had survived too long could look tired.

Tess.

Joel stood several steps behind her with a revolver in his hand. He was younger than Alaric remembered from most of the game, but there was no mistaking him.

Alaric was so excited that he almost forgot Tess still had a gun pointed at him.

He had found them.

Or they had found him.

Joel looked at the bodies, the dead clicker, the ruined truck, and the emptied garage. Then he looked at Alaric.

"How many people are with you?" Joel asked.

"None."

Tess gave him a flat look. "Try again."

"It's only me."

"Eight dead bandits, one dead clicker, and a kid standing in the middle of it with a clean pistol," she said. "Want to explain that?"

Alaric glanced at his new weapon. The system had not added dirt or scratches.

That was going to be hard to explain.

"The pistol was in a sealed case," he said.

It was technically true.

Joel walked toward the garage without taking his eyes off Alaric. "Where are the supplies?"

"I moved them."

"Where?"

"Somewhere safe."

Joel's expression did not change, but Tess almost smiled.

"He's a terrible liar," she said.

"I noticed," Joel replied.

Alaric raised both hands. "I'm not looking for a fight."

"Good," Tess said. "Because you already had one."

She came closer and took the pistol from his hand. Then she checked his waistband, jacket, and boots for more weapons.

"FEDRA school," she said when she saw the symbol on his jacket.

"Former FEDRA school. The annex fell apart."

"How old are you?"

"Fifteen."

Tess looked at Joel.

Joel looked annoyed. "Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

Alaric had to stop himself from grinning. They sounded exactly like themselves.

That made them feel more real, not less.

Tess noticed his expression. "What's funny?"

"Nothing. Sorry."

Joel entered the garage and returned with the shotgun shell Alaric had missed under the workbench.

"You cleaned the place out," he said.

"Most of it."

"Why?"

"They were hunting a kid. His aunt was wounded. I got them out, then I made sure the camp couldn't start again."

Tess studied him for a long moment. "Where did they go?"

Alaric pointed west. "Maintenance trail. They were heading toward a farm."

"That's a real trail," Joel said.

"I know."

"How?"

"The aunt told me."

Joel's eyes moved to the pistol in Tess's hand. "And you know how to shoot?"

"Better than I did this morning."

That answer earned him another look.

Tess checked his face and saw the swelling on his cheek. "You need somewhere to sleep?"

Joel sighed.

"What?" Tess asked.

"We don't know him."

"He's fifteen."

"He's fifteen and he cleared a camp."

"Then he might be useful."

"Useful how?"

"We need someone who can be quiet," Tess said. "You make enough noise for three people. I can play the injured traveler and draw a target out. If the kid can shoot half as well as this camp suggests, he can cover me without warning the whole street."

"You're already planning jobs with him?"

Tess shrugged. "I'm considering it."

Joel looked at Alaric. "You planning to rob us?"

"No."

"Planning to follow us?"

Alaric considered lying. There was no point.

"If you're going toward Boston, yes."

Tess handed the clean pistol back to him, grip first.

Joel stared at her.

"He could've shot us before we saw him," she said.

Alaric decided not to mention that his Perception was only Competent and he had not heard either of them approach.

Tess nodded toward the road. "We're finishing a delivery. You can walk with us until morning. After that, we'll decide what to do with you."

"Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet. Joel gets mean when he's tired."

"I get mean when you collect strays," Joel said.

Tess started walking. "Come on, stray."

She took a few steps, then looked back. "What is your name, anyway?"

"Alaric. Alaric Stark."

"Tess," she said, pointing to herself. Then she nodded toward her partner. "That's Joel."

"I can introduce myself," Joel said.

Tess smiled. "You had plenty of time."

Alaric smiled too. "Nice to meet you."

Joel grunted. "We'll see."

Alaric followed her.

Joel waited until he passed, then fell in behind him where he could watch his back.

Alaric knew he was not safe with them yet. Joel did not trust him, and Tess had only offered one night.

That was fine.

He had completed his first quest, earned his first real upgrade, and met the two people he had hoped to find.

His second life was off to a very good start.
