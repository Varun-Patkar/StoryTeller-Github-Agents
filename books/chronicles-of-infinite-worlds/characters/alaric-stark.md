# Alaric Stark

## Appearance

Alaric's original body was frail after years of illness. One of his final medications damaged the pigment above his right temple, leaving a white forelock. The system restores him to health and adapts his build, clothing, and apparent background to each world, but every body remains recognizably his, including the white forelock. He carries himself with relaxed confidence and an approachable warmth that often causes strangers to underestimate how closely he is observing them.

## Personality & Traits

Kind but calculating, Alaric studies danger as a system of causes, choices, and consequences. He is disarmingly friendly, listens well, and uses dry wit when fear threatens to overwhelm a group. His compassion is genuine, but he often expresses it by making himself useful rather than admitting that he needs anyone in return.

Alaric's greatest flaw is his need to control outcomes. Knowledge of the games encourages him to treat the future as a route he can optimize, even though real people are less predictable than scripted characters. Unexpected deviations unsettle him, and a failed plan can drive him toward reckless overcorrection.

## Backstory

Alaric was born into a wealthy family and never lacked money, treatment, or material comfort. After he developed a rare blood disease, however, his parents withdrew from him emotionally. They continued paying for the best care available while directing their affection toward their other children. Confined by his health and starved of ordinary closeness, Alaric turned to games for challenge, freedom, and companionship.

He died at sixteen wishing for the chance to become better and experience the worlds he loved one last time. Instead, he awakened with a system that offered new bodies, new identities, and the power to change tragedies he once could only watch unfold.

## Motivations & Goals

- **External want:** Complete each world's main objective, save those marked for tragedy, and grow strong enough to survive increasingly dangerous settings.
- **Internal want:** Become indispensable so that people will choose to keep him close.
- **Internal need:** Accept that temporary bonds still matter and that he can be loved without earning it by solving every problem.
- **Moral boundary:** He begins determined never to sacrifice an innocent. Later worlds will force him to confront situations where every available choice harms someone.
- **Central conflict:** His urge to protect people draws him into their lives, while his control and secrecy can deny those same people the right to choose their own risks.

## Location References

Alaric has no permanent location at the beginning of the story. The system supplies a setting-appropriate identity and body whenever he enters a new world.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Alaric speaks like an intelligent teenager, not a professor or professional comedian. He uses normal words, short explanations, and occasional simple jokes. Around people he loves, he can be awkward, affectionate, annoyed, or honest. His intelligence appears through useful ideas and good decisions rather than elaborate dialogue.

> "I have a plan. It isn't perfect, but it's better than waiting here."

> "You don't owe me anything. Just stay alive, okay?"

> "The game told me what happened before I got here. It can't tell me what you'll choose now."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Alaric believes usefulness is the safest path to affection. He approaches each world as a tragedy to solve and assumes enough preparation can preserve every innocent life.
- **Key turning points:** His foreknowledge first fails because people act differently around him; saving one person creates danger elsewhere; he faces a choice with no innocent outcome; companions discover how much fear and need he hides; someone chooses to remain loyal when he has nothing useful left to offer.
- **Ending state:** Alaric accepts that no plan can guarantee a painless future and that leaving does not make a relationship meaningless. He eventually builds a found family capable of traveling between worlds, made up of people who stand beside him by choice rather than obligation.
