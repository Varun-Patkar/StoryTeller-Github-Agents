## Chapter Text

Three years passed before Anca let Coen ask again. He spent them reading at her hut, learning enough about herbs to help with simple injuries, and training whenever Pieter or Alin was willing.

Anca taught him more than she had intended at first, but she never explained everything. Some of her knowledge came from books she had found hidden at St. Tyna's abbey. She had read them alone, told one person she trusted, and lost her home when that trust failed. Coen understood why she watched him so carefully.

He kept training with Pieter too. Sword practice in the yard became harder drills, long walks with loaded baskets, and wrestling with Alin whenever his brother decided lessons had stolen enough of Coen's time.

Alin won more often than he used to.

Alin never learned why Coen visited Anca so much. No one did. Coen had kept her secret for three years.

---

At the end of spring, Anca asked him one question.

"Who have you told?"

"No one."

"Alin?"

"No."

"Your mother?"

"No."

"Has anyone asked why you spend so much time here?"

"They think I come to read and help with herbs."

"Which is true."

"Yes."

She opened the chest beneath her table.

Inside lay folded cloth, a narrow knife, a small bottle of strong spirits, and a pair of dark leather gloves. The gloves were worn but carefully mended.

Coen stared at them.

"You are still young," Anca said. "I may be making a mistake. The last person I trusted with this secret was Leonica. I lost the abbey because I told her."

"You said to ask again."

"I did. I did not promise to say yes."

"Are you saying yes?"

Anca looked at him for a long moment. "I am saying that if we do this, you listen when I tell you to stop. You do not try anything alone."

"I understand."

"Repeat it."

Coen repeated the promise.

Only then did she set his left arm on the table.

She cleaned his arm and drew the marks in dark ink. Coen tried to follow the shape, but looking at it for too long made his eyes ache.

"I learned this from books hidden beneath the abbey," Anca said. "The books never said what answers when the blood is offered. I only know that something does."

"Ready?"

He was not.

"Yes."

The knife was sharper than any blade in his father's house. The first cut burned. The second hurt worse because Coen knew what was coming.

Anca worked slowly while Coen bit down on folded leather and kept his arm still.

By the time she finished, sweat had soaked Coen's shirt. His arm throbbed from elbow to palm.

Anca covered the cuts with a thin layer of paste and wrapped them in clean cloth.

"No magic today," she said.

Coen spat out the leather. "I wasn't going to ask."

"You looked ready to ask."

She slid the gloves across the table.

"Keep the marks covered. Use the glove or a wrap. A sleeve can pull back."

Coen put the gloves into his coat.

"Thank you."

"Wait until tomorrow before thanking me."

---

The marks healed into dark red scars over the next two weeks. Anca checked them often and refused to discuss magic until the swelling was gone.

Then she took Coen beyond the village and taught him Mercurial Fervour.

She did not explain where the speed came from. She showed him what to do, corrected him when he rushed, and stopped him whenever the scars began to bleed too freely.

They walked beyond the village to a level stretch of grass between two stands of pine. Anca had chosen ground without rocks, trees, or a cliff nearby.

Coen removed the glove and bracer. Under Anca's guidance, he prepared the hex and opened a small cut near his wrist.

The world tightened around him.

"Run to the stump," Anca said. "Then stop."

The stump stood perhaps thirty paces away.

Coen ran.

His first step carried him too far. His second came before he had recovered from the first. The grass blurred beneath his boots, and the stump rushed toward him.

He tried to stop.

His feet tangled. Coen hit the ground shoulder-first, rolled once, and ended on his back with the sky spinning above him.

Anca reached him and knelt beside him.

"Do not move yet. Where does it hurt?"

"My shoulder."

She checked it carefully, then had him move his fingers and turn his head. Nothing seemed broken.

Blood continued to bead from the cut near his wrist. Anca pressed folded cloth against it and made him lie still until the spinning eased.

Anca raised his arm and waited until the bleeding slowed.

"The hex worked," Coen said.

"It did. You lost control before you reached the stump, and you gave it too much blood."

"I couldn't stop."

"Next time, ten paces. Not today."

Coen closed his eyes until the spinning stopped. His arm hurt, his shoulder would bruise, and he had used more blood than Anca intended.

He had crossed thirty paces in a few heartbeats, but his wrist still bled and his shoulder hurt whenever he moved it.
