## Outline

1. A well-off modern man dies quietly in his sleep after an ordinary evening surrounded by the stories and games he loved.
2. His soul is drawn toward innumerable bright worlds, then tears apart and falls into many of them.
3. Years later in Vale Sangora, Alin plays on a frozen river while Coen watches from the bank.
4. Coen's dormant fragment warns him an instant before the ice breaks, allowing him to save Alin.
5. The fear and instinctive surge restore the modern man's memories inside the local Coen, but no voice explains what happened and no other incarnation answers.

**Arc beats advanced:** The soul splits; Vale Sangora Coen becomes the first awakened fragment.
**Foreshadowing planted:** The many world-spheres; Coen's unexplained warning instinct; the distinction between memory awakening and collective connection.
**Foreshadowing paid off:** None.

## Chapter Text

Earth, sometime in the twenty-first century.

Coen finished his game a little after midnight.

He checked the clock, decided against starting another match, and shut the computer down. It was a work night. He still had two meetings in the morning, and being his own boss did not make either of them optional.

His apartment was quiet by the time he entered the bedroom. He turned off the lights with his phone, set the air conditioner to twenty-two degrees, and checked his alarm for seven. It was the same routine he followed most nights.

There was nothing special about his last day.

He had eaten dinner while watching an episode of a show he had been putting off. He had answered a few messages from friends, ordered a collector's edition he did not need, and spent two hours gaming. Before going to sleep, he made a note to call his mother over the weekend.

Then he lay down and died.

His heart stopped sometime before morning. There was no warning and no chance to call for help.

Coen became aware again while standing beside his bed.

For several seconds, he stared down at the body under the blanket and failed to understand what he was seeing. It looked like him. Same face, same old scar near the eyebrow, same black shirt he had worn to bed.

He tried to touch the body. His hand passed through it.

"Oh," he said, though he heard no voice.

That was when the bedroom disappeared.

Coen found himself floating in a dark space filled with enormous spheres of light. They were too large and too distant to be planets. Some looked almost normal from afar, while others gave him brief, confusing images when he focused on them.

A city of towers and colored lights.

A castle surrounded by mountains.

A battlefield where people threw fire with their hands.

He recognized pieces of them. Not enough to name every place, but enough to realize they resembled worlds from games, books, films, and television.

The idea should have excited him. Under better circumstances, it probably would have.

At that moment, he was more concerned by the fact that he was dead and apparently drifting toward one of them.

He could not control his movement. The nearest sphere pulled him in, slowly at first and then with enough force that the other lights began to blur.

Something went wrong before he reached it.

Coen felt a sharp pull from another direction. Then a second. Then dozens at once.

Pain ran through him. It did not feel like a wound because he no longer had a body to injure. It felt as if every memory and thought that made him a person had been caught on hooks and dragged apart.

He tried to hold himself together. He had no hands with which to do it.

The pressure increased.

His soul split.

Fragments flew toward different spheres. Coen saw one fall toward a land buried in snow, another toward a city burning beneath black banners, and many more disappear before he could make sense of them.

Each fragment carried part of him. Before he could see where they went, his awareness disappeared.

---

"You're scared."

Coen glared at his younger brother.

Alin stood several steps out on the frozen river with his arms spread for balance. He was nine and missing one of his front teeth.

"Am not."

"Are too."

"Shut up."

Alin laughed and slid farther from the bank.

The river below Laslea was not particularly wide. In summer, their father could throw a stone across it, though he usually needed two attempts and refused to count the first. Winter covered the water with thick white ice, and the village children had crossed it more than once without trouble.

Coen still did not like it. The ice looked solid, but dark water moved beneath it, visible through patches where the snow had been scraped away.

"Come on!"

"No. Get back."

Alin stuck out his tongue. "Chicken."

Their bundle of branches lay beside the path. Coen considered dragging Alin back by the collar. Let him complain. At least they would be home.

"Wait," Coen said.

Alin bent his knees and pushed off.

"Alin, wait."

His brother pushed toward the middle of the river.

Something struck Coen from inside.

For an instant, he saw Alin beneath black water. His brother's hands beat against the ice while the current carried him away.

Coen moved before the image vanished. He snatched the longest branch from their bundle and ran onto the river.

"Coen?"

The crack came a moment later.

A black line spread beneath Alin's boots. The ice broke, dropping him into the water.

Coen was already close. He threw himself flat and shoved the branch across the hole.

"Grab it!"

Alin surfaced with a gasp. His hands struck the branch and slid along the wet bark.

Coen caught his wrist.

Cold water closed over his hand, but his grip did not loosen. Something inside him surged again. His fingers locked around Alin's arm, and strength ran through his shoulders hard enough to hurt.

He pulled.

Alin came out of the river coughing and kicking. Coen dragged him away from the broken ice until they reached the snow-covered bank. Then Alin curled on his side, shaking so badly that his teeth knocked together.

Coen dropped beside him and checked that he was breathing. He was. His eyes were open too.

Alin coughed, spat water onto the snow, and began to cry.

"I want Mama."

"We're going home."

Coen's whole body had begun to shake, though he had barely touched the water.

He looked back at the hole and saw the other ending again. Alin trapped below the ice. A body recovered near sunset. His mother collapsing beside the door.

The fear became too much, and a second life forced its way into his head.

He remembered electric lights and tall buildings. He remembered going to school, starting a business, arguing with his mother over the phone, and spending far too much money on games. He remembered cars, hospitals, the internet, and enough useless passwords to make his head hurt.

He also remembered going to sleep in his apartment and never waking up.

The memories arrived all at once. Coen turned away from Alin and vomited into the snow.

"Are you sick?" Alin asked.

Coen could not explain it. He did not understand it himself.

He knew he was Coen, son of Pieter and Esme, born in Laslea. He remembered his sisters, his brothers, and every winter he had spent in the valley. He also knew he had lived another life in a world where Vale Sangora was the setting of a game.

That memory brought several names with it.

Brencis. The Vrakhiri. The Blood Mass. Anca. Dawnwalker.

He knew what would happen to his family.

He remembered the game well. He had been a fan, the sort who read lore entries after finishing quests and then went online to see what the other choices changed. The important parts were still clear: the people, the major quests, the powers, and the different endings. What time had taken were smaller things such as exact conversations, minor quest steps, and names he had only seen once.

Alin's death was one of those details. Coen remembered a frozen river and a buried childhood memory. In the game, Alin had died here.

Coen looked at his brother. Alin was soaked, frightened, and very much alive.

The strange warning had changed what should have happened.

They left the branches and hurried home. Alin could barely walk by the time they reached the house, so Coen carried him the last part of the way.

Esme pulled Alin inside, stripped off his wet clothes, and wrapped him in two blankets beside the stove. Pieter shouted at both boys until his voice cracked, then sat down and held Alin against his chest. Yanna brought hot water while keeping Mirto and Lunka back. The two little ones were too young to understand why everyone was frightened.

By evening, Alin was warm enough to complain about the taste of the broth.

No floating screen appeared. There was no system, no reward, and no voice explaining why he remembered another life. He felt exactly as weak and cold as he had that morning.

He had broad knowledge rather than a perfect record, but the warning had given him enough time to save Alin.

Coen thought about the future he remembered. In the game, Alin's death had been the first blow that broke his mother. The plague and Brencis's takeover had pushed her further inward until she barely spoke, ate, or left her bed. That future had already changed. Esme was shaken now, but she was still the strong woman Coen knew.

The other dangers remained. The plague would reach Vale Sangora. Lunka would catch it, and Brencis would cure her with vampire blood before taking the family. Much later, after Coen fought his way into Greifberg Castle, Brencis would turn her into a vrakhir out of spite.

He could not recall the exact number of years before those events, and every change would make the smaller details less reliable. Waiting for the story to begin would still be stupid.

Across the room, Alin had fallen asleep against Pieter. Esme sat beside them, tired but smiling whenever she checked her son's breathing.

In his old world, they had been characters in a game. Looking at them now, he hated remembering them that way. They were his family, and for once, all of them were safe.

Coen needed to keep them that way.

He could not stop Brencis as he was now. Remembering the future did not make him stronger, and whatever had moved through him on the river was already gone. When the time came, he could not repeat the game's endings by storming the castle and giving Brencis time to hurt Lunka. He would need a quieter way inside and enough power to kill the vampire before an alarm was raised.

Anca knew witchcraft. She had years to teach him before the plague reached Vale Sangora.

Coen watched the fire and began deciding what he would ask her first.
