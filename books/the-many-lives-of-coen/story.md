# The Many Lives of Coen

## Synopsis

A modern media fan dies quietly, but his soul tears apart while crossing into the worlds he loved. When the first Coen awakens in Vale Sangora and becomes a Dawnwalker, his scattered lives begin to synchronize, turning separate boys shaped by separate worlds into the first facets of a growing multiversal being.

## Tone & Style

Accessible webnovel prose with clear action, direct emotional reactions, and concrete stakes. The narration stays close to one Coen at a time and lets each world's upbringing shape his habits, priorities, and voice. Dark events retain their weight, but the story does not manufacture suspicion or misery when relief, affection, competence, or wonder is earned.

The story reveals its multiversal rules through consequences. There are no status screens, quests, levels, or system messages. Coen was a serious fan of the worlds he loved and remembers their major stories, characters, lore, powers, and endings. Time has blurred exact dialogue, minor routes, incidental names, and other minutiae. Canon knowledge becomes less reliable as he changes events.

Dialogue is localized into simple modern English for readability rather than written as literal medieval speech. Children use short, ordinary words and unpolished reactions. Avoid dialect terms tied to the British Isles, modern specialist language in local speech, and clever banter that assumes education they do not have.

## Notes

- Canon spine: *The Blood of Dawnwalker* game continuity; *The Witcher* books followed by CD Projekt Red's game continuity.
- Every incarnation is named Coen. They share an origin but are complete people shaped by different childhoods and worlds.
- In the Council, incarnations use world or nature based identifiers: Dawnwalker Coen, Witcher Coen, Cyber Coen, and equivalent names for later members. In their own worlds, each is simply Coen.
- Dawnwalker Coen is the first awakened incarnation and initially leads the Council because he is the strongest and earliest connected facet.
- Witcher Coen is a thirteen-year-old street orphan and one of Ciri's childhood playmates. Their relationship grows into the story's sole romance much later. There is no harem.
- Coen may prevent canon tragedies, including Ciri's time with the Rats, but should not erase her agency or replace her as the center of her own conflicts.
- Saving Alin prevents the first loss behind Esme's canon withdrawal. She remains traumatized by later events but active, physically capable, and involved with her family rather than becoming the weak, nearly catatonic woman from the game opening.
- Lunka catches plague and is cured by Brencis's blood; that cure is not her turning. In canon she remains herself throughout captivity and sends letters about Greifberg Castle, then Brencis turns her near the finale. Coen's planned divergence is a covert infiltration and assassination using her intelligence, denying Brencis the warning and time needed to retaliate against her.
- No Coen reveals future knowledge, reincarnation, or the existence of the collective to anyone outside the collective. He may lie, omit, or give ordinary reasons for informed choices. Ciri is the only possible exception, and that disclosure can happen only much later after deep trust and direct multiversal events make continued secrecy unreasonable.
- Candidate later worlds include *Cyberpunk*, followed by a setting with broader magic such as Marvel. These remain uncommitted until their role in the progression is clear.
