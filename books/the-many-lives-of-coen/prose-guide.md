# Prose Guide

This file records binding prose and characterization decisions for *The Many Lives of Coen*. Read it before drafting or revising any chapter, then review the complete chapter against it.

## Voice

- Use direct, accessible webnovel prose similar in pacing to mainstream translated fanfiction: immediate orientation, plain exposition, functional paragraphs, clear reactions, and quick forward movement.
- Do not imitate copyrighted wording from reference chapters.
- Avoid literary narration, trailer-like fragments, tidy narrator slogans, forced profundity, and paragraphs arranged only to sound dramatic.
- Do not make prose artificially terse to avoid sounding generated. Natural connective language is better than clipped notes.
- Keep serial momentum high. Compress repetitive training, travel, and preparation into selected consequential scenes rather than giving every incremental step its own chapter.
- Childhood ends in Chapter 5. Dawnwalker Coen is turned and the collective first connects in Chapter 8. Do not delay these milestones with additional setup chapters.

## Dialogue

- Every response must logically answer the exact question or command before it.
- Use complete sentences when natural. Short fragments belong to fear, urgency, stubborn children, or established habits.
- Coen and Alin are unschooled village children in the opening. They do not trade polished jokes, analyze supernatural timing, or speak like skilled comics.
- Alin is nine at the river. He boasts simply, cries after nearly drowning, asks for Mama, and does not recognize or discuss Coen's precognition.
- Mirto and Lunka are very young during the river incident. They do not understand it and will not retain a meaningful memory of canon Alin.
- Use neutral localized English. `Mama` is appropriate; British regional terms such as `Mam` are not. Familiar localized taunts such as `Chicken` are allowed when they sound natural in English.
- Anca is not a hard schoolmistress. During reading lessons she wants Coen to infer meaning from grammar, sentence structure, and known roots. She gives clues, accepts reasonable guesses, and corrects without humiliation.
- Anca hated the strict teacher and institutional discipline at St. Tyna's abbey. She may sometimes imitate that clipped severity by habit or under stress, but the resemblance is ironic and should prompt her to soften, rephrase, or guide with a question.

## Coen's Knowledge And Secrecy

- Coen was a serious fan. He remembers major stories, people, lore, powers, world rules, and endings across the media he loved.
- He remembers minutiae hazily: exact dialogue, dates, minor quest steps, routes, and incidental names.
- No Coen tells anyone about future knowledge, reincarnation, or the collective. He uses ordinary motives and plausible lies. Ciri is the sole possible exception, much later.
- Do not place planning-only explanations in chapter prose. Keep future-route logic, source justification, and mechanical conclusions in research or plot files until the scene needs them.

## Character And Canon Corrections

- Coen's dormant fragment warns him before the ice breaks. He saves Alin; extreme fear unlocks the modern memories.
- Alin survives. Do not restore his death for drama.
- Esme's canon withdrawal begins with Alin's death and worsens through plague and occupation. With Alin alive, she remains active and physically capable, though later events can traumatize her.
- Lunka catches plague and is cured with Brencis's blood. This is not her turning. She remains herself during captivity and is turned by Brencis only near the finale.
- The planned Vale Sangora solution is covert infiltration and assassination, not another loud castle assault. Keep that in planning until it becomes relevant on page.
- There is no system interface.
- Vale Sangora witchcraft begins with one foundational scar pattern that acts as the conduit. Individual hexes are then learned and prepared through it; do not carve a separate foundational rune for every hex.
- Casting draws blood or health. The conduit scars are dangerous evidence and must be hidden with long sleeves, wraps, bracers, or gloves.
- Latin is ordinary scholarship, not the language of hexes. Magical glyphs are tied to older Uriashi traditions and sacred stones. Do not call them Latin or invent a named universal magical language without canon evidence.

## Practical Plausibility

- Modern Coen shuts down his gaming computer before sleeping.
- Check everyday object use, injuries, movement, and cause-and-effect before saving a draft.
- Do not invent arbitrary nervous gestures merely to signal hidden intuition.
