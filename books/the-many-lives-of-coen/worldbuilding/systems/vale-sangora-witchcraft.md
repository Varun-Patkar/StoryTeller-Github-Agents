## Overview

Vale Sangora witchcraft uses a foundational pattern scarred into the practitioner's body. This pattern acts as the gate or conduit for magic. It is not a separate full carving for every spell.

## Rules & Limitations

1. **Foundational conduit:** The basic scar pattern is carved first and remains visible. Accuracy matters because it establishes the practitioner's connection to witchcraft.
2. **Learned hexes:** Individual hexes are learned from instruction, manuals, and ancient glyph sites after the conduit exists. A practitioner can prepare or equip selected hexes through the same foundational scars.
3. **Blood cost:** Casting draws the practitioner's blood or health through the conduit. A stronger or mishandled working can take more than intended.
4. **Concealment:** The scars identify forbidden witchcraft. Practitioners hide them beneath long sleeves, cloth wraps, bracers, or gloves, including during hot weather and physical work.
5. **Legal danger:** Discovery by hostile clergy or the Inquisition can mean imprisonment, torture, or execution.
6. **Dawnwalker limit:** After Coen becomes a Dawnwalker, his night-form regeneration heals the scars and blocks witchcraft until his human form restores the conduit.

## Language And Glyphs

Latin is not the language of hexes. Anca teaches it for ordinary literacy, disciplined reading, and access to human scholarship. Witchcraft itself uses older magical glyphs strongly associated with Uriashi traditions, sacred stones, and their practice of inscribing power. The available canon does not establish one confirmed name for a universal magical language, so the story should not invent one.

## Training Sequence

Anca requires disciplined literacy, separate study of magical glyphs, practical knowledge of bleeding and pain, and proven discretion before she will carve the foundational gate. Later hex acquisition does not require repeating the full scar pattern.
