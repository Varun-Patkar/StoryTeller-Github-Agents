## Overview

Vale Sangora witchcraft begins with permanent marks cut into the practitioner's body. The same marks support different hexes; they are not fully recarved for every spell.

The craft's deeper source is deliberately uncertain. Its signs appear in Uriashi traditions, while its effects and imagery point toward Anachora. The sleeping entity within Anachora may be the source of the power and the vrakhiri may be its spawn, but the story should reveal that connection through events rather than explain it as settled fact.

## Rules & Limitations

1. **Permanent marks:** The first scars remain visible and must be hidden.
2. **Learned hexes:** Different hexes can be used through the same marks. The exact process should remain mysterious unless a scene requires it.
3. **Blood cost:** Casting draws the practitioner's blood or health through the conduit. A stronger or mishandled working can take more than intended.
4. **Concealment:** The scars identify forbidden witchcraft. Practitioners hide them beneath long sleeves, cloth wraps, bracers, or gloves, including during hot weather and physical work.
5. **Legal danger:** Discovery by hostile clergy or the Inquisition can mean imprisonment, torture, or execution.
6. **Dawnwalker limit:** After Coen becomes a Dawnwalker, his night-form regeneration heals the scars and blocks witchcraft until his human form restores the conduit.

## Language And Glyphs

Latin is not the language of hexes. Witchcraft uses older signs associated with Uriashi traditions and other forbidden sources, but the available canon does not establish one universal magical language. Avoid detailed invented grammar or mechanical explanations.

## Training Sequence

Anca requires patience, practical knowledge of wounds, and proven discretion before she will mark a student. Later hexes do not require repeating the full scar pattern.
