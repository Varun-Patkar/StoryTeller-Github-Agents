# Chapter 63: A Song to Come Back To

July 2033.
Crystal Falls.

Joel was tuning the guitar on the back porch when Ellie stopped in the doorway and pretended she had only come out for the cooler air.

He glanced at her, then back at the strings. "You need somethin'?"

"Yeah," she said. "A lesson."

That made him look up.

Ellie stood with her hands in her pockets and her knife somewhere on her, same as always. She had that guarded, ready posture like the world might rush her if she stood wrong. But she was here. That counted for something.

"You askin' me to teach you guitar?"

"Unless you got a better hobby."

"I got plenty of better hobbies." He held the instrument out. "Sit down."

She took the chair beside him and watched his hands while he showed her where to put her fingers. First chord, simple enough. Ellie tried it, missed it, and made a face at the fretboard like the guitar had lied to her.

"Your fingers are too stiff," Joel said.

"It's a stupid guitar."

"It's a guitar."

"Same thing."

He almost laughed. Instead he moved her hand, one finger at a time, until the chord rang clean.

Ellie frowned at the sound, then tried not to smile. It didn't work.

Joel caught himself staring at her long enough to notice the same thing Sarah used to do when she was small and stubborn and sure she could beat the problem by sheer force of will. Ellie had the same look now. Different face. Same fight.

"There," he said. "You hear that?"

"I hear that you made it do what you wanted."

"That's the point."

They worked through the next chord, then the next. Ellie kept muttering under her breath whenever she missed one, but she didn't quit. She adjusted faster than most grown men he'd taught. Not because it came easy. Because she hated losing to a thing twice.

After a few minutes she looked down at the strings and said, too casual, "Sarah said you used to play when she was little."

Joel kept his face on the guitar. "Yeah."

"Teach me the lullaby," Ellie said.

That one landed quieter than it should've.

He looked at her. She met his eyes for half a second, then looked away first, like she'd already said more than she meant to. "The one she knows," she added. "The soft one."

"That song was hers first," Joel said.

Ellie shrugged. "Then it can be mine second."

He sat with that for a beat, thumb resting on the strings.

Sarah had sung it when she was small, voice thin and sleepy from a bad dream or a fever or just being tired of the dark. Joel had never thought much about teaching it to anybody else. He'd just kept it because it was hers, and because some habits stayed even when everything else broke.

Now Ellie was looking at him like it was the most natural thing in the world to ask.

So he played.

Slow at first. Barely more than a line of notes. Ellie listened, then leaned in when he repeated it. The tune was simple, the kind of thing a kid could remember half-asleep and still hum the next morning.

Lay your head down, little one.

Dark will pass and morning comes.

Hold the blanket, close your eyes.

Safe inside till daylight rises.

Ellie mouthed the words once, then again, testing the shape of them.

"That all it is?" she asked.

"That all it needs to be."

"Sarah had better taste in music when she was a kid, I hope."

Joel snorted. "She told me I sang like a dying goat."

Ellie barked a laugh at that, sudden and real.

He played the line again and watched her shoulders loosen a little as she found the rhythm. Not relaxed. Not safe. Just less braced. Enough to breathe around.

On the next pass she got the chord change right on her own.

Joel nodded once. "There you go."

Ellie glanced at him, suspicious of praise the way she was suspicious of fresh food. "Don't get all proud. I still hate it."

"Good. Means you're payin' attention."

She rolled her eyes, but the corner of her mouth kept trying to turn up.

Outside, the watch lights moved along the fence. Somewhere in the lodge, somebody laughed, then the sound faded into the normal evening noise of dishes and footsteps and a door shutting. The world was still the world. Still ugly. Still dangerous.

Inside the porch light, Joel taught Ellie the song a second time.

By the end she could carry the first part on her own if he backed her with the lower chords.

She let the last note hang, then looked down at her hands like they had surprised her.

"Again tomorrow," she said.

Joel set his thumb on the strings and gave her a small nod. "Yeah. Tomorrow too."

Ellie stayed there another minute, guitar warm against her leg, like she was deciding whether to say something else. Then she didn't. She just took the guitar back with careful hands, like it mattered, and stood up to go inside.

At the door she paused and looked over her shoulder.

"Hey, Joel?"

"Yeah?"

"You ain't terrible at this."

He huffed a quiet laugh. "High praise."

She disappeared inside before he could see the smile she was probably trying not to wear.

Joel sat on the porch a while longer with the tune still in his hands and Sarah's voice in his memory, and for the first time since Ellie showed up, the shape of her beside him felt less like a stranger's burden and more like somebody he'd be there to teach again tomorrow.
