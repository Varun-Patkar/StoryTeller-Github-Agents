# Chapter 17: What Hides in the Dark

## — Marcus —

The town didn't have a name anymore.

Whatever sign had been posted at the town limits was gone. Blown over, fallen down, or ripped off by someone who figured the name didn't matter now that the place was empty. What was left was a main street with a feed store, a grain elevator, a post office the size of a shed, and a church with a steeple shorter than the one they'd slept in last night. Six buildings total, if you counted the gas station at the far end.

Six red dots on the map. All Level 1 Runners. Spread across the feed store and the post office, two and four.

"Same as Buckner," Marcus said. "I go in, clear them out. Shouldn't take long."

Joel checked the SIG. "I'll take the post office."

"I've got it."

"I know you've got it. I'm takin' the post office." Joel looked at him. The look from this morning. Not suspicious. Something else. "Two buildings, two people. Faster."

Tommy set up on the grain elevator's loading platform with the Remington. Clear sight lines down the main street in both directions. Sarah stayed with him, binoculars in hand, journal in her back pocket.

Marcus took the feed store. Two Runners. They were standing in the back near the loading dock, facing the wall, doing the slow rocking sway that meant they hadn't spotted prey in a while. Dormant mode. Easy.

He came in through the side door. Knife in his right hand, the hunting knife he'd bought on Day 1 that now felt like an extension of his arm. The first Runner didn't turn until he was three feet away. He put the knife through the base of its skull and lowered the body to the floor. The second heard the thump and charged. Marcus sidestepped, caught it by the collar of what used to be a flannel shirt, and drove the blade in behind the ear.

**[Runner killed. +8 points]**
**[Runner killed. +9 points]**
**[Points: 725]**

Two down. He wiped the blade on his jeans and moved to the front of the store to watch through the window.

Across the street, Joel came out of the post office. He rolled his shoulders once. The SIG was holstered. He hadn't fired a shot either. He held up four fingers.

Marcus held up two.

**[Runner killed (x4, Joel Miller assist). +38 points]**
**[Streak 6: +12 bonus points]**
**[Nest Breaker (Town): +18 bonus points]**
**[Points: 793]**

Seven hundred and ninety-three. Fifty-eight points over the settlement starter kit threshold.

Marcus stood in the doorway of the feed store and stared at the number. He'd been chasing 735 for days. Through the rest stop and Gerald's death and four days of empty road with a clean map and no way to earn. And now, in the time it took to clear two buildings in a town with no name, he'd passed it.

He opened the Mall. Found the category. Infrastructure, Tier 2, Settlement Packages.

**[Settlement Starter Kit — 735 points]**
**[Includes: Generator (diesel, 10kW), basic tool set (30+ tools), 500 ft lumber (mixed), 200 lb hardware (nails, screws, bolts, hinges), water filtration unit (gravity-fed, 50 gal/day), 10 rolls fencing wire, first aid station supplies, 2 solar panels (100W each), seed collection (temperate climate vegetables)]**

He bought it.

The items didn't materialize all at once. They never did. The system deposited them in a loose pile behind the feed store, out of sight from the road. Lumber stacked. Hardware in canvas bags. The generator sitting on the dirt like it had always been there. The solar panels leaned against the wall, still in foam packaging that shouldn't exist in this world.

**[Settlement Starter Kit purchased. -735 points]**
**[Points: 58]**
**[Achievement Unlocked: Foundation Layer — "You bought the future. Now build it."]**

Fifty-eight points. Almost broke. Worth it.

Tommy came down from the grain elevator. He looked at the pile of materials behind the feed store. Then he looked at Marcus. Then back at the pile.

"Well," Tommy said. "That's somethin'."

"We'll need to cache it. Bury or cover anything we can't carry. Come back with a vehicle once we're set up."

"Marcus." Tommy walked around the lumber stack. Touched the generator. Ran his hand over the solar panels. "This is real."

"It's always real."

"I know it's real. I mean this is it. This is a settlement. In a pile behind a feed store in the middle of nowhere." He shook his head. Not disbelief. Awe, maybe. Or the thing that came right before planning. "We need a truck."

"We need a lot of things. But this is the start."

Joel walked over. He looked at the pile the same way he looked at most impossible things Marcus produced. Brief assessment. Practical calculation. No questions about how, only about what next.

"Generator's diesel," Joel said. "We'll need fuel."

"Mall has jerry cans. But I'm down to fifty-eight points."

"So we earn more."

Marcus almost smiled. Joel said "we" about the points now. Like it was a joint account.

They spent an hour covering the cache with tarps and brush. Tommy marked the GPS coordinates in the atlas, penciling a small X at the town's location with a note: SK-1. Settlement Kit, cache one. Sarah helped drag tarp corners over the lumber. She didn't say anything about the materials appearing from nothing. She'd stopped being surprised by that weeks ago.

...

They left the town heading northwest. The road narrowed to a county two-lane that cut through rolling rangeland. Fence posts on both sides, wire long since sagged or cut. The wind had died and the afternoon was warm for October. Almost pleasant. Almost normal.

Marcus was thinking about fuel calculations when the map pinged.

Not the soft chime of a Runner detection. A different sound. Sharper. Like a bell struck once and muted. He'd never heard it before.

He pulled up the display. Five contacts. Two hundred yards ahead, clustered inside a large structure set back from the road. But they weren't red like Runners. They were orange.

He stopped walking.

"What?" Joel said. He'd learned to read Marcus's sudden stops.

"Contacts ahead. Five of them. Inside a building." Marcus paused. "They're a different color on my map."

"Meanin' what?"

"Meaning they're not Runners."

The building was a school. Or it had been. A single-story brick structure with a flat roof, half of which had collapsed inward. A sign out front read BUCKHORN CONSOLIDATED SCHOOL, HOME OF THE MUSTANGS. The letters were faded. The sign was cracked down the middle. A swing set in the side yard had rusted into abstract sculpture.

Marcus studied the map. The five orange dots weren't standing still. Runners stood still when dormant. They rocked in place. These contacts were moving. Slow, deliberate patterns. One would drift to a corner, pause, then drift back. Another was making a circuit of what looked like a hallway. Patrolling.

"They're moving with purpose," Marcus said. "Runners don't do that."

Tommy unslung the Remington. "Stalkers?"

The word landed like a cold stone. Marcus had read about them. Played against them. Stage 2 infected. Weeks to months into the transformation. Beginning fungal growth but still with enough brain function to think. Not well. Not like a person. But enough to hide, wait, and ambush. They were the infected equivalent of a predator rather than a mindless herd animal.

He'd killed dozens of Runners. He'd killed a Clicker with Joel in Buckner. But he'd never faced a Stalker. And in the games, Stalkers were the enemies he'd hated most. Not because they were the strongest. Because they cheated. They hid behind walls and waited for you to walk past. They crouched in dark corners and didn't move until you were looking the other way. They made you feel safe, and then they took it away.

"Stalkers," Marcus confirmed. "Five of them."

"We go around," Joel said. Flat. Not a suggestion.

"At fifteen to twenty-five points each, plus first-kill bonuses, plus a nest clear..." Marcus did the math. "That's a hundred fifty to two hundred points. Maybe more."

"Points ain't worth dyin' over."

"I need the points, Joel. I just spent everything on the settlement kit. If we hit trouble between here and Colorado, I can't buy us out of it. I can't buy food. I can't buy ammo. I can't buy medicine."

Joel was quiet. He looked at the school. Looked at Marcus. Looked at the school again.

"Tommy stays with Sarah," Joel said. "You and me."

"Joel..."

"You said you need the points. I'm not lettin' you go in there alone." He pulled the SIG. Checked the magazine. Twelve rounds. "We go slow. We go quiet. First sign of bad, we pull back."

Marcus nodded. He bought a flashlight from the Mall. Five points. Down to 53. The cheapest one in the catalog, barely brighter than a candle, but Stalkers liked the dark.

Tommy took up position in the schoolyard behind a concrete planter with clear sight lines to the main entrance and two visible windows. Sarah was beside him, small and still.

"Twenty minutes," Marcus said. "If we're not out in twenty minutes..."

"I'll come get you," Tommy said. No hesitation.

Joel walked toward the school's front entrance. The double doors were hanging open, one on its hinges and one flat on the ground. Beyond them, a hallway disappeared into darkness. Floor tiles cracked and heaved by roots growing up through the foundation. Ceiling panels sagging. Water damage everywhere, black mold climbing the walls in fractal patterns.

Marcus checked the map one more time. Five orange dots. Two in what looked like a gymnasium on the left side of the building. Two in classrooms along the main hall. One in the back, near what the floor plan suggested was a cafeteria or kitchen.

"Two left, two center, one back," Marcus whispered. He pointed. Joel nodded.

They stepped inside.

---

The smell hit first.

Not the sharp rot of fresh death. Something older. Fungal. A wet, earthy stink like a basement that had been flooded and never dried, mixed with something sweet and wrong. Cordyceps. Marcus had smelled it on Runners. Faint, like body odor with a chemical edge. This was different. Thicker. The air tasted like mushrooms left in a bag for a week.

The hallway stretched forty feet to a T-intersection. Lockers on both sides, some hanging open, some dented shut. A water fountain on the wall, dry, with a fungal growth sprouting from the drain like a tiny coral reef. Children's artwork still pinned to a bulletin board. A crayon drawing of a house with a yellow sun and stick figures holding hands.

Marcus moved left. Joel followed two steps behind, covering the rear. Their footsteps were barely audible on the cracked tile.

Marcus checked the map. The two center contacts had moved. One was in a classroom ten feet ahead on the right. The other had drifted back toward the T-intersection. Both stationary now.

He held up one finger. Pointed right.

Joel moved to the left side of the hallway. Marcus took the right. They approached the classroom door. It was closed. A small window in the door, wire-reinforced glass, mostly intact but smeared with something dark.

Marcus leaned forward to look through.

The classroom was trashed. Desks overturned. A teacher's desk against the far wall with papers scattered across it like confetti. Bookshelves toppled. And in the corner, crouched behind an overturned filing cabinet, something that used to be a person.

It was watching the door.

Marcus pulled back. His heart was doing something aggressive in his chest. Runners didn't watch. Runners stood and swayed and charged when they heard you. This thing was crouched, still, with its head tilted at an angle that said it was listening. Waiting. The fungal growth on its face was patchy, clusters of white and grey covering the left eye and spreading across the forehead. One eye was still human. Brown. Wide open.

"It's behind the cabinet," Marcus breathed. "Watching the door."

Joel processed that in about half a second. "Bait."

"What?"

"It's bait. You open that door, you're lookin' at it. Which means you're not lookin' behind you."

Marcus checked the map. The second center contact. It had moved again. It was in the hallway now. Behind them. Fifteen feet back, pressed against the lockers.

His stomach dropped.

He turned slowly. The flashlight beam swept the hallway.

Nothing. Lockers. Floor tiles. The bulletin board with the crayon house.

But the map showed it right there. Against the lockers. Fifteen feet away. Not moving.

Marcus pointed the flashlight directly at where the dot was. The beam caught the edge of an open locker door. Behind it, in the shadow, something shifted. Just barely. A slight movement of pale skin and fungal growth, adjusting its position the way a person would when they realized they'd been seen.

Then it charged.

No warning sound. No screaming. Runners screamed when they charged, a raw, torn-throat shriek that told you exactly where they were and how far away. This thing came at him silent. Fast. Faster than a Runner. It covered fifteen feet in two seconds and hit him at the waist.

Marcus went down. His back hit the tile floor and the flashlight spun out of his hand, throwing wild shadows up the walls. The Stalker was on top of him, one hand grabbing his jacket, the other swinging for his face with fingers that had started to fuse together from the fungal growth. It didn't scream. It hissed. A wet, ragged sound like air pushed through a clogged pipe.

He caught its wrist. The skin was wrong under his fingers. Not skin. Something between skin and bark, rough and cool and slightly damp. The Stalker pushed down with the weight of a full-grown man plus whatever mass the fungal growth added, and Marcus's arm shook with the effort of holding it back.

Joel's boot caught it in the ribs.

The Stalker rolled sideways. Joel was on it before it could recover, the SIG pressed against the side of its head. He fired once. The sound in the enclosed hallway was enormous. Marcus's ears rang.

**[Stalker killed (Joel Miller). +22 points]**
**[First Stalker Kill bonus: +15 points]**

The classroom door banged open.

The second Stalker came out low, on all fours, scuttling like something that had forgotten how legs worked and invented a new way. It crossed the hallway in a blur and hit Joel from the side. Joel staggered into the lockers. The SIG clattered on the floor.

Marcus grabbed his knife and got up. The Stalker had Joel pinned against the lockers, both hands around his forearm, teeth snapping at his face. Joel had his free arm braced against its chest, pushing. The muscles in his neck stood out like cables.

Marcus drove the knife into the Stalker's back, aiming for the spine. The blade sank in three inches and hit something hard. Bone? Fungal plate? He didn't know. He pulled it out and stabbed again, higher, hitting the neck this time. The Stalker released Joel and turned on Marcus, swinging wildly. He stepped back. It lunged.

Joel picked up the SIG and shot it twice. Once in the shoulder, which barely slowed it. Once in the head.

**[Stalker killed (Marcus Webb + Joel Miller). +18 points]**

They stood in the hallway, breathing hard. Marcus's back ached where he'd hit the floor. Joel had scratches on his forearm where the Stalker's fused fingers had scraped through his jacket sleeve. Not deep. Not a bite. But close.

"You good?" Marcus asked.

Joel checked his arm. Rolled up the sleeve. Three shallow scratches, beading blood. Skin, not bite.

"Good," Joel said.

Three contacts left on the map. Two in the gymnasium. One in the kitchen.

"We can leave," Marcus said. "Pull back. Two kills is still points."

Joel looked at the dead Stalker on the floor. Looked at the hallway ahead. Looked at Marcus.

"Gymnasium's that way?" He pointed left, toward the T-intersection.

"Yeah."

Joel picked up the flashlight from where it had rolled against the wall. Checked the SIG. Nine rounds left.

"Then let's finish it."

---

The gymnasium was worse.

The roof had collapsed on the far end, letting in daylight through a jagged hole. But the near end was intact and dark. Bleachers folded against the wall. A basketball hoop hanging by one bolt. The floor was warped from water damage, buckled into ridges and valleys. Fungal growth covered the collapsed section like moss on a log, thick and pale, with fruiting bodies the size of fists pushing up through the debris.

Two orange dots on the map. Both on the far side, near the collapse. Not moving.

Marcus and Joel entered through a side door that opened onto the bleacher section. They moved along the wall, staying in the shadow of the folded bleachers. The air was worse in here. Dense with spores. Marcus pulled his shirt up over his nose. Joel did the same with his bandana.

Twenty feet in, Marcus's flashlight caught movement.

Not where the dots were. Closer. Much closer. Against the bleacher frame, three feet to his left.

He swung the light.

A face. Half-covered in fungal growth. One human eye staring at him from behind a bleacher support strut.

It had been there the whole time. They'd walked right past it.

The map updated. The contact had been invisible. Hidden behind the metal structure, its signature masked by the dense fungal growth on the walls. A sixth Stalker that hadn't shown on the initial scan.

It launched itself from behind the strut.

Marcus caught it with an elbow. Pure instinct, not skill. His elbow connected with its jaw and something cracked. The Stalker stumbled sideways. Marcus followed with the knife, driving it into the thing's chest. It grabbed his arm with both hands and pulled, trying to drag his wrist toward its mouth. Its jaw worked in a chewing motion. Mechanical. Repetitive.

Joel couldn't shoot. Marcus was too close. Instead, he stepped in and grabbed the Stalker by the back of its neck and wrenched it sideways, breaking Marcus's arm free. Marcus stabbed it again. Neck. The thing made a gurgling sound and went limp.

**[Stalker killed. +20 points]**
**[Hidden Enemy bonus: +10 points]**

"Six," Marcus panted. "There were six. The map only showed five."

"How many more it ain't showin'?" Joel asked.

Good question. Bad question. The kind of question that made you want to leave immediately.

The two remaining dots on the map chose that moment to stop being still.

They came from the collapsed end of the gym. Fast. Together. Coordinated in a way Runners never were. One went left along the wall. The other came straight up the middle of the warped floor.

Joel took the one on the left. Three shots, controlled. The first two hit center mass and the Stalker barely slowed. Fungal armor on the torso, thicker than the others. The third shot caught it in the throat and it went down hard, sliding on the wet floor.

The one coming up the middle reached Marcus before he could set his feet. It hit him like a linebacker. They went through the side door together, tumbling into the hallway. Marcus lost the knife. The Stalker was on top of him, and this one was bigger than the others. Heavier. The fungal growth covered most of its head, leaving only a strip of grey skin around the mouth, which was open and snapping.

Marcus got both hands around its throat. Pushed. Its mouth was six inches from his face. Four inches. The fungal smell was overwhelming, sweet and rotten, and he could see individual spore clusters on the growth around its eyes, tiny and pale like grains of rice.

Then Joel was there. He didn't shoot. He drove the hunting knife, Marcus's knife, the one that had fallen in the doorway, into the Stalker's temple. All the way to the hilt.

The thing went rigid. Then it went heavy. Two hundred pounds of dead weight collapsed onto Marcus's chest.

Joel pulled it off him. Grabbed Marcus's arm and hauled him to his feet.

**[Stalker killed (Joel Miller). +19 points]**
**[Stalker killed (Marcus Webb + Joel Miller). +23 points]**
**[Streak 6 (Stalkers): +18 bonus points]**
**[Nest Breaker (Buckhorn School): +25 bonus points]**
**[New Enemy Class Discovered: Stalker — +30 bonus points]**
**[Points: 258]**

Marcus leaned against the hallway wall. His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From the leftover cocktail of whatever the body dumps into your blood when something tries to eat your face.

Joel leaned on the opposite wall. He was breathing hard. The bandana had slipped down around his neck. There was a scratch on his cheek that he hadn't had before.

Neither of them said anything for a while. The hallway was quiet. The map was clean. Just two green dots. Them.

"I hate schools," Marcus said.

Joel looked at him. And then Joel laughed.

Not a big laugh. Not a movie laugh. A short, rough exhale through his nose with the corner of his mouth turned up. The kind of laugh that happened when you'd almost died and the person next to you said something stupid and your body decided that was funny because the alternative was shaking apart.

"Let's get out of here," Joel said.

They walked out into the sunlight. Tommy was already moving toward them, Remington up, face tight. Sarah was behind the planter, binoculars raised.

"We heard shots," Tommy said. "You okay?"

"Stalkers," Joel said. "Six of 'em."

Tommy looked at Joel's scratches. Looked at Marcus's torn jacket and the grime and fungal residue on his clothes. His jaw worked once.

"Six Stalkers. In a school." Tommy lowered the Remington. "You two are either brave or stupid."

"Both," Marcus said. "Definitely both."

## — Joel —

They made camp a mile past the school, in a drainage culvert under a county road. Not comfortable. Not scenic. But it had concrete walls and two exits and Joel could see the road in both directions from the east opening.

He sat on the culvert's edge and cleaned the scratches on his forearm with water from the canteen. The skin was red but unbroken in any meaningful way. Not a bite. Not even close to a bite. He'd checked four times and he'd check again before he slept.

Inside the culvert, Tommy was building a small fire from dead brush and fence posts. Sarah was sorting the food supplies. Marcus was sitting against the wall with his eyes closed and his hands resting on his knees. He'd been quiet since the school.

Joel watched him.

The kid, and he was a kid in some ways that Joel couldn't quite define, had taken a beating in that hallway. The first Stalker had put him on the ground. The last one had nearly gotten his face. In between, he'd killed two of them with a knife and helped kill two more. He'd been scared the entire time. Joel had seen it in his eyes, in the way his hands moved, in the set of his jaw.

But he'd stayed.

That word again. The one Sarah had used by the fire last night. The one that Joel had been turning over in his head like a stone in his pocket, wearing it smooth.

Marcus stayed. When the Stalker tackled Joel into the lockers, Marcus didn't run. He stepped forward with a knife and put it in the thing's back. When the sixth one appeared from behind the bleachers, a contact that wasn't even on his map, Marcus fought it hand to hand while Joel repositioned. When the last one dragged him through a doorway, Marcus held it by the throat with both hands and waited for help to arrive.

Joel had fought alongside other people. Tommy, in the early years. Tess, in Boston. People he trusted to cover his blind side and not run when things got ugly. It took months, sometimes years, to build that kind of trust. The kind where you could put your back to someone and know, really know, that they wouldn't leave.

Marcus had earned it in twenty minutes in a school that smelled like death.

Not because Marcus was a better fighter than Joel. He wasn't. Joel had seen better, and Joel had been better. Marcus's form was rough. He telegraphed his swings. He didn't protect his center when he committed to a stab. In a straight fight, Joel would put him down in ten seconds.

But Marcus didn't quit. And Marcus didn't hesitate when Joel was the one in trouble. That mattered more than form. More than skill. In the places where things went bad, the only question that counted was whether the person beside you would still be there when you turned around.

Joel cleaned the knife. Marcus's knife, the one he'd picked up off the hallway floor and driven through a Stalker's temple. There was still fungal residue on the blade, grey-green and flaking. He wiped it down with a rag and set it on the concrete beside him.

Sarah brought him a water bottle and sat down next to him. She didn't say anything for a while. Just sat and watched the road and the sky going orange at the horizon.

"He's okay?" she asked. Meaning Marcus.

"Bruised. Tired. Not bit."

"Are you okay?"

Joel held up his forearm. The scratches were already scabbing. "Nothin'."

Sarah studied his face. That look she had. The one that was too old for twelve. The one that saw through things.

"You went in there with him," she said.

"Somebody had to."

"You could've said no. You could've made him go around."

Joel didn't respond. She was right. He could have. The smart play was always to avoid unnecessary risk. Tommy would have backed him. They could have walked a quarter mile around the school and never known what was inside.

But Marcus had said he needed the points. And Joel had said "you and me" without thinking about it, the way you'd offer to help someone carry a heavy box, because the job needed two people and he was standing right there.

"I owed him," Joel said. Quiet. Not looking at Sarah.

"For what?"

Joel did look at her then. At the scar on her hip that her shirt covered. At the girl who was walking and running and arguing and writing in her journal because a stranger had shown up at the worst moment of the worst night and refused to leave.

"For you," Joel said.

Sarah's expression shifted. Something moved behind her eyes. Not surprise. Recognition. She'd been waiting for him to say it.

"Okay," she said.

She leaned against his shoulder. Joel put his arm around her. They sat like that while the sky turned from orange to purple and the first stars appeared.

## — Marcus —

The numbers were good.

**[Points: 258]**
**[Distance traveled: 222 miles]**
**[Distance remaining: ~578 miles]**

Two hundred and fifty-eight points. Not rich. But not broke anymore. The settlement kit was cached back in the nameless town, buried under tarps and brush, waiting for them to come back with a vehicle. And the Stalker kills had opened a new line in the Mall.

**[New items unlocked: Stalker-class equipment]**
**[Night vision goggles (basic) — 180 points]**
**[Reinforced body armor (torso) — 220 points]**
**[Combat helmet (ballistic) — 150 points]**

Serious gear. Not affordable yet, not without gutting his reserves, but visible on the horizon. The system was telling him something. You've faced the dark. Here's what you need to go back in.

He closed the display and looked at the fire.

Tommy was asleep, the Remington across his chest. Sarah was writing in her journal, her pen scratching softly in the quiet. Joel was at the east end of the culvert, first watch, silhouette dark against the last light.

Marcus's body hurt in a dozen places. His back from hitting the floor. His elbow from the bleacher fight. His ribs from the last Stalker's tackle. His hands from gripping a throat hard enough to hold death at arm's length for the ten seconds it took Joel to cross the room.

But he was alive. Joel was alive. Nobody was bitten.

And something had changed between them in that school. Not a grand moment. Not a speech or a confession or a handshake. Just the quiet fact that they'd walked into something terrible together and walked out together, and neither of them had left when leaving was an option.

Marcus had saved Sarah on Outbreak Day. That was why Joel tolerated him.

But today, in a collapsed school full of things that hunted in the dark, they'd saved each other. And that was different. Tolerance wasn't trust. Tolerance was a wall you could see through. Trust was the wall coming down.

Sarah closed her journal. She looked at Marcus.

"How bad?" she asked.

"I'll be sore tomorrow. Nothing's broken."

"You smell like a compost heap."

"That's the Stalker residue. Apparently they secrete something."

"Gross." Sarah wrinkled her nose. Then, softer: "Dad said he owed you. For me."

Marcus didn't know what to do with that. So he defaulted to honesty. "He doesn't owe me anything."

"I know. But he thinks he does. And that's why he went in there with you today." She paused. "He's not going to say it in words. That's not how he works. But he went into a building full of monsters because you needed points, and that's the Joel Miller version of a thank-you card."

Marcus almost laughed. "That's a hell of a thank-you card."

"He's a hell of a guy." Sarah tucked the journal under her arm. "Get some sleep. You look terrible."

"Thanks."

"You're welcome."

She walked to her sleeping bag and lay down near Tommy. Within minutes, her breathing was steady.

Marcus sat by the dying fire. The pain in his ribs was a dull, warm throb. The wind outside the culvert made a low sound, like someone blowing across a bottle.

Five hundred and seventy-eight miles to Colorado. Three weeks, maybe less if they found a vehicle. The settlement kit was cached. The points would come. The Stalkers were a new threat, but they were also a new resource. The system rewarded growth. Every new enemy type unlocked new gear, new bonuses, new possibilities.

And Joel had called the points "ours."

Marcus closed his eyes. The fire popped once and went quiet.

Tomorrow. More road. More miles. More of whatever was waiting out there in the dark.

But not alone.
