# Chapter 55: Room 204

## — Marcus —

**Day 30. Boston QZ perimeter. 11:14 PM.**

They left the farmhouse at dusk.

Rivera's directions were precise. South on Route 9 for two miles, then east through a residential neighborhood that wasn't residential anymore. The houses had been stripped years ago. No doors, no windows, no siding on some of them. Just frames standing in rows.

Joel moved ahead. Old habit. He liked to read the terrain before Marcus's map could tell him what was there. Twenty years of partnership and Joel still wanted his own eyes on things first.

The QZ wall appeared through the tree line at 10:30. Concrete barriers topped with razor wire, streaked with rust and water stains. Graffiti layered over years on the outside. A Firefly symbol. "FEDRA LIES" in red paint. "R.I.P. BOSTON" in white, half-faded.

Marcus listened. Two heartbeats on the wall, two hundred yards north. A patrol. Walking west, away from their position. No contacts to the south.

"Patrol's moving away," Marcus said. "Four minutes."

Joel nodded. His breathing had already shifted. Slow, controlled, each inhale measured. Combat breathing. The body preparing for whatever came next, even if next was just a hole in a wall.

They moved along the base of the barrier. The parking garage was three stories of bare concrete, its ramps visible through missing wall panels. Behind it, the breach. Water damage had cracked the foundation and the panels above had shifted, leaving a gap about three feet wide at the base.

Marcus went first. If something waited on the other side, he could take a bullet. Joel couldn't.

The gap was tight. Concrete scraped his jacket on both sides. A piece of rebar snagged his pack strap. He freed it and kept moving.

Inside the Boston QZ.

The map updated. No contacts within two hundred yards. The nearest infected cluster was underground, six blocks northwest. Dormant. Human signatures scattered through residential buildings to the south. Sleeping. The war paused for the night.

Joel came through behind him. Stood. Looked around.

"Huh," he said.

---

## — Joel —

The QZ looked like every other failed one, except bigger.

Narrow streets between brick apartment buildings. Trash piled against walls in drifts. A car burned to its frame sat in the middle of an intersection. Lampposts without lights. Power lines sagging between poles, useless.

But people lived here. Laundry hung from windows on the second floor. A generator hummed somewhere below street level. The smell of cooking drifted from a basement apartment. Something starchy and bland. A handwritten sign on a door read "Dr. Kim — Tuesdays and Thursdays — No Ration Card Required."

This was the part outsiders didn't see. Not the fighting, not the patrols. Just people eating bad food in bad buildings, trying to get through. The same thing Crystal Falls had been in the first year. The same thing everywhere was, underneath the flags and the guns.

Joel kept his hand off his pistol. Two strangers with weapons in a QZ at midnight would draw attention he didn't want. He walked like he belonged. Head up, pace steady. A man going somewhere specific and not interesting enough to stop.

Six blocks.

They turned right on a street Marcus identified through whatever he did. The buildings shifted from residential to commercial. A boarded-up laundromat. A barbershop with the pole still standing. A corner store with "RATIONS INSIDE — FEDRA AUTHORIZED" stenciled on plywood.

Then the school.

Three-story brick, set back from the street behind chain-link. Institutional. The kind of place that had been ugly before the outbreak and hadn't improved since. Two of four floodlights still working at the front entrance. A FEDRA flag hung from the second-floor railing.

Joel counted the guards. Two at the front entrance. Both sitting. One had his rifle across his lap. The other was reading something by flashlight. Not soldiers. Babysitters with guns.

"Loading dock?" Joel whispered.

"South side. Two more. Both stationary." Marcus paused. "One's asleep."

"Fire escape?"

"East side. No guards. No lights."

They circled the block. The east face backed onto an alley between the school and an apartment building. The fire escape was old iron, bolted to the brick. Ladder down, landings at each floor.

Joel tested the bottom rung. Solid. Didn't creak. Someone maintained this. Probably the older kids, sneaking out after lights-out. Same thing kids did in every institutional setup Joel had ever seen.

He went up first. One rung at a time, slow. The metal was cold against his palms. His knees complained on the vertical ladder and he told them to shut up.

Second floor landing. A door. Joel tried the handle. Locked. He looked at Marcus, who'd come up behind him without a sound. A two-hundred-pound man climbing an iron ladder like it was carpeted stairs.

Marcus pulled out a pick set. Worked the lock in about eight seconds. The door opened inward.

Dark hallway. Linoleum floor. Fluorescent lights off. Classroom doors on both sides, all closed. The smell of industrial cleaner and sweat. A school smell. Same everywhere, apparently, even after the end of the world.

Marcus pointed left. East wing.

They walked the hallway. Joel checked each door as they passed. Not opening them. Just looking. Behind each one, kids were sleeping. Sixty kids, ages ten to eighteen. Orphans, most of them. Wards of a state that could barely keep the lights on.

Room 201. 202. 203.

204.

This door was different. A padlock on the outside. A biohazard sticker, peeling at the edges. A clipboard hanging from a nail with two columns: DATE and STATUS. The last entry, in blue pen: "Day 24 — No symptoms. Physician notified."

Twenty-four days. She'd been locked in a room for twenty-four days while people checked twice daily to see if she'd turned into a monster.

She hadn't.

Joel looked at the clipboard. Looked at Marcus. Marcus was already working the padlock.

Three seconds. Click.

Marcus eased the door open.

---

## — Marcus —

The room was small. Ten by ten, maybe less. A cot against the wall, a metal desk, a chair. A window with iron bars and a view of the alley. One overhead light, off. Moonlight through the bars threw a ladder pattern across the floor.

A small figure under a thin blanket, turned toward the wall.

Marcus listened. Heartbeat fast. Too fast for sleep. She was awake. Had been awake before they opened the door. Probably heard the padlock.

"Ellie," Marcus said. Quiet. Not a whisper. Whispers sound like threats. Just low and clear.

The figure on the cot went still. Heartbeat spiked.

Then she moved. Rolling off the cot and onto her feet in one motion, putting the desk between herself and the door. A switchblade appeared in her left hand, open, the blade catching moonlight.

Left-handed. He'd been right about that.

She was small. Shorter than he'd expected. Red-brown hair pulled back in a messy ponytail. Freckles across her nose and cheeks. Green eyes, wide but steady. She wore a faded T-shirt and cargo pants, both too big. QZ-issue. A bite mark on her right forearm, healed to a rough scar.

She looked at Marcus. Then at Joel in the doorway behind him. Two grown men in her room at midnight, one of them armed.

"Who the fuck are you?" she said.

Her voice was hoarse. The kind of hoarse you get from not talking to anyone for three weeks.

"My name is Marcus," he said. Hands visible. No weapon. "This is Joel. We're not FEDRA. We're not Fireflies. We're not going to hurt you."

"Oh, cool. That's exactly what every guy breaking into a girl's room at midnight says." She adjusted her grip on the switchblade. "Try again."

"We came from Colorado. A settlement called Crystal Falls. We're here because you're in danger, and the people who are supposed to be protecting you are planning something you don't know about."

"Colorado." Ellie's eyes narrowed. "You walked from Colorado."

"Drove part of it. Walked the rest."

"To Boston."

"To this room."

She processed that. The switchblade didn't move. Scared, but holding.

"That's a lot of effort for one kid," she said. "What do you want from me?"

"Nothing."

"Bullshit." The word came out flat. Practiced. "Everybody wants something. FEDRA wants me to stay in this room until I turn. The nurses want me to stop asking questions. Marlene wants..." She stopped. "How do you know my name?"

"We know about the bite," Marcus said. "We know you're immune. We know the Fireflies know, and we know what they're planning to do about it."

The room was quiet. Somewhere below them, a guard coughed.

"What are they planning?" Ellie asked. Her voice was different now. Smaller.

Marcus looked at Joel. Joel looked at the girl. A fourteen-year-old with a knife, standing behind a desk in a locked room, asking a question she already half-knew the answer to.

"The Firefly doctors want to study your immunity," Marcus said. "The growth is in your brain. To get to it, they'd need to remove it. The procedure would kill you. Marlene is trying to get you out of this QZ and to a hospital in Salt Lake City. She hasn't told you what happens when you get there."

Ellie didn't say anything. Five seconds. Ten. The switchblade lowered an inch.

"That's..." She swallowed. "That's a big claim from a stranger."

"It is."

"You could be lying."

"I could be."

"You could be FEDRA trying to get me to run so they can shoot me in the hall."

"We came through the fire escape. If we were FEDRA, we'd have used the front door."

Ellie looked at the window, the fire escape beyond the bars. Then back at Marcus. Then at Joel, who still hadn't spoken since the hallway. He was leaning against the doorframe, watching her the way he watched everything. Careful. Quiet.

"What's his deal?" Ellie asked, nodding at Joel. "He looks like someone's angry grandpa."

Joel blinked.

Marcus kept his expression neutral. "He's the reason we're here. He's the one who said we should come."

"He came all the way from Colorado and he's not gonna say anything?"

Joel straightened. Cleared his throat. This wasn't his strength. Talking to scared teenagers he'd never met. Explaining things with words instead of actions. He'd been better at this with Sarah, but Sarah had been his daughter. He'd had context. History. Here he had nothing but the truth.

"I've got a daughter," Joel said. "She's about twenty years older than you. When she was twelve, she got shot. By a soldier. Worst night of my life. A stranger showed up out of nowhere and saved her. Didn't ask for anythin'. Didn't explain himself. Just showed up and stayed."

Ellie watched him. The knife was at her side now. Not put away. Just not pointed at anyone.

"I'm here to do the same thing for you," Joel said. "That's it. No tricks. No plan that ends with you gettin' hurt. Just two people who came a long way because you shouldn't be locked in this room, and you sure as hell shouldn't be goin' to Salt Lake City."

The guard below coughed again. A door opened and closed somewhere on the first floor.

Marcus checked his system interface. 12:03 AM. They had maybe forty minutes before the morning check on Room 204.

"We need to go, Ellie," Marcus said. "There's a breach in the east wall. We can be outside the QZ in fifteen minutes. After that, it's your call. You want to come with us, we'll take you somewhere safe. You want to go your own way, we won't stop you. But you can't stay here."

"Why not?"

"Because in a few days, Marlene's going to send someone for you. A smuggler. They'll tell you they're taking you somewhere to help make a cure. And they are. They just won't mention the part where you die."

Ellie looked at the bite mark on her arm. The scar that should have killed her and didn't.

"I didn't ask for this," she said. Not to Marcus. Not to Joel. Just into the room.

"I know," Joel said.

She looked at him. Whatever she was looking for, she found enough of it. Or maybe she just didn't have a better option. A locked room, a scar that made no sense, and two strangers offering a door.

"Fine." She folded the switchblade and slid it into her back pocket. "But if this is bullshit, I'm stabbing both of you."

"Fair," Marcus said.

Ellie crouched and pulled a backpack from under the cot. Already packed. She'd been ready to run for a while. Just hadn't had anywhere to run to.

She shouldered it and turned to Marcus. "There's a girl down the hall. Sofia. Room 208. Her brother asked you to get her too, right?"

Marcus paused. "How do you know about that?"

"I don't. But the only person who's visited me in three weeks is a kid named Sofia who slides notes under my door because she thinks quarantine means I'm lonely, not dying. She told me her brother Danny was out there somewhere, trying to find a way to get her. If you're getting me out, she comes too."

"She comes too," Marcus said.

"Good." Ellie moved toward the door, then stopped. "And dude? Her room doesn't have a padlock. So maybe try the door handle like a normal person."

---

## — Joel —

Sofia was asleep.

Ellie opened the door without knocking. The room was identical to 204, minus the biohazard sticker and the padlock. A twelve-year-old girl with dark hair curled on the cot, a stuffed rabbit tucked against her chest.

"Sof." Ellie crouched beside the cot. "Wake up. We gotta go."

Sofia opened her eyes. Blinked. Saw Ellie, then the two men in the doorway. Her hand went to the rabbit.

"It's okay," Ellie said. "These guys are from outside. They're taking us out of here."

"Danny?" Sofia said. One word. Her brother's name. The only thing that mattered.

"He's waiting for you," Marcus said from the doorway. "At a safe house, a few hours west."

Sofia was off the cot in seconds. Shoes from under the bed, a jacket from the chair, and the rabbit. Nothing else. She'd been waiting too.

They moved down the hallway. Ellie led. She knew the school better than Rivera's map could show. Which floorboards creaked. Which doors stuck. Where the guards' footsteps echoed from.

At the fire escape door, Ellie held up a hand.

"Guard smokes out here sometimes," she whispered. "Check the landing."

Marcus listened. No heartbeat. No smoke. Just cold metal and night air.

"Clear."

Down the ladder. Sofia first, then Ellie, then Marcus, then Joel. Joel went last because that's where the threat came from. Someone follows, someone notices the open doors, the empty rooms. The rear man handles it.

Nobody came.

The alley was dark. They moved east toward the wall. Ellie walked beside Marcus, backpack straps gripped in both hands, her eyes everywhere. She'd never been outside the school grounds at night. Joel could tell from the way she looked at the buildings. Not scared. Taking it in. Cataloguing.

She reminded him of Sarah. Not the personality. The attention. The way she filed information without being told to.

They reached the breach. Marcus went through first, then Sofia, then Ellie. Joel brought up the rear, checking behind one last time.

Nothing. The QZ slept. Two girls who shouldn't have been in that building were outside it, and nobody had noticed.

On the other side of the wall, Ellie stopped. Put her hands on her knees. Three long breaths. Then she straightened.

"Okay," she said. "That happened."

"That happened," Marcus said.

"So. Colorado."

"Colorado."

"How far is that?"

"About eighteen hundred miles."

Ellie stared at him. "Dude."

"We'll take it slow."

"Eighteen hundred miles is not 'taking it slow.' That's literally the most insane thing anyone has ever said to me, and yesterday a doctor told me I should be dead." She adjusted her backpack. "You guys are out of your minds."

Joel shifted his pack on his shoulder. "Welcome to the group."

---

## — Marcus —

They walked west through the Massachusetts night. Sofia stayed close to Marcus's left side, clutching the rabbit, not speaking. Ellie walked ahead of both of them, then beside Joel, then behind Joel, unable to pick a position. Restless. Alert. Her head turning at every sound.

The suburbs thinned. Houses gave way to trees. The road opened up and the sky opened with it.

Ellie stopped walking.

"Holy shit," she said, looking up. "There's so many."

Stars. Thousands of them. No floodlights, no QZ wall glow, no generator hum to blur them. Just the sky, wide and full and cold.

Joel glanced up. The same stars Sarah used to name from her bedroom ceiling in Austin. The ones he could never remember.

"Yeah," Joel said. "There are."

Ellie stood there for a few seconds, neck craned back, mouth slightly open. Then she shook her head, pulled a face like she'd been caught doing something embarrassing, and started walking again.

"Whatever. Stars. Cool. Not like I've been staring at a ceiling for three weeks or anything."

Nobody called her on it. Some moments you let a person have without commentary.

...

The farmhouse appeared at 3:17 AM. Dark windows, the rooster weather vane a black silhouette against the grey sky. Marcus heard three heartbeats inside. Rivera. Danny. And a third that hadn't been there before, faster and lighter. Danny was pacing.

Marcus knocked three times. The same pattern Joel had used that afternoon.

The door opened. Danny stood there, taller than Marcus remembered from the afternoon, or maybe just standing straighter. His eyes went past Marcus, past Joel, past Ellie, and found Sofia.

Sofia let go of the rabbit.

She ran. Danny caught her in the doorway, dropping to one knee, arms wrapping around her. He didn't say anything. Neither did she. The rabbit lay on the floorboards between them.

Ellie stood on the porch step and watched. She had her hands in her jacket pockets and an expression Marcus couldn't quite read. Not jealousy. Not sadness. Something quieter. Like watching a thing she'd heard described but never seen up close.

Joel saw it too. Marcus could tell from the way Joel's eyes moved from the siblings to Ellie and stayed there.

Rivera appeared behind Danny. She looked at Ellie, then at Marcus.

"Room 204?" she asked.

"Room 204," Marcus said.

Rivera nodded. Stepped aside to let them in.

The system updated in Marcus's peripheral vision.

**[QUEST: Prepare for the Retrieval]**
**Sub-objectives:**

- ✅ Establish intelligence network in eastern corridor
- ✅ Map route to Boston QZ
- ✅ Identify local contact for QZ infiltration
- ✅ Confirm target location
- ✅ Extract Ellie Williams
- ◻ Secure safe departure from Boston metropolitan area
- ◻ Return to Crystal Falls

Five of seven.

Inside, Rivera boiled water on the camp stove. Danny and Sofia sat in the back bedroom with the door closed, their voices low and constant. Ellie sat at the kitchen table, feet on the chair, knees drawn up. She was looking at the maps taped to the walls. The hand-drawn QZ layout. The route markers. The blue and red annotations.

"You planned this," she said. "Not today. Not last week. This took months."

"Years," Marcus said.

Ellie pulled the switchblade from her pocket. Opened it. Closed it. Opened it again. The clicking sound was the only noise in the kitchen besides the stove.

"Why me?" she asked. "There are sixty kids in that school. Half of them would kill for someone to show up and get them out. Why me?"

Marcus sat across from her. The table was the same one Rivera had spread the map on that afternoon. Same nicks in the wood. Same coffee ring stains.

"Because you're immune, and the people who know about it will use you up. FEDRA would study you in a lab. The Fireflies would put you on a table and not let you get off it. You deserve better than being someone's science project."

"Deserve," Ellie said. She tested the word like she wasn't sure it applied to her.

Joel sat down on the bench by the door. He didn't join the conversation. He pulled out his canteen, drank, and set it on his knee. His presence was enough. A third person in the room who wasn't asking for anything or explaining anything. Just sitting.

Ellie looked at him. Then at Marcus.

"Eighteen hundred miles," she said.

"Give or take."

"And your settlement. Crystal Falls. It's real?"

"Six hundred and thirty-seven people. Hydroelectric dam. Running water. Electric lights. A school that doesn't lock kids in rooms."

Ellie opened the switchblade. Closed it. One more time.

"I want my own room," she said. "With a door that locks from the inside."

"Done," Marcus said.

"And a bookshelf."

Joel looked up.

"Done," Marcus said again.

Ellie put the knife away. She pulled her backpack onto her lap and unzipped the front pocket. Inside, worn and dog-eared, a copy of Savage Starlight Issue #4. She held it for a second, then zipped it back up.

"Okay," she said. "Let's go to Colorado."

Outside, the first grey of predawn touched the eastern sky. The rooster weather vane turned slowly in a wind that smelled like pine and coming rain. Eighteen hundred miles west, Sarah was waking up in a house with an east-facing window, starting another day of running a federation that didn't need its founder to function.

And in a farmhouse kitchen in Massachusetts, a girl who'd spent three weeks waiting to die was eating a granola bar Rivera had offered and asking Joel if his knees were going to make it eighteen hundred miles.

"They'll make it," Joel said.

"They better. I'm not carrying you."

"Nobody asked you to."

"Good. Because I wouldn't."

Joel almost smiled. Almost.

Marcus leaned against the wall and let them talk. The system interface was quiet. No alerts. No new quests. Just five checkmarks and two empty boxes and the long road home.

Eighteen hundred miles. A girl with a switchblade. A man with bad knees and a good heart.

Time to go.
