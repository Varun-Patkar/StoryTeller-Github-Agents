# Chapter 43: The Horde

## — Marcus —

The Snowmass resort sat fifteen miles northwest of Crystal Falls, sprawled across a mountainside like a small town that had tried to look expensive and half-succeeded. Condominiums with cracked balconies. A lodge with a peaked roof and ski racks still bolted to the walls. Restaurants with outdoor patios where nobody would sit again. All of it buried under eight years of mountain weather and fungal growth.

Marcus reached the outskirts by mid-morning. He'd left the dam at dawn, running the first twelve miles on the highway and the last three through forest where the road had collapsed under a landslide. Late April in the Rockies. Snow on the peaks, mud in the valleys, sky so blue it looked painted.

He opened the system map at the tree line and stopped walking.

The Carbondale church had shown nineteen contacts. A tight cluster in a single building. An anomaly that had raised questions.

Snowmass showed sixty-three.

They were spread across the resort complex in a pattern Marcus recognized immediately. Not random. Not drifting. Concentrated in three buildings with a clear center of gravity: the main lodge at the heart of the resort. The same radial distribution he'd seen in Carbondale. Infected arranged around a focal point like iron filings around a magnet.

Another Beacon.

Marcus zoomed the map. The contacts in the lodge were packed tight, maybe twenty in the building itself. The rest fanned out through the condos and restaurant buildings in concentric rings. Sentries.

This one had been here longer. The Carbondale Beacon had been growing for months. But Kate Schultz said her people had been losing runners from Snowmass since 2019. Two years of a Beacon pulling infected from the surrounding mountains, building its congregation, broadcasting its signal across the valley.

Sixty-three contacts inside his map range. How many more had been walking toward the signal from beyond it?

Marcus checked the handgun. Fourteen rounds plus one in the chamber. Not enough for sixty-three. Not even close.

But the Beacon was the priority. Kill the signal source, the horde scatters. Same principle as Carbondale. Just more of everything.

He circled the resort from the north, staying above the tree line. The lodge had a back entrance through a kitchen loading dock. If he reached the Beacon fast enough, the rest was cleanup.

Two hundred yards from the loading dock, the Beacon's pulse changed.

The frequency jumped. A sudden shift, like a switch thrown. The breathing inside the buildings changed with it. Shuffling feet. Bodies turning. Sixty-three contacts reorienting at once.

It knew he was here.

Marcus ran.

He hit the loading dock at full sprint, crashed through a screen door hanging by one hinge, and entered the lodge kitchen. Three Runners stood among the stainless steel prep tables. They turned toward him as one unit.

Knife through the first. Handgun to the second's forehead. Sidestep, blade across the third's throat and up into the skull. Three seconds.

**[+39 Points]**

The main hall was a large open space with high ceilings and picture windows overlooking the mountain. Ski memorabilia on the walls. A stone fireplace the size of a truck. And behind the reception desk, consuming the entire back wall, the Beacon.

Bigger than Carbondale. Much bigger. Mycelium roots spread across the ceiling and down both adjacent walls, thick ropes of fungal tissue branching through the drywall and into the structural studs. The host body was unrecognizable. A torso and a head, everything else absorbed. The fungal plates covering the skull were dense and pocked with spore openings, each one exhaling in slow rhythm.

Seventeen Runners stood in the hall. Already moving toward the kitchen door.

Marcus charged through them. Knife and gun, the geometry of close-quarters work burned into his muscles over eight years of clearing. Kill, step, kill, step. Enhanced speed let him move between them faster than the Beacon's pulse could coordinate a response. Twelve seconds. Seventeen dead.

**[+221 Points]**
**[Streak: 20]**

He reached the Beacon and drove the knife in. The fungal mass shuddered. The pulse stuttered, spiked, and died.

**[Beacon Killed]**
**[+380 Points]**
**[Nest Cleared: Snowmass Resort]**
**[Bonus: +65 Points]**

Quiet. Spore haze drifting in grey light. Twenty bodies on the lodge floor. A brochure rack near the door still held pamphlets for ski lessons. Forty dollars a session. Group rates available.

Marcus checked the map.

His stomach dropped.

The remaining forty-three contacts in the surrounding buildings were moving. Outward, scattering without the Beacon's hold. But that wasn't the problem.

The problem was south. Beyond the resort perimeter. Beyond anything he'd counted.

New dots streaming into his map range from the north, the east, the northwest. Infected that had been in transit toward the Beacon's signal for days or weeks, walking through the mountains, still coming even though the signal had stopped. Without a destination, they followed the terrain. Downhill. South. Through the valley toward the Crystal River corridor.

Toward Crystal Falls.

Marcus counted the dots as they entered range.

A hundred. Two hundred. Two hundred and sixty, and still growing.

He turned and ran.

---

Fifteen miles in under ninety minutes. His boots hit the Crystal Falls access road at quarter past noon, lungs burning even through Tier 4 conditioning. He'd been sprinting at a pace that would have killed a normal person.

Joel was at the gate with Keiko, reviewing patrol reports. A thermos of chicory on the post between them.

"We've got a problem," Marcus said.

Joel looked at his face and set the thermos down.

## — Joel —

The alarm bell rang three times. Long, short, long. Helen's signal for general assembly. Joel had drilled the pattern into her two years ago.

Within four minutes, every adult in Crystal Falls was either at their station or heading there.

Joel stood on the gate platform and watched. People moving with purpose, not panic. Mothers carrying children toward the powerhouse. Militia members jogging to the armory, boots on gravel. Frank handing out spare rifles from the workshop rack. Tommy emerging from the turbine room, wiping his hands on his jeans, reading the situation in two seconds flat.

"How many?" Tommy asked, climbing up beside him.

"Marcus says two sixty on his map an hour ago. Probably more by now."

Tommy was quiet for a moment. He looked north up the road. The valley stretched ahead, narrow, forested, the access road threading between the creek and the mountainside. A bottleneck. That's why they'd picked this place. A bottleneck was only good if you could hold it.

"Where's Marcus?"

"Talkin' to Keiko. He'll be up front."

Tommy nodded. Nobody questioned that.

The militia assembled in twelve minutes. Twenty-eight trained members, plus six volunteers who'd been through basic drills but never fired under pressure. Joel would have preferred the full thirty-two, but Mateo's patrol was eight miles south and there was no time to recall them.

Joel placed them in three lines.

First line: twelve shooters behind the barricade at the road bottleneck. Keiko commanding. Rifles and shotguns, disciplined fire, aimed shots only. They didn't have ammunition for spray.

Second line: eight shooters on elevated positions. The tree stand, the granite shelf, the dam walkway. Overlapping fields of fire. Helen and Carson up here.

Third line: eight reserves behind the dam, guarding the powerhouse where the civilians sheltered. Paul Crane in charge. Last resort.

Joel walked the line once. Checked positions, checked sight lines, checked faces. Fear. Good. Fear kept you sharp if you let it work for you instead of against you.

"Listen up." The line went quiet. "Most of you haven't fought more than ten infected at a time. Today's different. What isn't different is the drill. Aimed shots. Head if you can, center mass if you can't. Reload before you're empty. Watch your lanes. Don't shoot the person next to you." He paused. "Marcus is goin' out ahead of the line. Whatever you see him do, keep your eyes on your own work."

Keiko caught his eye.

"How bad?" she asked.

"Bad enough that I'm glad he's on our side."

Joel climbed to the tree stand and picked up Tommy's scoped rifle. He could see two miles up the road from here. Clear afternoon light. Mountains, trees, silence.

He'd been here before. Not this exact spot. The feeling. Austin, 2013. Running through burning streets with his daughter dying in his arms. That had been chaos. No plan, no line, no barricade. Just running and praying and failing.

This was different. They'd built something since then. Walls. People. Drills. Eight years of turning a broken dam into a place worth defending.

He chambered a round. Settled the rifle. Waited.

A hawk circled above the valley entrance. Hunting something in the tree line. Didn't care about the humans with rifles or the monsters heading their way. The hawk had its own problems.

Joel watched it bank against the blue sky and wished, briefly, for that kind of simplicity.

## — Marcus —

They came at sundown.

The first Runners appeared on the road a mile north. Moving fast, stumbling over broken pavement, silhouettes against the fading light. Then more behind them. Then more. They spilled from the tree line on both sides of the road, a loose wave of bodies in various stages of decay. Hiking clothes, work uniforms, fabric so degraded it was more fungus than cotton. The sound reached Crystal Falls before the bodies did. Not screaming. Not moaning. Just the noise of hundreds of feet and hundreds of ruined throats producing something that wasn't language, wasn't anything. The sound of nervous systems with nothing left in them but forward.

Marcus stood fifty yards ahead of the barricade. The road under his boots, creek on his left, mountain on his right. The knife in one hand, handgun in the other.

"If any get past me, that's what the line is for," he said over his shoulder.

"Then don't let 'em past," Keiko called back.

The first wave hit.

Thirty Runners. The front edge of the horde, the fastest, the hungriest. They sprinted toward him in a ragged line, feet slapping asphalt.

Marcus met them halfway.

He'd fought in rooms, buildings, nests, churches, basements, corridors. Always enclosed spaces, always walls to use. The road was different. Open ground, no cover, nothing to put at his back. Just him and the infected and the fading daylight and the geometry of violence that eight years of clearing had made as natural as breathing.

He moved through the first wave the way a butcher moves through a side of beef. Not elegant. Not dramatic. Each kill precise, positioned, economical. Knife to the temple on the left, pivot, base of the skull on the right, step forward, grab one by the collar and throw it into two others, close the gap, finish the pile. Tier 4 reflexes made the Runners look slow. They weren't slow. He was fast.

Thirty seconds. Thirty dead.

**[+396 Points]**
**[Streak: 30]**
**[Horde Engagement: +120]**

The second wave was bigger. Fifty or sixty, Runners mixed with Stalkers. The Stalkers peeled off the road and went for the tree line, flanking. Marcus heard them moving through the brush. Enhanced hearing tracked each footstep. Ten of them. Twelve.

"Stalkers in the trees, left side!" he shouted. "Helen, Carson!"

Rifle fire cracked from the elevated positions. The Stalkers were smart but they were moving through open forest against shooters with clear sight lines. Helen dropped three in quick succession. Carson got two. The rest scattered deeper into the woods, looking for another angle.

Marcus focused on the road. The main body of the horde was arriving now, not in waves but in a continuous stream. Runners packed shoulder to shoulder, tripping over each other, climbing over the fallen. The sound was enormous. Somewhere in the mass, the wet clicking of Clickers.

He fought forward into them. Headshots when there was space, blade work when they got close. He took hits. Runners grabbed his arms, his jacket, his shoulders. Their fingers couldn't pierce Tier 4 skin but they could slow him down. He shrugged them off and kept killing.

Behind him, the barricade line opened fire. Keiko's voice, steady: "Aimed shots! Low on the ones behind him! Stagger your reloads!" The militia fired in disciplined groups, dropping Runners that Marcus hadn't reached yet. Bodies fell. The road became an obstacle course of dead.

**[+612 Points]**
**[Streak: 50]**

A Clicker broke through the Runner mass on the right. Bigger, armored in fungal plating, moving by sound. It swung toward Marcus with a screech that cut through the horde noise. He shot it twice in the chest. The rounds cratered the plating but didn't penetrate. Third shot found the gap where plating met neck. It dropped.

**[+58 Points]**
**[Clicker Kill — Chain Progress: 25/25]**
**[Chain Complete: Clicker Specialist]**
**[Passive Bonus: +25% point gain vs. Clickers]**

Two more Clickers emerged from the mass. Then a third. Then two more. The Beacon had organized them by type. Runners in front, Clickers in the middle, heavy threats at the back. Even dead, the Beacon's structure held.

Marcus killed the Clickers one at a time. Each required precision. Shoot the plating gaps, close the distance, finish with the knife. The militia couldn't handle Clickers. Their rifles didn't have stopping power for fungal armor, and a Clicker at close range was a death sentence for anyone without enhanced reflexes.

Six Clickers total. All his.

**[+441 Points]**

The stream of Runners thinned. Marcus had killed over a hundred personally. The barricade line had dropped dozens more. Bodies covered the road in a carpet of fungal flesh and ruined clothing. The air was thick with the smell of it. Sweet and rotten and wrong.

Then the ground shook.

The Bloaters came through the tree line where the road curved, forty yards ahead. Two of them. Stage 4 infected, each the size of a small car. Armored in thick fungal plates, shoulders hunched, arms swinging. They moved with the slow inevitability of things that didn't need to hurry because nothing could stop them.

The first one ripped a chunk of asphalt from the road and threw it. Marcus dodged left. The chunk hit the ground where he'd been standing and shattered, spraying gravel across the barricade. Someone yelped. Not hurt. Surprised.

"Fall back to the line." Joel's voice from the tree stand. Calm. The kind of calm that came from decades of things going wrong. "Let Marcus handle the big ones. Keep shootin' stragglers."

Marcus looked at the two Bloaters. Eight hundred pounds each, minimum. Fungal armor thick enough to shrug off rifle rounds. In Carbondale, he'd killed one by collapsing a mine tunnel on it. No mine here. No tricks. Just the road and the knife and whatever his body could do.

He ran at them.

The first Bloater swung a fist the size of a cinder block. Marcus ducked under it and drove the knife into the thing's knee joint, where the plating was thinnest. Not deep enough to drop it. Deep enough to slow it. The Bloater stumbled. Marcus climbed its back, using the fungal plates as handholds, and drove the knife into the base of the skull. Once. Twice. Three times, hunting for the brain stem through inches of hardened growth.

The Bloater reached back with both hands and ripped him off. Threw him fifteen feet down the road. Marcus hit the pavement, rolled, and was on his feet before his body registered the impact. His left shoulder screamed. Dislocated or cracked. Hard to tell through the adrenaline. It would heal. Everything healed now.

The second Bloater charged while the first was still stumbling from the knee wound. Marcus sidestepped and jammed the handgun against the soft tissue under its jaw, the only spot where the plating hadn't fully closed. Fired three times. The rounds tore through and into the brain case. The Bloater dropped like a building falling, hitting the pavement hard enough to crack it.

**[+285 Points]**

The first Bloater was still up. Leaking dark fluid from the skull wounds, fungal plating closing over the damage, but still moving. It turned toward Marcus with the blind determination of something that didn't understand the concept of dying.

Marcus picked up a chunk of the asphalt the first Bloater had thrown. Thirty pounds of broken road. He threw it into the Bloater's face at close range. The impact snapped its head back and cracked the frontal plating wide open. Marcus closed the gap before the thing could recover and buried the knife through the crack, all the way to the hilt, into the brain.

The Bloater swayed. Stood for one more second, like it was thinking about something. Then it fell.

**[+340 Points]**
**[Bloater Kill — Chain Progress: 4/5]**

The road was quiet.

Marcus stood between two Bloater corpses, breathing hard. His shoulder was already knitting back together, the joint popping into place with a grinding sensation he'd never get used to. The knife was buried in fungal tissue. He pulled it free and wiped it on his jeans.

Behind the barricade, nobody moved. Thirty-four people stood at their positions and stared at the road ahead, at the carpet of bodies, at Marcus standing in the middle of it covered in blood that wasn't his.

Keiko broke the silence. "Clear?"

Marcus checked the map. Stragglers in the tree line, scattered contacts moving away from the road. No organized movement. No more waves.

"Clear," he said.

## — Sarah —

She came out of the powerhouse when the shooting stopped.

Noah was with Lena and Dr. Reyes in the turbine room. He'd been drawing through the entire battle. Dogs in armor. Dogs with swords. Dogs driving tanks. His way of turning sounds he couldn't see into something manageable. She'd left him with Captain pressed against his side and a fresh stack of paper.

The road was worse than she'd imagined.

Bodies. Hundreds of them. The smell hit first. Fungal decay, gunpowder, and something sweet underneath that she'd never learned to name. The barricade was intact but pocked with chips where the asphalt chunk had sprayed. Shell casings littered the ground behind the firing line like brass seeds.

Marcus sat on the barricade. His jacket was torn down the left side, his arm held at an angle that was straightening as she watched. Accelerated healing. She'd seen it enough times to recognize the process. The wrongness of a joint correcting itself in minutes instead of months.

Joel climbed down from the tree stand. He walked past the militia, past the barricade, past the first row of bodies, and stopped in front of Marcus.

"You good?"

Marcus nodded. "Shoulder's coming back. Couple minutes."

Joel looked at the road. At the Bloater corpses. At the hundred-plus bodies Marcus had put down personally, each one a clean kill, each one dropped in a matter of seconds. Joel had watched from the tree stand through the rifle scope. He'd seen every second of it.

"I've seen a lot in eight years," Joel said quietly. "I ain't ever seen anything like that."

Marcus didn't answer. His eyes had the unfocused look that meant he was reading the system display. Sarah could always tell.

"How many points?" she asked.

He was quiet for a moment.

"A lot."

"Marcus."

"Three thousand, six hundred and twelve from the horde. Seven hundred and five from Snowmass. Plus chain bonuses." He paused. "I completed the Clicker Specialist chain."

Sarah did the math. He'd started the day at 4,275. "Over eight thousand."

"Eight thousand, five hundred and ninety-two."

**[Points: 8,592]**

She looked at the road. At the bodies. At the militia members sitting on the barricade because their legs wouldn't hold them up. Helen was already walking among the dead with her clipboard, counting. Of course she was counting. Helen counted things the way Joel cleaned rifles. Reflex under stress.

"Injuries?" Sarah asked.

"Six. Nothing critical." Keiko appeared behind them, rifle slung over one shoulder. "Dale took a bite to his forearm. His jacket stopped the teeth. Ana sprained her ankle on the barricade. Dr. Reyes is checking everyone." She looked at Sarah. "No fatalities."

"No fatalities," Sarah repeated.

Joel hadn't moved. He was still looking at the road with the expression he wore when he was measuring something. Calculating load-bearing weight. Assessing structural integrity. Deciding if a foundation would hold.

"This won't be the last one," he said.

"No," Marcus said. "It won't."

"Then we need to find every one of those Beacons between here and the state line. Kill 'em before they build another horde."

"That's why we need the coalition." Sarah pulled her pencil from behind her ear. The pencil was a tool and a comfort object and a promise that somewhere in her head, a plan was already forming. "Basalt, Glenwood, Willow Creek. Every settlement patrols their own territory. Any cluster that's growing instead of shrinking gets reported. We find the Beacons early, before they mature."

Joel nodded. Not enthusiasm. Acknowledgment. The nod of a man who'd survived eight years by assuming every solution was temporary and every threat was patient.

Marcus stood up from the barricade. His shoulder was straight again. Healed.

"Monday council meeting," Sarah said. "You brief on the Snowmass Beacon. I present the coalition timeline. We accelerate everything."

"Yeah."

"And you need a new jacket."

Marcus looked down at the shredded remains of his left sleeve. "I liked this jacket."

"You like all your jackets. They all end up like this."

Joel walked toward the workshop. Tommy was already organizing a cleanup crew for the road. The bodies needed burning before the spores spread. There was work to do. There was always work to do. That was the thing about survival. It didn't end when the shooting stopped. It just changed shape.

Sarah stood on the barricade for another minute. The sky was dark now. Stars coming out over the mountains, cold and clear. The string lights on the dam walkway clicked on behind her. Tommy's timer. Same time every evening.

Three hundred and forty-seven people. Still here. Still breathing. No funerals tonight.

She climbed down and went to find her son.
