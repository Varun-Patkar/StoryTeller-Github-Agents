# Chapter 3: First Aid

Joel Miller was bigger than the game made him look.

That was a stupid thing to notice. Completely irrelevant. The man was kneeling on asphalt with his daughter bleeding out in his arms and Marcus's brain decided the important observation was that Joel was broad across the shoulders, thick through the chest, the kind of frame that came from years of swinging hammers and hauling lumber. A contractor's build. The hands pressing against Sarah's side were massive, calloused, and red.

"Baby girl, look at me. Look at me, Sarah. Keep your eyes open."

Joel's voice was wrong. Not wrong like a stranger's voice, the way Marcus's own new voice was wrong. Wrong like it had been stripped of everything except the one thing that mattered. He wasn't talking to his daughter. He was talking to the universe, begging it, bargaining with it, his Texas drawl cracking on every other word.

Sarah wasn't answering.

Tommy stood three feet away, the handgun still pointed at the soldier on the ground. The soldier wasn't moving. Two red holes in his chest, close together, center mass. Tommy's aim had been good. His hand was steady. The rest of him wasn't. His face had the look of a man standing at the edge of a cliff and trying very hard not to look down.

Marcus was forty feet away. Thirty. Twenty.

Tommy saw him first.

The gun came up fast. One second it was pointed at a dead man and the next it was pointed at Marcus's chest. Tommy's eyes were wide and his jaw was set and there was nothing friendly about any of it.

"Stop right there."

Marcus stopped. His hands came up, the hunting knife in his right hand catching the sodium light from the overpass above. Bad look. Very bad look. He tossed the knife to the side. It clattered on the asphalt.

"I can help her," he said. "I have medical supplies."

His voice came out ragged, thin, the voice of a man who'd been sprinting for the last quarter mile on lungs that had nothing left. He bent over, hands on his knees, sucking air. Not the most convincing introduction.

Tommy didn't lower the gun. "Who the hell are you?"

"My name's Marcus. I can help her. Please."

Joel looked up. His face was wet. Not just sweat. His eyes found Marcus and there was nothing in them but hostility and terror and the kind of raw, desperate fury that was one wrong word away from violence.

"Get away from us."

"She's bleeding out." Marcus straightened up. "I can see it from here. Where was she hit?"

"I said get away from us!" Joel's voice broke on the last word. He pulled Sarah closer to his chest, cradling her head against his shoulder. She was limp. Not dead. Her chest moved. Shallow, fast breaths, the kind that meant shock was setting in. But alive.

Marcus looked at Tommy. Tommy was the bridge. Tommy was the practical one, the one who'd joined the Fireflies because he believed in something bigger than himself, the one who ran Jackson and made it work. Tommy was the one who would listen.

"Your brother can't stop the bleeding with his hands," Marcus said. He kept his voice level. Calm. The opposite of how he felt. "I've got bandages. Let me help."

Tommy's eyes went from Marcus to Joel to Sarah and back to Marcus. The gun stayed up.

"Where'd you come from?" Tommy asked.

"I live nearby. I heard the shots. Please. She doesn't have time for twenty questions."

Tommy's expression changed. A calculation. The math of a man who knew his niece was dying and that the stranger with the empty hands was the only person offering anything besides prayers.

"Joel," Tommy said. Not a question. Not a command. Just the name, loaded with everything that needed to be said.

Joel didn't move. His hands pressed harder against Sarah's side and blood ran between his fingers, dark and steady, soaking into his flannel shirt.

"Joel. Let him look."

"I don't know him, Tommy."

"I know you don't. But she's..." Tommy didn't finish the sentence. He didn't have to.

Joel's jaw worked. His eyes stayed on Marcus for a long, terrible moment. Then he looked down at Sarah and the fight went out of him all at once.

"Help her," he said. The words came out like they cost him everything he had. "Please help her."

Marcus moved.

He dropped to his knees next to Joel, the asphalt biting through his jeans. Sarah was small. Twelve years old and small for her age, or maybe she just looked small because Joel was so big and because there was so much blood. She was wearing a t-shirt and pajama pants. The blood was on her right side, lower, where the hip met the thigh. The t-shirt was soaked through and the pajama pants were dark and wet from the waist down.

Her eyes were half-open. Unfocused. She was conscious but not really there, drifting in that grey space between awake and not.

"Sarah?" Marcus said. "Sarah, my name's Marcus. I'm going to help you, okay?"

No response. Her breathing hitched. A small sound came out of her, barely audible, that might have been a whimper.

"Mall," Marcus thought.

The blue interface flickered open. He navigated to Medicine with a thought. Bandages: 2 points. He had 3 points.

He bought the bandages.

**[Purchase: Sterile Gauze Bandages (Pack) — 2 Points]**
**[Remaining Points: 1]**

A vacuum-sealed packet of white gauze appeared in his left hand. No flash. No sparkle. Just suddenly there.

Joel flinched. "What the..."

"Don't worry about it." Marcus ripped the packet open with his teeth. "Move your hands."

Joel didn't move his hands.

"I need to see the wound. Move your hands."

Joel moved his hands. Slowly, like each finger had to be pried away individually. The wound was ugly. A ragged hole on the outside of her right hip, just below the waistband of her pajama pants. Entry wound. Marcus couldn't see an exit wound without rolling her, which he didn't want to do. The bleeding was steady but not spurting. Steady was bad enough, but no spurting meant no arterial hit. She was lucky. If you could call any twelve-year-old with a bullet hole in her lucky.

He wadded the gauze and pressed it against the wound. Hard. Sarah made a sound, a thin, sharp cry that cut through the sirens and the helicopters and Joel's ragged breathing.

"I know," Marcus said. "I know it hurts. I'm sorry. I have to press hard."

Joel's hand came up and gripped Marcus's wrist. Not to stop him. To hold on. Like Marcus's arm was a rope and Joel was drowning.

"Is she gonna be okay?" Joel asked.

Marcus didn't answer that. He didn't know. Gauze and pressure could slow the bleeding but she needed more than gauze and pressure. She needed a real first aid kit. Antiseptic. Proper wound packing. Pain management.

He had one point. One point bought him nothing useful.

"I need more supplies," Marcus said. "Give me a minute."

"A minute? She's bleedin' out and you need a minute?"

"I need a minute."

He looked around. The highway above them was chaos. Headlights and sirens and the distant crackle of automatic weapons. The underpass was dark, lit only by the spillover from the sodium lamps on the road above. The dead soldier lay fifteen feet away, rifle next to him, radio still squawking tinny commands that nobody was going to follow.

And at the far end of the underpass, at the edge of the light, something was moving.

It came out of the shadows the way they always did. Fast, twitching, wrong. A man in a dress shirt with no tie, one sleeve ripped off at the shoulder, blood down the front. A Runner. Drawn by the screaming. Drawn by Joel's screaming, which had been loud enough to hear two blocks away.

Tommy saw it.

"Contact!" Tommy raised the handgun and fired once. The shot cracked off the concrete walls and the Runner stumbled but didn't go down. Tommy fired again. The Runner's left leg buckled and it fell to one knee, then got back up because that's what Runners did, they got back up, the Cordyceps didn't care about bullet holes in non-vital areas.

Marcus scooped up his hunting knife from where he'd tossed it. He stood, hands sticky with Sarah's blood, and crossed the distance in four steps.

The Runner lunged at him. He sidestepped, grabbed a fistful of its shirt collar, and drove the knife into the base of its skull from behind. The blade went in deep, angled upward, and the Runner dropped. Dead weight. It hit the concrete and didn't move.

He stood over it, breathing hard. His hands were shaking again.

**[INFECTED ELIMINATED — Runner]**
**[+7 Points]**
**[Assist Bonus: +2 Points (Target was previously wounded)]**
**[Total Points: 10]**

Ten points.

"Mall," he thought, already moving back toward Sarah. Medicine. Basic first aid kit: 8 points.

He bought it.

**[Purchase: Basic First Aid Kit — 8 Points]**
**[Remaining Points: 2]**

A green canvas pouch appeared in his hand, about the size of a thick paperback. He unzipped it as he knelt back down next to Sarah. Inside: antiseptic wipes, adhesive bandages, a small roll of medical tape, a packet of hemostatic gauze, a tube of antibiotic ointment, a pair of nitrile gloves, a compact emergency blanket, and two single-dose packets of acetaminophen.

Not a trauma kit. Not what an ER would have. But it was something.

He pulled on the nitrile gloves. His hands were steadier now. Not because the shaking had stopped, but because there was something to do with them. Panic was a luxury. Right now, he was the only thing between this girl and bleeding to death on a road in Austin.

"Joel, hold her still. This is going to hurt."

Joel nodded. He put one hand on Sarah's shoulder and the other on her uninjured hip, gentle and firm at the same time.

Marcus peeled away the gauze he'd pressed against the wound. It was soaked through, heavy and dark. The bleeding had slowed from the pressure but it hadn't stopped. He tore open an antiseptic wipe and cleaned around the wound. Sarah flinched. A small moan.

"You're doin' great, baby girl," Joel said. His voice had steadied. Not calm, not by a long shot, but steadier. He had something to focus on now. Hold her still. Talk to her. Be her dad. "You're so brave, sweetheart."

Marcus opened the hemostatic gauze. The good stuff. The kind that had clotting agents baked into the fibers. He packed it into the wound, pressing firmly, layering it in. Sarah cried out and her body tried to curl away from the pain. Joel held her.

"I know," Marcus said again. "Almost done. Almost done."

He wasn't almost done. He was maybe halfway done. But you said almost done because that's what you said to a twelve-year-old who'd been shot.

Medical tape. He taped the hemostatic gauze in place, wrapping it tight around her hip, pulling the tape snug enough to maintain pressure. Then a second layer of regular gauze over the top, secured with more tape. It wasn't pretty. It wasn't textbook. But the bleeding was stopping. He could see it. The gauze was darkening at the center but not spreading, not soaking through the way the first application had.

He tore open the emergency blanket and draped it over Sarah. The silver foil crinkled as Joel adjusted it around her shoulders. She was shivering. Shock. Her body was dialing everything down, pulling blood to the core, trying to keep the essential organs running.

"She needs to stay warm," Marcus said. "Keep her legs elevated if you can."

Tommy was standing guard at the end of the underpass, gun up, scanning the dark. He'd watched the whole thing without a word. Now he glanced back.

"How bad?"

"The bullet hit the outside of her hip. Missed the bone, I think. Missed the femoral artery for sure, or she'd already be gone. She's lost blood but the bleeding's controlled now." Marcus sat back on his heels. "She needs a real doctor. Antibiotics. But she's stable for now."

"Stable," Joel repeated. He said it like he was tasting the word, trying to decide if he believed it. His hand was on Sarah's forehead, brushing her hair back. "She's stable?"

"Yeah. She's stable."

Joel looked at Marcus for a long time. The hostility was still there. It hadn't gone anywhere. But it was sitting next to something else now, something that Joel clearly didn't know what to do with. Gratitude was too small a word. This was a man who'd been watching his daughter die thirty seconds ago and was now being told she wasn't going to.

"How?" Joel said. "Where did you... how did you have all that?"

Marcus didn't have an answer for that. Not one Joel would believe. Not one that made sense in any world, even a world where people turned into fungus zombies and ate each other.

"I was a paramedic," he lied. It was the first thing that came to mind and it was terrible, but Joel wasn't in any state to fact-check anybody.

Joel nodded. He didn't believe it. Marcus could tell. But he accepted it, the way you accept something that doesn't matter next to the thing that does matter, which was that Sarah was breathing and the blood wasn't spreading.

"Dad?"

Everyone froze.

Sarah's voice was small. Thin. Barely there. But her eyes were open and they were focused and they found Joel's face.

"I'm right here, baby girl." Joel leaned down, his forehead almost touching hers. "I'm right here. You're gonna be okay."

"It hurts."

"I know. I know it does."

"Who..." Sarah's eyes moved. They found Marcus. Held there for a second, confused and foggy. "Who's that?"

"He's... he helped you," Joel said. The words came out careful, measured, like he was still working out what they meant. "He's a friend."

"Hi," Marcus said.

Sarah looked at him for another second. Then her eyes closed and her breathing evened out. Not unconscious. Just exhausted. The kind of sleep that a body demands when it's been through more than it can process.

The blue notification appeared, soft and quiet, in Marcus's peripheral vision.

**[QUEST COMPLETE: Save Sarah Miller]**

**[Sarah Miller has been stabilized. Vital signs: weak but steady.]**

**[Reward: 500 Points]**
**[Reward: Trait Unlocked — Guardian]**
**[Guardian: +15% healing effectiveness when treating others. +5 Willpower.]**

**[Total Points: 502]**

Marcus stared at the notification. Five hundred and two points. He'd gone from broke to rich in the time it took to pack gauze into a bullet wound.

He dismissed the notification with a thought and said nothing. This wasn't the time to celebrate. A twelve-year-old was sleeping on bloody asphalt under a highway overpass while her father cried and the world fell apart around them. None of that was a victory. It was just survival.

But she was alive.

---

"We gotta move," Tommy said from the end of the underpass. He hadn't raised his voice, but the urgency was clear. "More soldiers comin'. I can hear the trucks."

He was right. Marcus could hear it too. Diesel engines, getting closer. The radio on the dead soldier's belt was still squawking, and somewhere in the static and the clipped military jargon he caught the words "sweep" and "clear" and "weapons free." None of those were good.

"Can she be moved?" Joel asked Marcus. He was already lifting Sarah, cradling her against his chest. Her head lolled against his shoulder and the silver emergency blanket rustled.

"Carefully. Don't jostle her right side. The packing needs to stay in place."

Joel stood. Sarah weighed nothing in his arms. Or maybe she weighed everything, but he carried her like she weighed nothing, because that's what fathers did.

Tommy moved to the edge of the underpass and checked both directions. The road leading south was blocked by abandoned cars and the body of a woman who had been running and wasn't running anymore. The road north, back toward the neighborhoods Marcus had come through, was dark and relatively clear.

"North," Tommy said. "Away from the highway, stay off the main roads. I know these neighborhoods."

"The military's north too," Marcus said. "I came through there. They're settin' up checkpoints."

"Then we go around 'em." Tommy looked at Marcus. Really looked at him, for the first time since the underpass. Taking in the bloodstained hoodie, the hunting knife, the fact that this stranger had appeared out of nowhere with medical supplies that hadn't existed ten seconds before he used them. "You comin' with us?"

Marcus looked at Joel, who was already walking north, Sarah in his arms, not waiting for anyone's permission or opinion. Joel didn't care about Marcus's plans. Joel didn't care about anything except the girl in his arms and the road ahead.

"Yeah," Marcus said. He picked up the hunting knife and wiped it on his jeans. "I'm coming."

Tommy nodded. No thank you. No handshake. Just a nod, one practical man to another, acknowledging that the stranger with the impossible medical supplies was useful and that useful was all that mattered tonight.

They moved north, into the dark, away from the highway and the soldiers and the dead man on the ground.

...

The first three blocks were quiet. Not peaceful. Nothing about this night was peaceful. But the stretch of residential street they were on had been emptied out. Cars gone, houses dark, front doors standing open like the whole neighborhood had evacuated in a hurry and left the lights off behind them.

Joel walked in front, carrying Sarah. He hadn't spoken since they left the underpass. His boots made steady sounds on the pavement, measured and even, the pace of a man who could walk all night if he had to.

Tommy walked to the left, the handgun held low against his thigh, checking driveways and yards with the kind of habitual alertness that suggested military training or something close to it.

Marcus walked behind them both and tried to process the last fifteen minutes of his life.

He'd saved Sarah Miller.

The quest was complete. The notification had said so. Five hundred points and a trait called Guardian that apparently made him better at treating injured people. His hands were still shaking. The nitrile gloves were bloody. He peeled them off and shoved them in his pocket because littering felt wrong even during an apocalypse.

"Status," he thought.

**[Name: Marcus Webb]**
**[Age: 14]**
**[Points: 502]**
**[Health: 89%]**
**[Attributes: Strength 6, Agility 5, Constitution 6, Perception 5, Intelligence 7, Willpower 11]**
**[Skills: None]**
**[Traits: Guardian — +15% healing effectiveness when treating others]**
**[Evaluation: Average civilian. Saved one life. It's a start.]**

Willpower 11. Up from 6. The Guardian trait had bumped it by 5. He wasn't sure what Willpower actually did in practical terms, but having more of it felt better than having less.

Five hundred and two points. He could afford real weapons now. Body armor. More medical supplies. He could buy a handgun and still have four hundred left. He could buy antibiotics for Sarah. He should buy antibiotics for Sarah.

"Mall," he thought. Medicine. Antibiotics, one course: 15 points. He bought them.

**[Purchase: Antibiotics (Amoxicillin, 10-day course) — 15 Points]**
**[Remaining Points: 487]**

The pill bottle appeared in his jacket pocket. Small, orange, pharmacy-style. He'd give them to Joel in the morning, with instructions. Right now Joel wasn't in any state to process anything beyond "walk" and "hold Sarah."

They reached the end of the residential street and Tommy held up a fist. Military signal. Stop.

Marcus stopped. Joel stopped. Tommy crouched behind a sedan and looked around the corner of a brick wall.

"Checkpoint," Tommy said quietly. "Two trucks. Soldiers. They're checkin' people."

Marcus peered past him. A hundred yards ahead, where the residential street met a wider road, two military trucks were parked nose to nose, creating a bottleneck. Floodlights on portable stands lit the area in harsh white. A line of civilians stood with their hands up while soldiers patted them down. A woman was arguing with a sergeant. A kid was crying.

Normal checkpoint stuff, if you forgot about the part where these same soldiers had been shooting people an hour ago.

"We go around," Marcus said. "Two blocks east, there's a street that runs parallel. I came through there on my way here."

Tommy looked at him. "You know these streets?"

"I've been running through them all night."

Tommy didn't ask why a stranger had been running through neighborhoods toward the sound of gunfire. He just nodded and started moving east, hugging the fence line of somebody's backyard. Joel followed. Marcus followed Joel.

Two blocks east, the parallel street was clear. Dark houses, empty cars, a streetlight that had been shot out. They moved fast, Tommy on point, Joel in the middle with Sarah, Marcus covering the rear with a hunting knife and 487 system points and the deeply unsettling awareness that he was the most well-equipped person in a three-person group that included a grown man with a handgun.

A dog barked somewhere behind them. Then stopped.

They walked for another ten minutes without talking. Sarah whimpered once and Joel murmured something too quiet for Marcus to hear. The sirens were behind them now, the helicopter circling further south, the orange glow of fires fading as they moved deeper into the residential sprawl north of the highway.

---

Tommy found a house. One story, brick, set back from the road with a high wooden fence around the backyard. The front door was locked. Tommy checked a window on the side and found it unlatched. He slid it open, checked inside with the handgun first, then waved them in.

The house was empty. Recently lived in. There were dishes in the kitchen sink and a newspaper on the dining table and a pair of reading glasses next to a half-finished crossword. Whoever lived here had left, probably earlier tonight, probably in one of those cars that had been streaming north on every road Marcus had crossed.

Joel carried Sarah to a couch in the living room and laid her down. Carefully. So carefully, like she was made of something that would break if he breathed too hard. He arranged the emergency blanket around her and checked the bandages on her hip. The gauze was dark at the center but dry at the edges. Holding.

He sat on the floor next to the couch and put his hand on her forehead.

He didn't move after that.

Tommy closed the blinds, checked all the doors, tested the locks on the back windows. Then he came back to the living room. He stood in the doorway, looking at Joel and Sarah, and for the first time since Marcus had seen him, the practiced calm slipped. His chin trembled. He pressed his lips together and breathed through his nose and got it under control.

"She's tough," Tommy said.

Joel didn't respond.

Tommy looked at Marcus. "Kitchen."

They stepped into the kitchen. Tommy set the handgun on the counter and leaned against the sink. He crossed his arms and looked at Marcus with the steady, appraising gaze of someone who was used to making fast decisions about people.

"You just happened to be out there," Tommy said. Not a question.

"Yeah."

"With bandages that appeared out of thin air."

So he'd noticed. Marcus had hoped the chaos and the dark and the adrenaline would cover the materialization thing. No such luck. Tommy was observant.

"I had them in my bag," Marcus said.

"You don't have a bag."

Marcus looked down at himself. Bloody hoodie. Jeans with a rip from the fence. No bag. No pockets big enough for a first aid kit. Not even a backpack.

"Look," Tommy said. "I don't know what I saw and I don't know what you did and right now I don't much care. My niece is alive because of you and that buys you a lot of rope." He unfolded his arms and picked the gun back up. "But if you're a threat to my family, I'll put you in the ground. We clear?"

"Crystal clear."

"Good." Tommy's face softened. Not much. A fraction. The kind of softening that a hard man allowed himself when nobody else was watching. "Thank you. For what you did."

"You're welcome."

Tommy pocketed the gun and walked back to the living room. He sat in an armchair across from the couch and stretched his legs out and stared at the ceiling.

Marcus stood alone in the dark kitchen of a stranger's house and listened to Joel talk quietly to his sleeping daughter and the distant sound of the world coming apart outside the walls.

He opened his status screen one more time. 487 points. Willpower 11. One trait. No skills. No plan beyond keeping a twelve-year-old alive until morning.

The system was quiet. No new quests. No helpful tutorials. Just the soft blue glow of the interface and the hum of the refrigerator against his back when he sat down on the kitchen floor.

It was still running. The fridge. Keeping somebody's milk cold and somebody's leftovers fresh in a house that nobody lived in anymore. A normal, stupid, beautiful sound.

Sarah was alive.

He closed the status screen and leaned his head back against the fridge door.

"One down," he said to the empty kitchen. "About a hundred to go."
