# Chapter 54: The Quarantine Zone

## — Marcus —

**Day 27. Western Massachusetts.**

The highway cut through hills covered in maple and birch, the trees so thick on both sides that the road felt like a tunnel. Twenty years of unchecked growth had narrowed the drivable lanes to a single track in places, with saplings pushing through cracks in the pavement and root systems buckling the concrete barriers. An overturned tractor-trailer blocked the eastbound lanes near a rest area, its cargo of bottled water long since scavenged, plastic wrap flapping in the wind.

Joel's pace had picked up over the last three days. Not faster, exactly. More deliberate. The careful steps of a man who'd decided that his knees could complain all they wanted, but they weren't going to slow him down.

The system map was getting busier. More contacts. Not just scattered Runners and the occasional Stalker, but clusters. Groups of eight, ten, fifteen infected packed into buildings and underground spaces. No Beacon signatures. Just natural density. Twenty years of Cordyceps doing what Cordyceps did in a region with millions of initial hosts and nobody clearing them out.

"More of 'em," Joel said. He didn't need the map. He could read the signs. Fungal growth on the roadside guardrails. Spore patches in the shade of underpasses. The sweet-rot smell that carried on the wind when you got within a mile of a major cluster.

"Population density. We're close to the northeast corridor. The initial infection rate out here would have been ten times what we saw in Texas."

"How many on the map?"

Marcus checked. "Forty-seven within ten miles. Most dormant. Daytime, sheltered in buildings. They won't be a problem unless we go looking."

"We're not goin' lookin'."

They walked. A red-tailed hawk sat on a highway sign for Springfield and watched them pass. It didn't fly. No reason to. Two men on a road were barely worth noticing in a world that had mostly forgotten what men looked like.

## — Joel —

**Day 28. Central Massachusetts.**

The world got ugly around Worcester.

Not the infected kind of ugly. The human kind. Worcester had been a mid-sized city, maybe 200,000 people before. Now it was a graveyard with a fortress built on top. The highway ran along its southern edge, and from the elevated stretch Joel could see the layout.

FEDRA had walled off the city center at some point. Concrete barriers, shipping containers, razor wire. The same kit Joel had seen at every failed QZ between Texas and Colorado in those first years. But the walls here had been breached. Multiple points. Blast marks on the concrete. One section of shipping containers pushed inward like something had rammed through them at speed.

Inside the walls, smoke. Not fresh. Days old, maybe a week. But rising from multiple points in a thin grey haze that the wind pushed east.

"Civil war," Marcus said. He was reading the map. "Firefly and FEDRA signatures mixed together inside the walls. No clear front line."

"That's what happens when a QZ collapses from the inside. Seen it before. Denver. Pittsburgh. FEDRA squeezes too hard, the Fireflies recruit the locals, and eventually somebody fires the first shot. After that, everybody loses."

They didn't stop. Didn't engage. Didn't enter the city. They walked the highway shoulder with their heads down and their pace steady, two figures against a backdrop of distant smoke.

Joel watched a plume rise from a building near the center. Someone had set that fire on purpose. Whether FEDRA burning Firefly caches or Fireflies burning FEDRA checkpoints, the result was the same. People who could have been farming or building were instead destroying the last of what they had.

Twenty years. Twenty years since the world ended, and the ones who'd survived were still finding reasons to burn each other's things.

"Boston's gonna be worse," Joel said.

"Yeah."

"How much worse?"

"Boston was one of the largest QZs on the east coast. Four to five thousand people in the QZ proper. FEDRA's been losing control for years. The Fireflies have been escalating. More people, more guns, more to fight over."

Joel shifted his rifle on his shoulder. The strap had worn a groove into his jacket over the past month.

"And we're walkin' into the middle of it."

"The edge. Ellie's at a school on the eastern perimeter. If the information's right, we can get in and out without crossing the fighting."

"If."

"If."

They kept walking. The smoke faded behind them. The highway opened up again.

## — Marcus —

**Day 29. Forty miles west of Boston.**

Marcus found the safe house at the coordinates Vasquez's network had flagged: a brick farmhouse set back from Route 9, three miles south of the turnpike, with a blue tarp on the roof and a broken weather vane shaped like a rooster.

He'd been watching it for twenty minutes from a tree line across the road. Enhanced Senses sweeping for information. Two heartbeats inside. One steady, one faster. Someone nervous. No infected contacts within a mile. No other human signatures.

"Two people," Marcus told Joel. "One calm, one anxious."

Joel was crouched behind a stone wall, watching the farmhouse through his rifle scope. "Windows are blacked out. Door's shut. Truck in the driveway with no plates."

"The contact is Rivera. Former Firefly radio operator. Vasquez vouched for her through a three-step chain."

"Three-step chain over seven months. Things change in seven months."

"They do."

Joel lowered the scope. Looked at the farmhouse. The weather vane's rooster silhouette twitched in the afternoon breeze.

"I go in first," Joel said. "You watch from here. If it's wrong, come get me."

"I should go. I'm harder to kill."

"That's why you stay outside. If it's a trap, I need someone who can take apart whatever they've got. If it's fine, I signal and you follow."

Sound logic. Marcus didn't like it, but it was sound.

Joel stood. Straightened his jacket. Checked his pistol, holstered it. Walked across the road with the easy, rolling gait of a man going to check his mailbox. No urgency. No threat. Twenty years of experience in exactly this kind of moment: approaching an unknown door and deciding whether to knock or kick.

He knocked. Three times. Waited.

The door opened a crack.

Marcus heard the conversation through the walls. Every word. Every shift in breathing.

"Rivera?" Joel's voice. Low, even.

"Who's asking?" Female. Mid-thirties. Controlled but tight.

"A friend of Vasquez. Hartford network."

A pause. The faster heartbeat inside slowed by two beats. Recognition, or at least the right code words.

"How many are you?"

"Two. My partner's across the road."

Whispering inside. Two voices. The second one younger, male.

The door opened wider. Joel stepped in.

Marcus waited. Counted to sixty. At sixty, Joel appeared in the doorway and waved him across.

The farmhouse was clean inside. Not lived-in clean. Operational clean. Furniture pushed against walls to create clear sightlines to the windows. Two sleeping bags in the back bedroom. A camp stove on the kitchen counter with a pot of boiled water. Maps taped to the walls, hand-drawn, with pencil annotations Marcus could read from across the room.

Rivera sat at the kitchen table. Dark hair pulled back. Thin face. She wore a faded military jacket with the Firefly patches removed. You could see the thread marks where they'd been stitched, then cut.

The second person was a kid. Seventeen, maybe eighteen. Skinny, sitting on the counter near the back door with one foot resting on the handle of a baseball bat propped against the cabinet. He had the look of someone who'd been assessing threats since before he could drive.

"Rivera," the woman said. "This is Danny. He was at the St. Mary's outpost before it fell."

Danny didn't offer a hand. Joel didn't expect him to.

"You came a long way," Rivera said.

"We did," Joel said.

"Vasquez said you were from a settlement out west. Colorado."

"That's right."

"And you're here for the girl."

Joel looked at Marcus. Marcus nodded.

"We're here for Ellie Williams," Marcus said. "What can you tell us?"

Rivera pulled a map off the wall and spread it on the table. Hand-drawn, detailed, with the care of someone who'd walked every street. The Boston QZ. Wall perimeter. FEDRA checkpoints in blue. Firefly positions in red. Civilian areas in green. One building on the eastern edge, circled three times in pencil.

"Here," Rivera said. "FEDRA military prep school. East sector, three blocks inside the wall. They use it as a boarding school for orphans and wards of the state. About sixty kids, ages ten to eighteen. Eight FEDRA personnel. Low security because it's a school, not a barracks."

"How current is this?" Marcus asked.

"I left the QZ eleven days ago. As of then, Ellie was still at the school. She'd been moved to a private room after the bite. FEDRA doesn't know she's immune. They think she's in quarantine, waiting to turn. The school physician checked her on Day 3 post-bite and couldn't explain why she wasn't symptomatic. He filed a report. That report went up the chain and got flagged by a Firefly mole in the medical office."

"Which is how Marlene found out."

"Marlene knew before the report. She's had people watching Ellie for years. The mole confirmed what she already suspected." Rivera tapped the circled building. "Here's the problem. Marlene was wounded in a Firefly operation against FEDRA two weeks ago. Gunshot to the abdomen. She's recovering in a safe house in the north sector. Can't travel."

"But she's still planning to move Ellie," Marcus said.

"She's planning to hand Ellie off to a smuggler. Get her out of the QZ through the tunnels and west to Salt Lake City with a Firefly escort."

"Who's the smuggler?"

"Was a man named Robert. He's dead. Killed three days before I left. Turf dispute. Marlene's scrambling for a replacement. Last I heard, she was reaching out to a woman named Tess. Operates in the QZ with a partner. Tough. Connected. Knows the smuggling routes better than anyone."

Marcus processed the name. Tess. In canon, Tess and Joel had been partners. She'd died in the State House, buying Joel and Ellie time to escape. But in this timeline, Joel had never gone to Boston. Never became a smuggler. Never met Tess. She was just a name in a briefing, attached to a woman living a life that Marcus's choices had made irrelevant to Joel's story.

Except now their paths were converging again. Different angles, same girl in the middle.

"What's Marlene's timeline?" Marcus asked.

"Days. A week at most. She's desperate. She knows FEDRA will figure out Ellie's immunity eventually, and when they do, they'll take her to their own labs for study. Marlene wants Ellie in Firefly hands before that happens."

"And what happens when Ellie's in Firefly hands?"

Rivera looked at him. Her expression stayed flat, but her heartbeat spiked. Just once. Then settled.

"You already know," she said.

"I want to hear you say it."

"Dr. Anderson's team in Salt Lake City has been waiting for a viable immune subject for years. The Cordyceps mutation grows inside the brain, integrated with the host's neural tissue. To study it properly, to reverse-engineer immunity, they need to remove the growth." She stopped. "The procedure is fatal. They would kill her to extract it."

The kitchen was quiet. Danny's foot had stopped swinging against the cabinet. He was looking at the floor.

"That's why I left," Rivera said. "I was Marlene's radio operator for six years. I believed in the mission. Find the cure, save humanity. I did runs between outposts. I relayed orders during firefights. I helped coordinate supply drops to Firefly cells across three states." She unfolded her arms, then folded them again. "Then I learned what the cure actually costs, and it's a fourteen-year-old girl who doesn't get a say in whether she lives or dies."

She looked at Danny. The kid met her eyes and looked away.

"Danny's sister was at the school with Ellie," Rivera said. "She's twelve. He wants her out too."

"I want her out regardless," Danny said. First words he'd spoken. His voice was steady. "The QZ's falling apart. FEDRA's pulling security from the school to reinforce the western checkpoints. Two months from now, those kids won't have guards. They'll have nothing."

Joel was quiet through all of it. Marcus could hear his heartbeat. Steady. Controlled. The heartbeat of a man who'd made his decision days ago and was just collecting the details he needed to execute it.

"The school," Joel said. "Tell me the layout."

## — Joel —

Rivera walked them through it.

The Boston QZ was a rough rectangle cut from the Back Bay and South End. Concrete walls on three sides, the harbor on the fourth. FEDRA held the gates, the checkpoints, and the military installations in the west and center. The Fireflies held pockets in the north and east through safe houses and sympathetic civilians. The fighting was concentrated along the dividing line.

The school sat on the eastern edge. Three blocks inside the wall. Low-priority FEDRA territory because patrols had been pulled west to deal with Firefly attacks.

"This spot here," Rivera said, drawing a line along the eastern perimeter. "There's a breach in the wall behind a parking garage on Atlantic Avenue. Been there for months. FEDRA knows but doesn't have the bodies to fix it. Smugglers use it. Fireflies use it. Half the black market in the east sector runs through that gap."

"How wide?"

"Wide enough for two people. You'll need to shift some debris, but it's passable."

Joel traced the route with his finger. Wall breach to the school. Six blocks. Residential, then commercial, then the school compound. Two right turns and a straight shot.

"Security at the school?"

"Eight FEDRA. Four on, four off. Guards, not soldiers. Their job is keeping kids inside, not keeping threats out. After lights-out, two at the front entrance, two at the loading dock. The east side of the building has a fire escape. Second floor access."

"And Ellie's room?"

"Second floor. East wing. Room 204. The quarantine room. Separated from the other kids by a hallway and a locked door. They check on her twice a day, morning and evening, to see if she's turned."

Joel memorized the layout. He'd done this hundreds of times. A building, a target, an approach, an exit. The difference was that usually the target was a supply cache or a hostile position. This time it was a kid sleeping in a room she'd been locked in because everyone thought she was going to die.

"We go tomorrow night," Joel said. He looked at Marcus.

Marcus nodded. "After dark. Through the breach, to the school, up the fire escape, room 204. We talk to Ellie. We tell her the truth. And we leave before Marlene or FEDRA knows she's gone."

"And if she doesn't want to come?" Rivera asked.

Joel and Marcus looked at each other. They'd talked about this. In the hunting cabin. In the kitchen at Crystal Falls. In twenty years of conversation about a girl neither of them had met.

"Then we stay until she does," Joel said.

Rivera studied them for a long moment. Whatever she was looking for, she found enough of it.

"Danny and I will stay here through tomorrow. If things go wrong and you need a fallback, the farmhouse is yours. After tomorrow night, we're heading north. There's a settlement in Vermont I've been trying to reach."

"Thank you," Marcus said.

"Don't thank me. Get the girl out. That's enough."

## — Marcus —

Rivera offered them the back bedroom for the night. Danny took the first watch on the farmhouse roof with his binoculars and his bat.

Joel lay on one of the sleeping bags with his hat over his eyes. Same position he'd slept in a thousand times. Three minutes from alert to unconscious. A skill he'd never lost.

Marcus sat against the wall. Enhanced physiology meant he could go seventy-two hours on four hours of rest. He'd sleep later. After.

He opened the system interface. The blue light was familiar after twenty years, but tonight it felt smaller. A phone screen in a dark room.

**[QUEST: Prepare for the Retrieval]**
**Status: Active**
**Sub-objectives:**

- ✅ Establish intelligence network in eastern corridor
- ✅ Map route to Boston QZ
- ✅ Identify local contact for QZ infiltration
- ✅ Confirm target location
- ◻ Extract Ellie Williams
- ◻ Secure safe departure from Boston metropolitan area
- ◻ Return to Crystal Falls

Four of seven. The system didn't give point values for these objectives. No experience rewards, no Mall unlock. Just checkboxes. Whatever the system understood about purpose, it understood that this quest wasn't about progression.

Marcus closed the interface.

Tomorrow night. Eleven days since Rivera had left the QZ. In those eleven days, Marlene had been looking for a new smuggler. If Tess hadn't agreed yet, or if Marlene's wound was slowing negotiations, then Ellie was still at the school. Still waiting in room 204 for the infection that would never come.

But if Tess had agreed. If Marlene had moved Ellie already. If they walked into an empty room tomorrow night...

He stopped the thought. Didn't help. Wouldn't change what they did in the morning.

He thought about what Joel had said in the cabin in Pennsylvania.

_We just tell her she's in danger, and we're here, and we're not leavin'._

Twenty years of planning. Sixty-three pages of operational files. A federation of eighteen communities and 4,100 people. Tier 5 abilities that let him hear heartbeats through walls and tear armored vehicles apart. And the plan was: tell a scared girl the truth and stay.

It was the best plan he had.

Outside, the wind shifted. From the east, carrying new sounds over the Massachusetts hills. Distant. Almost inaudible even with Enhanced Senses. But there. A low, irregular rhythm that Marcus had heard before. In Worcester. In Kansas City. In every collapsed city they'd passed through on this road.

Gunfire. Sporadic. Miles away. The sound of the Boston QZ tearing itself apart.

Joel's breathing was steady and deep. The hat hadn't moved. He was dreaming, or he wasn't, and either way he'd be ready in the morning.

Marcus leaned his head against the wall and listened to the faraway war and the close, peaceful sound of his friend sleeping. Tomorrow they'd walk into that war. Not to fight it. Not to win it. Just to pull one girl out of the middle of it and take her somewhere safe.

One more night. Then room 204.
