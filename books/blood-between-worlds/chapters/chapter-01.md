# Chapter 1: A Life Already Lost

## Outline

1. After completing every ending of *The Blood of Dawnwalker*, the gamer goes
   to sleep still dissatisfied with the tragedies no route can repair.
2. Pain, cold, and helplessness replace sleep as he is born into a medieval
   household and recognizes Pieter and Esme.
3. The game's system confirms that he is Coen, but its empty starting state
   denies him an easy path to power.
4. He remembers Alin, Esme, the mines, Brencis, and the thirty-day rescue, then
   chooses his first goal: survive childhood and prevent the lake tragedy.

**Arc beats advanced:** Coen awakens and confirms the system's limits.
**Foreshadowing planted:** Silver imagery beneath the system display; the system
cannot identify the cause of reincarnation.
**Foreshadowing paid off:** None.

## Chapter Text

The final ending was supposed to feel complete.

Coen stood in the cart with Lunka hidden behind him. Vale Sangora disappeared
under the evening mist, and Neberu spoke of the Augurs as if one finished war
had only opened the road to another. Then the credits came.

He let them play.

His room was dark except for the monitor. Empty cups crowded one side of the
desk. A notebook lay open beside the keyboard, filled with quest names, missed
conditions, and the choices required to reach all seven endings. He had seen
the secret ones too. He had abandoned the valley, stayed in Xanthe's dream, let
the thirty days run out, betrayed allies, fought alone, and stood beside every
person the game allowed him to choose.

None of it changed the part that bothered him.

Alin still drowned before the story began. Esme still broke beneath that loss.
Coen still spent his childhood coughing silver dust out of his lungs because
his family needed another miner. By the time the player received control, most
of the damage had already happened.

Even the best ending was a choice among ruins.

He closed the notebook.

On the screen, the last line of the credits gave way to the main menu. Red moths
drifted behind Coen's silhouette. Human by day. Vampire by night. Both halves
useful. Both halves carrying their own weakness.

That idea had kept him playing long after he had learned every efficient route.
Not purity. Not a cure that erased one side. A man trapped between two worlds
who had to make something worthwhile from both.

He glanced at the clock. Far too late again.

"Work will be pleasant tomorrow," he muttered.

He shut down the computer, drank the last warm mouthful of water from a glass,
and went to bed.

There was no pain. No warning. He closed his eyes expecting an alarm in a few
hours.

Instead, pressure crushed him from every direction.

His head hurt. His chest would not open. Something squeezed his body and forced
him toward a place that was colder, louder, and terribly bright.

He tried to pull away.

His arms moved a few useless inches. His legs kicked without finding anything
solid. A hard contraction pushed him forward, and panic tore through every
thought he had.

He could not breathe.

Then hands caught him.

Cold air struck wet skin. His lungs opened with a pain so sharp that he screamed.
The sound was thin, high, and nothing like his voice.

"There," a woman said. Her words were strange for half a heartbeat, then clear.
"There you are. Let me see him."

He tried to turn toward her. His neck failed to obey. Rough cloth rubbed across
his face and body. Someone held him with one broad hand beneath his back while
another cleared his mouth.

"He's breathing," a man said.

"I can hear that, Pieter."

Pieter.

The name cut through the panic.

He forced his eyes open.

The world came in blurred patches. Firelight. Timber overhead, dark with smoke.
A man's bent head. A bald scalp. Heavy shoulders under a plain wool shirt.

No.

The man wrapped him in linen with careful, clumsy fingers. There were scars
across his knuckles. Not the pale lines of small accidents. These crossed and
overlapped, old marks on hands that knew weapons as well as tools.

Pieter looked younger than he had in the game, but Coen knew him.

He knew the father who had pulled Alin's body from beneath the ice. The retired
sellsword who went into the mines because silver fed his children. The man who
would one day send his eldest son to Anca because he no longer knew how to help
his wife.

"Give him here," the woman said.

Pieter obeyed at once.

Warmth replaced his hands. Coen's cheek pressed against damp skin. He smelled
sweat, smoke, and something metallic. The woman's face was pale with exhaustion.
Dark hair stuck to her forehead. She looked down at him with open relief.

Esme.

Not silent. Not staring through him from a bed. Not refusing food while Pieter
begged her to try one more mouthful.

Alive. Tired. Smiling.

"Coen," she said.

The room seemed to stop around that one word.

Pieter sat beside her. "Still set on it?"

"I was set on it before he made me labor half the night."

"He might have an opinion."

"He can share it when he learns to speak."

Her thumb moved across Coen's cheek. He tried to catch it. His hand opened and
closed somewhere below his chin, too slow and badly aimed.

Esme laughed softly. "There. He agrees with me."

Coen wanted to say her name. He wanted to ask the year, the ruler of Vale
Sangora, whether the mines were still open, whether Brencis had entered the
valley yet. He wanted to warn them about the plague and the Blood Mass. He
wanted to tell Pieter never to let Alin near the lake in winter.

His mouth made a wet, broken noise.

Pieter leaned closer. "Strong argument."

This could not be a dream. Dreams did not trap him inside a body that could not
hold up its own head. They did not make every breath a task. They did not leave
him cold along one side while the other pressed against his mother's warmth.

His mother.

The thought came without permission.

These were not characters on a screen now. Esme's heart beat against his ear.
Pieter had blood crusted beneath one thumbnail where she must have gripped his
hand. Their relief filled the small room because he had drawn breath.

Because he was their son.

Light appeared above Esme's shoulder.

Thin red lines gathered in the air. They crossed without casting any glow on
her skin, forming a panel as familiar as the one he had watched for hundreds of
hours.

He went still.

The letters sharpened.

> **CHARACTER RECORD INITIALIZED**
>
> **Name:** Coen  
> **Age:** 0 years  
> **Body:** Healthy newborn  
> **Unspent Skill Points:** 0  
> **Corruption:** 0  
> **Infamy:** 0

A second notice opened beneath it.

> **CONDITION RECOGNIZED: REINCARNATED SOUL**
>
> **Source:** Unknown  
> **Effects:** Prior-life memory retained; mature cognition constrained by
> current body  
> **Duration:** Permanent

He read both panels twice.

No legendary sword waited in an inventory. No gift package offered ten skill
points for surviving birth. His attributes were not even visible yet. The
system had given him a name, a healthy body, and a condition it did not
understand.

That was all.

Coen tried to focus on the word `Body`. A smaller explanation appeared.

> **Healthy newborn**
>
> Physical development is appropriate for age. Strength, coordination,
> endurance, and perception will develop through nutrition, rest, growth, and
> meaningful activity.

Meaningful activity.

He could barely close his hand.

The panic returned, quieter but worse. Brencis was somewhere in this world,
perhaps already centuries old. Xanthe had forgotten the island of her birth.
Bakir had spent lifetimes killing. Anca's magic demanded blood and scars. The
things in Anachora waited beyond death.

And Coen was less capable than any child he had ever escorted through a game.

He could not walk. He could not speak. He could not stay awake.

The panel blurred as his eyes lost focus.

No. He needed dates. He needed a plan.

He reached for the memories in order.

The game opened in 1347. The Black Death. Brencis ruling Vale Sangora. Blood
Masses every month. Silver declared contraband. Coen already grown and coughing
from his years underground.

Before that came Alin.

Coen remembered the lake from the dream Xanthe built. White sky. Dark trees.
Snow pressed flat across the ice. A boy walking where the frozen surface had
thinned. One crack, then black water.

The original Coen had run for Pieter. A frightened child making the only choice
he understood.

He had returned too late.

That death led everywhere. Coen's guilt. Esme's empty stare. Pieter's helpless
anger. Every conversation in their house shaped around someone who should still
have been there.

Not this time.

He did not know how many years he had until Alin was born, much less until the
lake. He did not know whether memory alone would survive infancy unchanged. But
he knew the sound that mattered.

The first crack.

When he heard it, he would not run away from the ice. He would move toward his
brother.

The system panel changed.

> **PERSONAL OBJECTIVE RECORDED**
>
> **A Brother Still Living**  
> Keep Alin alive through the winter of the lake accident.  
> **Known condition:** React before the ice fully separates.  
> **Time remaining:** Unknown  
> **Reward:** Not applicable  
> **Failure:** Alin dies; established consequences remain possible

Coen stared at the reward line.

Not applicable.

Good.

Saving Alin was not a task he should be paid to complete.

"Look at that face," Esme murmured. "So serious already."

"Probably thinking about milk," Pieter said.

"Then he has his father's depth."

"I walked into that one."

Their quiet laughter moved through Coen. It was ordinary, tired, and more
valuable than any ending slide he had unlocked.

The future he remembered pressed close. The mine. The cough. The herbs measured
wrong. Laslea burning. A tooth driven into his heart.

He could not fight any of it tonight.

Tonight, his work was smaller. Breathe. Eat. Sleep. Grow.

He closed the system panels. The red lines drew together until only one narrow
thread remained. For an instant it looked like silver beneath skin. Then it too
disappeared.

Esme shifted him against her chest and covered his head from the cold.

Coen listened to her heart until his new body dragged him toward sleep.

Alin had not even been born yet.

This time, Coen would be ready before he was needed.
