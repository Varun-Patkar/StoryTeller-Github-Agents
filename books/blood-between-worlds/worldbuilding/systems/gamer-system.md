# Gamer System

## Overview

The Gamer System is a private interface carried by Coen from his first life. It
uses the categories and progression logic of *The Blood of Dawnwalker* to
measure what his new body genuinely learns. It is a lens and record, not a
separate source of unlimited power.

## Rules & Limitations

- The system displays attributes, skills, conditions, XP, skill points,
  Corruption, Infamy, Focus information, recipes, and recognized abilities.
- Practice awards progress only when it imposes real effort, correction, or
  risk. Empty repetition produces sharply diminishing returns.
- Physical attributes remain constrained by age, nutrition, injury, sleep, and
  the body's development. An infant cannot spend points to become adult-strong.
- Skill points unlock or consolidate techniques Coen has physically prepared to
  perform. They cannot substitute for tools, knowledge, or required anatomy.
- Manuals accelerate structured learning but must be read and understood.
- The system does not create money, equipment, inventories, healing potions, or
  quest rewards from nothing.
- Quest notices describe commitments and changing conditions. They do not reveal
  hidden motives, guarantee outcomes, or compel Coen to obey.
- Canon knowledge is stored in Coen's human memory, not a perfect database.
- Once events diverge, the system tracks the new reality rather than forcing the
  game plot back into place.
- Notifications use exact quantities, named ranks, durations, and conditions.

## History

The system first appears when Coen fully awakens after birth. Its origin is
unknown. Similarity to the game may reflect his mind, a deliberate intervention,
or a deeper link between worlds; it should not answer that question early.

## Practitioners

Only Coen can see or operate the interface. Other people can observe the results
of his training but not its messages. Henry does not gain a duplicate system.

## Impact on Society

The system has no direct public presence. Indirectly, it lets one peasant child
measure training, recognize conditions, and retain a broader spread of skills
than circumstances would normally allow. Discovery would invite fear of
witchcraft or attempts at exploitation.
